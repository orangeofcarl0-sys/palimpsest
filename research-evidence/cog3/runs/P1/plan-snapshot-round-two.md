## 1. Executive summary

The Partnership can meet the grant condition of fully digitising and publishing **two of the three priority collections** by 30 June 2026 by delivering:

- **Collection A — Parish registers:** 1,800 bound volumes. Stabilise the 220 brittle volumes, scan the collection at CRO between 2 February and 5 March, complete quality control and metadata by 29 May, and publish by **10 June 2026**.
- **Collection B — Wrenfield Film Society:** 240 nitrate reels. Complete the per-reel orphan-works rights risk assessment in January, scan at Northgate between **2 and 20 February** under the conservation policy, hold **23–27 February** as a buffer before the inspection room closes on 1 March, and publish by **15 April 2026**.
- **Collection C — Harrow Estate papers:** excluded from the base plan. There is no written permission, the estate's solicitor says a decision is unlikely before autumn 2026, and a verbal assurance is not sufficient for publication. C also has 76 mould-affected boxes (8% of 950) requiring isolation and treatment. C receives no grant spend and is subject to a 31 March decision gate.

The plan spends exactly **£84,000**, including a **£4,620 contingency (5.5%)**. The critical path runs through the B rights assessment, the Filmtek booking and the 1 March room deadline, the A conservation work and the final publication checks. Both publication dates sit ahead of 30 June, leaving a deliberate buffer. The Filmtek booking is **three weeks, not the two-week minimum**, because the 30-minute nitrate window and realistic scan times allow about one reel per batch. It must also finish before **1 March**, when the Northgate inspection room is booked for the spring public programme; 23–27 February are held as buffer days. This is the single most important calibration and calendar constraint in the plan.

The three open questions are resolved in section 4:

1. **Nitrate at Northgate:** yes, in the inspection room — not the cold store — subject to a timed calibration and strict batch protocol.
2. **2025 open day attendance:** **2,340 visitors**, recorded in the project journal (communications note, September 2025). This is the figure for the newsletter.
3. **Harrow estate permission:** cannot be relied on before 30 June 2026. Plan without C.

### What "fully digitised and published" means

For this plan, a collection is complete only when:

- every item in the collection has a master file and an access derivative;
- every file has passed technical QC and checksum validation;
- every item has a catalogue record and a persistent identifier;
- the collection is live on the publication server with a rights statement;
- the counts reconcile exactly to the source inventory (1,800 volumes; 240 reels);
- the completion evidence is signed off by the Programme Manager and the Partnership Director.

Partial publication of a collection does not satisfy the condition. That is why the plan protects A and B completely rather than spreading resources across C.

## 4. The three open questions

### Q1. Can the Northgate site support scanning the nitrate collection with the Filmtek scanner, and under what conditions?

**Answer: yes — in the Northgate inspection room, not the cold store, only with a calibrated batch protocol, and only before the room closes on 1 March.**

The evidence:

- The Northgate cold store is 4°C and 35% RH. Filmtek cannot operate at 4°C, so the cold store itself is not a scanning location.
- The adjacent inspection room is 16°C and 45% RH. This is inside Filmtek's 15–20°C and 40–50% RH operating range. The pass-through hatch means reels can move between the cold store and the inspection room without an acclimatisation delay.
- The conservation policy allows nitrate to leave the cold store in batches of **up to 40 reels** for **at most 30 minutes, including scanning time**. This is the binding constraint. The 40-reel figure is a cap, not a target: the actual batch size must be set so that loading, scanning and unloading all finish inside 30 minutes.
- The 2024 pilot at CRO failed: 1,200 nitrate frames were scanned in the CRO imaging suite, humidity rose to 62% by the afternoon, condensation appeared on 14 frames, and the conservator stopped the run. CRO is not certified for film and must not be used for nitrate. The Northgate inspection room is the only compliant location.
- Northgate has no public network. Digitised files must be carried to CRO on encrypted drives, with a chain-of-custody log and checksum verification at ingest.
- The inspection room is available for scanning only until **28 February 2026**. From **1 March to 30 June** it is booked for the spring public programme. The cold store is unaffected, but scanning cannot continue after 27 February; this makes the calendar a hard constraint alongside the 30-minute rule.

**Conditions the plan imposes:**

1. **Location:** Filmtek is installed in the Northgate inspection room only. It is never operated in the cold store.
2. **Batch size:** the 40-reel cap is not reachable at realistic scan speeds. Industry practice suggests a professional 35mm nitrate scan takes about **20–30 minutes per reel end-to-end** (loading, scanning, unloading and inspection); Filmtek must confirm this at calibration. At 20 minutes of scanning plus 10 minutes of transfer and logging, or 25 minutes plus 5 minutes of handling, the batch is **one reel per 30-minute window**. At 30 minutes end-to-end the window is fully consumed, so practical throughput falls to about 14 reels/day. The hard caps remain 40 reels and 30 minutes; the 40-reel figure would only bind if the Filmtek could scan a reel in under 45 seconds, which is not assumed.
3. **Daily throughput:** at one reel per batch and 16 batches per day, **16 reels per day**. The production window is **2–20 February (15 working days)**, which covers 240 reels. **23–27 February** are held as buffer days for shortfalls, re-scans and final verification before the room closes. The site day is nine hours, of which eight hours are batch time; breaks are taken between batches.
4. **Cold-store discipline:** reels return to the cold store after every batch and are never left in the inspection room overnight. In/out times are logged for every batch.
5. **Environmental monitoring:** a calibrated temperature/RH logger runs throughout the booking. If the inspection room moves outside 15–20°C or 40–50% RH, scanning stops and the conservator is called.
6. **Calibration gate:** **30 January** is a calibration day, not a production run. The batch size, scan cycle time and daily throughput are recorded and approved by the conservator and Programme Manager before full scanning begins on **2 February**. If the cycle is 14 minutes or faster, two reels fit in a 30-minute batch and the production window can finish early. If it is 15–25 minutes and 16 reels/day can be sustained, the 15-day production window stands. At 26–30 minutes, practical throughput falls to about 14 reels/day; the buffer days 23–27 February are used. The five buffer days cover throughput down to **12 reels/day** (15 × 12 + 5 × 12 = 240); the first three are funded from contingency, and the fourth and fifth require virement or Director approval. Below 12 reels/day, the scope is escalated to the funder. Industry practice spans roughly 20–40 minutes per reel; the 30-minute policy window means only the faster part of that range is usable, and the calibration decides which. If a single reel cannot be scanned inside 30 minutes including handling, the conservation policy cannot be met with this scanner and the Partnership must seek a policy variation or a faster machine; the 30-minute rule is never relaxed and no scanning happens after 27 February.

**Throughput check:** 240 reels ÷ 16 reels/day = 15 production days, **2–20 February**. At 16 batches/day × 30 minutes = 8 hours of batch time per day. **23–27 February** are buffer days. At 14 reels/day, 15 days give 210 and three buffer days give 42 — 252 in total. At 13 reels/day, 15 days give 195 and four buffer days give 52 — 247, but the fourth day needs virement. At 12 reels/day, all five buffer days are needed (180 + 60 = 240); that is the floor. The room closes on 1 March, so there is no post-27 February extension: the buffer must be used inside February or the scope must be escalated.

### Q3. Will the Harrow estate give written permission for collection C before 30 June 2026?

**Answer: no — the plan must not assume it, and C is excluded from the base plan.**

The evidence points one way:

- The estate's solicitor wrote in August 2025 that a decision is **unlikely before autumn 2026**.
- The donor liaison officer recorded a **verbal assurance** in September 2025 that the estate "expects to resolve it by spring". A verbal assurance is not written permission, it is contradicted by the solicitor's own timeline, and it cannot satisfy the grant condition or the donor restriction.
- The donor restriction runs to **2030**. Even a change of heart by the estate would need a written variation, not a conversation.
- C also has a practical problem: 8% of 950 boxes — **76 boxes** — are mould-affected and must be isolated and treated before scanning. That treatment, plus scanning and metadata for 950 boxes, is a programme in its own right and cannot be absorbed into the £84,000 without taking resources from A and B.

**Plan treatment:**

- A written request goes to the estate's solicitor on **19 January 2026**, copied to the donor liaison officer, asking for a decision by **31 March 2026**.
- **31 March 2026 is a hard decision gate.** If written permission has arrived, the Partnership may consider a separately costed variation for C; it must not divert A or B resources. If it has not arrived, C is closed for 2026 and recorded as unavailable.
- No grant spend is committed to C in the base budget. The donor liaison time is in-kind.
- Even if permission arrives, the recommendation is to defer C to a 2026–27 project with its own mould treatment and digitisation budget. A late start in April would not produce a fully digitised and published 950-box collection by 30 June.

## 7. Workstream plans

### 7.1 Collection A — Parish registers

**Conservation.** The conservator works from 5 January to 13 February on the 220 volumes with binding failure: 30 working days at about 7–8 volumes/day. Each volume gets a condition record and a stabilisation treatment appropriate to a brittle binding. Volumes are scanned only after the conservator releases them. The book cradle is used for every volume; spines are never pressed. The 1,580 stable volumes are scanned first so that conservation is not on the critical path for the whole collection.

**Scanning.** Bindwell is on site at CRO from 2 February. The plan uses 24 scanning days at 75 volumes/day, finishing on 5 March. The stable volumes are scanned from 2 February; the 220 stabilised volumes join from 16 February. If throughput falls to 60 volumes/day, scanning takes 30 days and finishes on 13 March. The additional six days cost £4,200. If the Filmtek buffer has not drawn on the contingency, this is covered in full; if it has, the balance is met by virement from publication polish and, if necessary, escalated to the Director and funder (see section 8.4). CRO's two existing copystands are the fallback for additional capacity, using existing staff; they are not in the base budget.

**Image and QC.** Masters are captured at 400 ppi, 24-bit colour, with a colour target and resolution test at the start of each session. Every file gets an automated technical check (resolution, colour, skew, cropping, file integrity) and a 10% visual sample. Exceptions are re-scanned within the Bindwell booking. Checksums are recorded at capture and re-verified at ingest. Condition checks are logged before and after scanning.

**Publication.** Collection A is public domain, so publication is unrestricted. Catalogue records, IIIF manifests and access derivatives are built in April–May. The collection goes live on 10 June, three weeks before the grant deadline.

### 7.2 Collection B — Wrenfield Film Society

The pack's "three weeks of staff time" is read as a three-week work package producing a per-reel record; three weeks per reel would be 13.8 staff-years and is not a feasible reading. The 26-minute average assumes a common template and shared research on repeat rightsholders. The assessment records the reel identity, provenance, rightsholder search, orphan-work status, risk rating and publication decision. The Partnership's solicitor reviews the method and a sample of records; the Director signs off the assessment on 27 January. No B file is published before that sign-off. Publication carries a clear rights statement and a takedown policy. If a reel is assessed as high-risk, the Partnership escalates it to the funder rather than quietly dropping it; the plan assumes all 240 can be published with documented risk acceptance.

**Storage and handling.** All 240 reels remain in the Northgate cold store at 4°C and 35% RH when not being scanned. They move through the pass-through hatch into the inspection room at 16°C and 45% RH. The conservation policy's limits are applied as hard limits: no more than 40 reels out of the cold store at once, and no more than 30 minutes out of the cold store including scanning. At the planning assumption of 20–30 minutes per reel end-to-end, the batch is **one reel per 30-minute window**. Sixteen batches per day give **16 reels/day** at 20–25 minutes; at 30 minutes, practical throughput is about 14 reels/day. The production window is **2–20 February (15 working days)**, with **23–27 February** held as buffer. At 14 reels/day, 15 days give 210 and three buffer days give 42 — 252 in total. The five buffer days cover down to 12 reels/day; the first three are funded from contingency, and the fourth and fifth require virement or Director approval. **30 January** is a calibration day. If the cycle is 14 minutes or faster, two reels fit per batch and the production window can finish early. If measured throughput falls below 12 reels/day, the scope is escalated; if a single reel cannot be completed inside 30 minutes, the policy cannot be met with this scanner. The 30-minute rule is never relaxed, and no scanning happens after 27 February.

**Scanning.** Filmtek is delivered and installed 26–30 January and scans from **2 to 20 February**, with 23–27 February held for shortfalls, re-scans and demobilisation. ## 9. Rationale for the major choices

### 9.1 Why two collections, not three

The grant requires at least two collections fully digitised and published. Collection C cannot lawfully be published without the Harrow estate's written permission, which the estate's own solicitor says is unlikely before autumn 2026. A verbal assurance is not a permission. Trying to include C would either breach the donor restriction or divert resources from A and B and risk the two-collection condition. The maximum compliant scope is therefore A and B in full, with C deferred. This is not a reduction in ambition; it is the largest publishable scope the rights position allows.

### 9.2 Why A and B are sequenced in parallel, not one after the other

A and B use different sites, different equipment and different specialist skills. A needs the CRO book cradle and the conservator; B needs the Northgate cold store and the Filmtek. Running them in parallel uses the February window before the inspection room closes and leaves April–June for quality control and publication. A strictly sequential plan (A then B) would push B scanning past 1 March, when the room is no longer available, and make the two-collection condition impossible to meet.

### 9.3 Why nitrate is scanned at Northgate, not CRO

CRO is not certified for film. The 2024 pilot failed at CRO when humidity reached 62%, condensation appeared on 14 frames, and the conservator stopped the run. The Northgate inspection room is 16°C and 45% RH, inside Filmtek's 15–20°C and 40–50% RH range, and it is adjacent to the licensed cold store with a pass-through hatch. Northgate is the only compliant location. The absence of a network is managed with encrypted drives and chain-of-custody, not by moving nitrate to CRO.

### 9.4 Why the Filmtek booking is three weeks, not the two-week minimum

Two constraints, not one, set the booking. The conservation policy limits nitrate to 30 minutes out of the cold store including scanning, and the inspection room is unavailable from **1 March** for the spring public programme. The plan therefore books **15 production days, 2–20 February**, with **23–27 February** held as buffer before the room closes. At 20–25 minutes per reel, one reel fills the 30-minute window and 16 reels/day gives 240 reels in 15 days. At 30 minutes, practical throughput falls to about 14 reels/day; 15 days give 210 and three buffer days give 42 — 252 in total. The five buffer days cover down to 12 reels/day; below that, even five days cannot reach 240 and the plan escalates. A two-week booking would stop at about 140–160 reels, leaving 80–100 unscanned and the two-collection condition unmet. The three-week booking costs an extra £6,000, funded by reducing management, QC and metadata days and publication polish, not by cutting conservation, scanning or rights work. If the 30 January calibration shows a 14-minute or faster cycle, two reels fit per batch and the production window can finish early, releasing buffer days; it is insurance, not padding.

### 9.5 Why 220 volumes are stabilised before scanning

About 12% of the 1,800 volumes (216, rounded to the conservator's 220) have binding failure. Scanning them on a book cradle without stabilisation would risk further damage. The conservator's six-week estimate (30 working days) is scheduled from 5 January to 13 February. The 1,580 stable volumes are scanned from 2 February, so conservation does not delay the start of A scanning. The brittle volumes join from 16 February and are finished within the Bindwell window.

### 9.6 Why the B rights assessment is done in January

The assessment is the longest lead-time rights task: 240 per-reel records, about three weeks of staff time. Doing it in January means it is signed off before any B scanning or publication, and any high-risk reel is identified while there is still time to adjust the publication plan. Leaving it until after scanning would put the publication date and the grant condition at risk. The assessment is reviewed by the solicitor and signed off by the Director; it is not a desk exercise completed after the fact.

### 9.7 Why publication is phased — B in April, A in June

B is smaller (240 reels) and its rights position is resolved in January, so it can be published first. Publishing B in April proves the publication pipeline, gives the team a real accessibility and user-testing cycle, and leaves A's larger metadata and QC workload a clear run to June. A publishes on 10 June, three weeks before the deadline. The phasing creates two independent chances to meet the grant condition, rather than one all-or-nothing June launch.

### 9.8 Why Bindwell rather than MobileScan for A

Bindwell is a book-cradle scanner at £700/day, designed for bound volumes and available at CRO from 1 February. MobileScan is £950/day plus £1.50 per item and handles paper only; for 1,800 bound volumes it would be more expensive and less suitable for brittle bindings. MobileScan is not nitrate-capable, so it is not an alternative for B. The CRO's two copystands are kept as fallback capacity for A, not as the primary solution.

### 9.9 Why the budget has a 5.5% contingency

The two throughput assumptions — 75 volumes/day on Bindwell and 16 reels/day on Filmtek — are planning assumptions, not contractual guarantees. The 5.5% contingency covers a modest overrun on either, not both at full extent. It is deliberately not larger because the grant is fixed and the three-week Filmtek booking has already absorbed £6,000 that would otherwise have been contingency or staff time. The release rules prioritise the Filmtek buffer risk because the nitrate window is fixed, the inspection room closes on 1 March, and there is no alternative nitrate scanner in the pack. The Bindwell risk has a fallback (CRO copystands, additional days and limited virement from publication polish).

### 9.10 Why C gets no grant spend

Spending grant on C would not produce a publishable collection, because written permission is missing and the restriction runs to 2030. It would also divert money from A and B, which are the collections that can satisfy the grant condition. A small permission-chasing effort is justified and is done in-kind; a mould treatment and scanning programme for 950 boxes is not. If the estate responds positively, C should be a separate 2026–27 project with its own budget, not a late add-on to this one.

## Appendix A — Calculations

### A.1 Collection A

- 1,800 volumes; 12% brittle = 216 volumes; plan uses the conservator's 220.
- Conservation: 220 volumes ÷ 30 working days = 7.3 volumes/day; 5 January–13 February = 30 working days.
- Scanning: 1,800 volumes ÷ 75 volumes/day = 24 scanning days; 2 February–5 March = 24 working days.
- Sensitivity: at 60 volumes/day, 1,800 ÷ 60 = 30 days; 2 February–13 March = 30 working days. Additional 6 days × £700 = £4,200.
- Throughput assumption: 75 volumes/day × about 150 exposures/volume = 11,250 exposures/day; at 1,500 exposures/hour = 7.5 hours. This is tight; validate in the first three days and adjust the daily target or add days if needed.

### A.2 Collection B

- 240 reels ÷ 16 reels/day = 15 production days, **2–20 February**. **23–27 February** are buffer days before the inspection room closes on 1 March.
- Batch: 1 reel per 30-minute window; 20–30 minutes scanning plus 0–10 minutes transfer/logging = 25–30 minutes. Hard caps: 40 reels and 30 minutes. The 40-reel cap does not bind at realistic scan speeds.
- Daily: 16 batches × 1 reel = 16 reels; 16 × 30 minutes = 8 hours of batch time in a nine-hour site day.
- Sensitivity: at 14 minutes/reel, 2 reels per batch (28 minutes + 2 minutes handling = 30), 16 batches/day = 32 reels/day, and production can finish in 7.5 days. At 25 minutes/reel, 1 reel + 5 minutes = 30, 16 reels/day, 15 days. At 30 minutes/reel, there is no slack; 14 batches/day = 14 reels, 15 days = 210, and three buffer days add 42 — 252, enough. At 13 reels/day, 15 days = 195 and four buffer days add 52 — 247, but the fourth day needs virement. At 12 reels/day, all five buffer days are needed (180 + 60 = 240); that is the floor. Above 30 minutes/reel, a single reel cannot be completed inside the policy window; the Partnership must seek a policy variation or a faster scanner. This is why the 30 January calibration is a hard gate.

- 104 hours ÷ 7 hours/day = 14.9 days; budget 15 days.
