## 1. Executive summary

The Partnership will **fully digitise and publish Collections A and B by 30 June 2026**. That is the maximum output that can be guaranteed and lawfully published in the funding period. It satisfies the grant condition that at least two of the three priority collections be fully digitised and published, and it does not depend on Collection C, whose donor restriction cannot be released in time.

**Collection A — Parish registers (1,800 bound volumes).** The 220 volumes with binding failure (about 12%) are stabilised first by the Partnership conservator over six weeks. Scanning on the Bindwell book cradle begins on 1 February, using the 1,580 stable volumes while conservation finishes, so the two workstreams run in parallel rather than in sequence. Bindwell is planned for 50 scanning days at 36 volumes per day, with June held as a throughput buffer. The budget remains within £84,000 on direct cost down to about 28 volumes per day, at which point the contingency and headroom are fully consumed.

**Collection B — Wrenfield Film Society (240 nitrate reels).** A documented rights risk assessment is completed for every reel in January–February, before any publication. Nitrate scanning takes place at the **Northgate Store inspection room**, not at the Central Record Office (CRO), using the Filmtek scanner over a 12-day booking spanning late February and March. The inspection room is at 16°C and 45% relative humidity, inside Filmtek's 15–20°C and 40–50% operating envelope. Reels remain in the 4°C cold store except during batches of no more than 40 reels and no more than 30 minutes, including scanning time. Files are carried to CRO on encrypted drives because Northgate has no network.

**The single biggest technical unknown is Filmtek's actual per-reel scan time**, which the pack does not state. The plan assumes 15 minutes per reel including handling, so two reels fit inside a 30-minute batch. The February test is a hard go/no-go: if a reel takes 16–30 minutes, only one reel fits per batch and B needs about 22 days in total (20 scanning plus 2 installation), costing about £12,000 more and taking the programme to or over the ceiling; if a reel takes more than 30 minutes, it cannot be completed within one compliant batch at all, and B — and therefore the two-collection guarantee — is at risk. This is flagged to the Fund in January and is the first item on the February board agenda.

**Collection C — Harrow Estate papers (950 boxes).** The donor restriction requires the Harrow estate's **written** permission before any publication. The estate's solicitor wrote in August 2025 that a decision is unlikely before autumn 2026; the donor liaison officer's September 2025 verbal assurance that the estate "expects to resolve it by spring" is not written permission and cannot be relied on. The plan therefore **does not schedule C for publication and commits no grant to C**. A written request is sent in January and a hard decision gate is set for **31 March 2026**. If signed permission arrives by then, a separately costed C phase could be activated only with funder approval; if it does not, C remains closed for 2026 and is carried forward beyond the programme, subject to the estate's permission.

The committed budget is **£80,795**, leaving **£3,205** of the £84,000 ceiling unallocated and a **£7,345 contingency** (10% of direct costs). The plan remains within £84,000 on direct cost down to a Collection A throughput of about 28 volumes per day; at that point the contingency and headroom are fully consumed, so below that the programme board must descope publication support or seek funder approval.

### 1.2 Headline decisions

1. **Commit to A and B, not C.** A and B are the only pair with a credible route to full publication by 30 June 2026.
2. **Move nitrate scanning to Northgate.** CRO is not certified for film, its climate range exceeds Filmtek's operating envelope at the top end, and the 2024 summer pilot failed when humidity reached 62%; the Northgate inspection room meets both the scanner and the conservation policy.
3. **Run conservation and scanning in parallel.** Scanning the 1,580 stable A volumes starts on 1 February while the 220 brittle volumes are stabilised.
4. **Complete B rights work before scanning, not after.** The per-reel assessment is the publication gate and must not become a June bottleneck.
5. **Test Filmtek's per-reel time before committing the March run.** The 40-reel limit is a ceiling, not a throughput; the 30-minute cap is the real constraint.
6. **Publish progressively.** A can be released in batches from March; B follows in April. This reduces the risk of a last-week publication failure.
7. **Gate C at 31 March.** That is the last responsible moment to treat 76 mould-affected boxes and digitise 950 boxes by 30 June; no C spend is committed before written permission.

### 2.2 What "fully digitised and published" means

For this plan, a collection is fully digitised and published only when:

- **100% of its items** have been captured to the agreed imaging specification — all 1,800 A volumes and all 240 B reels, not a sample;
- every file has passed integrity checking (checksums) and the agreed quality-control sample;
- preservation masters and access copies are stored on the CRO publication server, with backup copies;
- catalogue metadata is published and the access copies are discoverable on the Partnership's public platform;
- for B, a completed per-reel rights risk assessment is on file before that reel is released;
- the collection is signed off by the Collections Curator (A) or Film Archivist (B) and the Digital Imaging Lead.

The detailed imaging specification, file-naming convention and quality-control sampling rate will be confirmed in the January method statement. The plan assumes a preservation-quality specification appropriate to the material; no new standard is being procured for this programme.

## 3. Planning basis

### 3.1 Calendar and capacity

The programme runs from 1 January to 30 June 2026. There are 129 weekdays in the period; after the New Year, Good Friday (3 April), Easter Monday (6 April), Early May bank holiday (4 May) and Spring bank holiday (25 May), about **124 working days** are available. The schedule is built on five-day weeks and does not rely on weekend working. Any weekend or evening work would be a contingency decision, not a plan assumption.

### 3.2 Throughput assumptions

The pack supplies day rates but not item throughput. The following planning rates are therefore explicit assumptions, to be validated in the first week of each workstream. They are backed by the contingency and the sensitivity analysis in Section 6.3.

| Workstream | Planning rate | Days | Basis |
|---|---|---:|---|
| A conservation | 220 volumes in 6 weeks | 30 | Conservator's estimate in `condition.md` |
| A scanning | 36 volumes/day | 50 | 1,800 ÷ 36 = 50; moderate rate for bound volumes on a book cradle; validated in the first week |
| B rights assessment | 15 working days in total | 15 | "About three weeks of staff time" in `rights.md`; per-reel records |
| B nitrate scanning | 2 reels per 30-minute batch; about 12 batches/day | 10 + 2 = 12 | 240 ÷ 2 = 120 batches; 120 ÷ 12 = 10 days; 2 days for installation, timed test and demobilisation |
| C (indicative only, not funded) | 50 boxes/day | 19 | 950 ÷ 50; used only to test the 31 March gate |

**The 30-minute nitrate batch is the binding constraint on B.** The 40-reel figure is a maximum batch size, not a throughput target; the plan does not assume that 40 reels can be scanned in 30 minutes. The number of reels per batch is set by the scanner's actual per-reel time plus handling:

| Measured total time per reel (capture + handling) | Reels per 30-minute batch | Scanning days for 240 reels at 12 batches/day | Filmtek booking | Filmtek cost | Consequence |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 15 minutes or less | 2 | 10 | 12 days | £14,400 | Plan |
| 16–30 minutes | 1 | 20 | 22 days | £26,400 | About £12,000 more; at or over the ceiling; board/Fund decision |
| More than 30 minutes | Cannot complete one reel within one compliant batch | — | — | — | Hard stop; written policy ruling or alternative needed; B at risk |

The plan assumes **15 minutes per reel including handling** (about 12 minutes of capture and 3 minutes of handling), so two reels fit exactly inside 30 minutes. The 15 minutes is the full clock time from leaving the cold store to returning, including movement through the hatch; there is no slack, which is why the test is decisive. This must be proved in the February test batch using the longest reel in the collection (or a representative worst case), before the main March run. If the measured time is 16–30 minutes, only one reel fits and B needs about 20 scanning days plus 2 installation days; the extra £12,000 cannot be absorbed without cutting publication support or obtaining Fund approval. If a single reel takes more than 30 minutes, it cannot be completed within one compliant batch; splitting a reel across batches would require a written conservation-policy ruling and is not assumed. This is the programme's single biggest technical risk.

### 3.3 Rights assumptions

- **Collection A** is public domain. There is no rights gate on A publication.
- **Collection B** is orphan works. The pack requires "a documented rights risk assessment" before publication and says the search "takes about three weeks of staff time and must be completed for each reel." The plan reads this as **about 15 working days in total to produce a documented assessment record for each of the 240 reels**, not three weeks of elapsed time per reel. Three weeks per reel would be 720 weeks of work and would make the programme impossible; the assumption will be confirmed in writing with the Fund in January. If the Fund confirms a per-reel elapsed requirement, the programme board must escalate immediately because the two-collection condition could not be met.
- The B assessment is a **risk assessment and publication gate**, not a consent process. It records the searches done, the findings, the residual risk and the decision. Publication proceeds only where the assessment is complete and signed off under the Partnership's orphan-works policy. Any reel that cannot be cleared is escalated to the board and the Fund in February, not left to June.
- **Collection C** requires the estate's **written** permission before any publication. The August 2025 solicitor's letter is the only written position and says a decision is unlikely before autumn 2026. The September 2025 verbal assurance is recorded but is not permission. The plan does not treat it as a green light.

### 3.4 Cost assumptions

- Vendor rates are taken from `vendors.md`: Filmtek £1,200/day with a two-week minimum booking and £1,800 transport each way; Bindwell £700/day; MobileScan £950/day plus £1.50 per item (not used in the committed plan).
- Staff costs for the rights assessment, conservation and programme coordination use an internal planning rate of **£250/day**. The conservation line assumes the Partnership's conservator is available at that internal rate; if an external conservator is required, the rate will be higher and a variation may be needed.
- The budget assumes quoted vendor rates are inclusive of VAT and standard consumables. If VAT is additional, the Partnership will recover it or use the contingency; this is a January finance check.
- No inflation, currency or contingency-for-contingency assumptions are included. The 10% contingency is the only reserve.

### 3.5 Constraints that shape the plan

1. **CRO cannot handle nitrate.** Its environmental control is certified for paper at 18–22°C and 40–55% relative humidity, not for film. The top of that paper range is already outside Filmtek's 15–20°C and 40–50% film envelope. The 2024 pilot in the CRO imaging suite saw humidity rise to 62% by the afternoon, condensation on 14 frames, and a conservator stop. The lesson is explicit: do not repeat the pilot unchanged, especially in summer.
2. **Northgate is the only compliant nitrate location.** The cold store is 4°C and 35% relative humidity, licensed for nitrate. The adjacent inspection room is 16°C and 45% relative humidity, inside Filmtek's 15–20°C and 40–50% envelope. Filmtek cannot operate inside the 4°C cold store.
3. **The nitrate batch limit is 40 reels and 30 minutes, including scanning.** The pass-through hatch removes the need for acclimatisation but does not extend the time limit.
4. **Northgate has no network.** Files must be carried to CRO on encrypted drives, with checksums and a chain-of-custody record.
5. **Bindwell is on site from 1 February only.** January is therefore conservation, rights and mobilisation, not A scanning.
6. **C's written permission is unlikely before autumn 2026.** The plan must be viable without C.
7. **A's brittle volumes need stabilisation before scanning.** About 220 volumes, six weeks of conservator time; the other 1,580 can be scanned from 1 February.
8. **B's rights assessment must precede publication.** It is on the critical path and is scheduled in January–February, not after scanning.

### 4.3 February 2026 — A scanning starts; conservation completes; B rights signed off; Filmtek test

**Weeks 3–4**
- A scanning continues; stabilised volumes join the queue; weekly throughput review against 36/day.
- **Filmtek delivery, installation and timed test** in week 4. The 12-working-day booking starts with delivery and installation (day 1) and the timed test on the longest reel (day 2), then moves straight into scanning. Install in the inspection room, not the cold store. Qualify the room: temperature and humidity within 15–20°C and 40–50% relative humidity, scanner calibration, capture test pattern, network isolation confirmed. For the test, take no more than 40 reels from the cold store, keep them out for no more than 30 minutes including scanning, and measure the total time per reel including handling. Record the time out, time back, temperature and humidity. The target is 2 reels per batch.

If it achieves only 1 reel/batch, escalate immediately: B would need about 22 days in total (20 scanning plus 2 installation), so the booking must be extended by 10 days and the March schedule and budget re-planned before scanning continues. ## 5. Workstream plans

### 5.1 Collection A — conservation and scanning

**Conservation.** The 220 volumes with binding failure are stabilised before they go on the book cradle. The 12% estimate is 216 volumes; the conservator's planning figure of 220 is used here, giving a small margin. The conservator works to the six-week estimate (30 working days), with a weekly log. The book cradle is mandatory; spines are never pressed. Volumes that are stable do not wait for conservation: they are scanned from 1 February. This parallel running is the main schedule saving in A.

**Scanning.** Bindwell is booked for 50 days at 36 volumes/day. The CRO has two copystands for paper and bound volumes; they are the backup for stable volumes if Bindwell is unavailable, but they are not a substitute for the book cradle on brittle volumes. MobileScan is not used for A: it handles paper only and does not provide the book cradle that brittle bound volumes require. Daily capture logs record volume ID, operator, page count, file count, QC result and any condition incident.

**QA and publication.** A is public domain, so publication can be progressive. Batches are released after file integrity checks, QC sampling and metadata sign-off. The first batch goes live at the end of March, the second at the end of April, and the remainder in May–June. This proves the workflow early and avoids a June bottleneck.

**Throughput sensitivity.** At 36 volumes/day, A takes 50 days. If throughput is 30/day, A takes 60 days and needs 10 buffer days in June; that still fits within the £84,000 ceiling (direct cost about £80,450) and the calendar. If throughput is 28/day, A takes 65 days and needs 15 buffer days; direct cost is about £83,950, leaving only £50 before the ceiling. Below 28/day, the board must descope publication support or seek Fund approval before conservation or QC is compromised.

### 5.2 Collection B — rights and nitrate scanning

**Rights.** The B workstream starts with rights, not scanning. The rights officer builds a per-reel register (Appendix B), conducts and records the searches, assigns a risk rating, and obtains legal sign-off. The target is 240 records in 15 working days. No B reel is published until its record is complete and signed off. High-risk reels are escalated in February so that further searches, an orphan-works licence or a board risk decision can be taken before the April publication window.

**Nitrate scanning.** Filmtek is installed in the Northgate inspection room, not the cold store and not CRO. The room is qualified at 16°C and 45% relative humidity, inside the scanner's 15–20°C and 40–50% envelope. Each batch takes no more than 40 reels from the cold store and keeps them out for no more than 30 minutes, including scanning; in practice the plan uses 2 reels per batch. Reels return to the 4°C cold store through the pass-through hatch. MobileScan cannot handle nitrate, so it is not an alternative for B. A batch log records reel IDs, time out, time back, operator, scan result and any condition note; an environmental log records temperature and humidity at the start and end of each batch.

**February test protocol.** Use the longest reel in the collection (or a representative worst case). Measure the full clock time out and back for one reel, then for two, including movement through the hatch. Record temperature and humidity. If two reels fit within 30 minutes, confirm the 2-reel batch plan. If only one fits, replan at 20 scanning days plus 2 installation days and take the budget decision. If one reel exceeds 30 minutes, stop and escalate for a written policy ruling. The test result is a formal board decision, not a technical note.

**Data transfer.** Northgate has no network. Files are written to encrypted drives, checksummed, carried to CRO and verified before the Northgate copy is released. A chain-of-custody record accompanies each drive. The CRO publication server holds the preservation masters and access copies; a second backup copy is kept. Four drives are rotated so that a drive is never overwritten before its CRO copy is verified.

**Throughput.** 240 reels at 2 per 30-minute batch is 120 batches; at 12 batches/day that is 10 scanning days. The 12-day booking includes installation, the timed test, 10 scanning days and demobilisation; slippage is covered by contingency. If the test shows only 1 reel per batch, B needs 20 scanning days plus 2 installation days; the extra £12,000 takes the programme to or over the ceiling, and the board must decide whether to descope publication support or seek Fund approval. If a single reel cannot be completed within 30 minutes, B cannot be delivered under the current policy without a written ruling. This is a critical go/no-go in February.

## 7. Rationale for major choices

### 7.1 Why the plan commits to A and B, not C

The grant condition requires at least two collections to be **fully digitised and published** by 30 June 2026. A and B are the only pair with a credible route to full publication:

- **A** is public domain, so publication has no rights gate. Its risk is conservation and throughput, both of which are manageable with the book cradle, early conservation and a June buffer.
- **B** is orphan works, but the required step is a **rights risk assessment**, not a rights holder's consent. The assessment can be completed in January–February, before publication. Its risk is the 30-minute nitrate batch and the scanner's per-reel time, which is tested in February and buffered by the 12-day booking.
- **C** requires the Harrow estate's **written** permission before any publication. The only written advice says a decision is unlikely before autumn 2026. The verbal assurance is not permission. A plan that relied on C would risk committing roughly £25,500–£27,500 and three months of work to a collection that might still be unpublishable on 30 June. That would put the two-collection condition at risk.

The plan therefore maximises **publishable** output, not nominal digitisation. A and B are the maximum compliant scope. C is not abandoned: it is requested in writing, its mould-affected boxes are isolated as a preservation duty, and a 31 March gate allows a properly funded C phase if the estate resolves early. But it is not counted, and no grant is committed to it before written permission.

### 7.2 Why nitrate scanning moves to Northgate

The 2024 pilot is the decisive evidence. Nitrate was scanned in the CRO imaging suite in July; humidity rose to 62% by the afternoon, condensation appeared on 14 frames, and the conservator stopped the run. CRO's environmental control is certified for paper at 18–22°C and 40–55% relative humidity, not for film. The top of that range is already outside Filmtek's film envelope of 15–20°C and 40–50%, so even before the nitrate certification issue, CRO cannot guarantee the scanner's operating conditions.

The Northgate inspection room is the compliant alternative:

- it is at **16°C and 45% relative humidity**, inside Filmtek's **15–20°C and 40–50%** operating range;
- it is adjacent to the **4°C cold store**, with a pass-through hatch, so reels can be returned quickly and do not need acclimatisation;
- the cold store is licensed for nitrate and keeps reels at 4°C and 35% relative humidity when they are not being scanned;
- Filmtek cannot operate inside the cold store, so the inspection room — not the cold store — is the scanning location;
- the conservation policy allows batches of up to **40 reels for at most 30 minutes, including scanning time**, which the batch procedure is designed around;
- Northgate has **no network**, so files are carried to CRO on encrypted drives with checksums and a chain of custody.

This is not a convenience choice. It is the only location in the pack that satisfies both the scanner's operating envelope and the nitrate conservation policy.

### 7.3 Why Filmtek is booked for 12 days — and why the per-reel test matters

At 2 reels per 30-minute batch, 240 reels need 120 batches. At about 12 batches per day, that is 10 scanning days. The booking is 12 days: 10 days of scanning plus 2 days for installation, the timed test and demobilisation; slippage is covered by contingency. That also satisfies Filmtek's two-week minimum booking.

The 2-reel assumption is the critical number. It assumes 15 minutes per reel including handling, so two reels fit exactly within the 30-minute limit. The pack does not give the scanner's per-reel time or the film gauge/footage, so the February test measures it directly on the longest reel. If only 1 reel fits, B needs 20 scanning days plus 2 installation days; the extra £12,000 takes the programme to or over the ceiling. If a single reel takes more than 30 minutes, no compliant batch can complete it; that is a hard stop requiring a written policy ruling, not a scheduling fix. The plan does not hide that risk: it makes the test a go/no-go before the main scanning run.

### 7.4 Why Bindwell starts on 1 February and conservation runs in parallel

Bindwell is on site at CRO from 1 February. Waiting for all 220 brittle volumes to be stabilised would waste most of February and compress A into April–June. The plan instead scans the **1,580 stable volumes** from 1 February and adds the 220 stabilised volumes as they clear conservation in February. The conservator's six-week programme and the scanning programme therefore overlap, with the book cradle available for the brittle volumes when they are ready.

The 50-day booking is about half of the available working days, which leaves room for Bindwell's shared availability and for the CRO copystands to handle stable volumes if Bindwell is unavailable. The June buffer protects the 30 June deadline if throughput is below 36/day. At 30/day, A needs 10 extra June days; at 28/day, 15 extra days. The budget remains within £84,000 at 28/day, but only just.

### 7.5 Why rights assessment is a January–February workstream

B's rights assessment is the legal precondition for publication and sits on the critical path. If it were left until after scanning, April and May would become a rights bottleneck and the June buffer would disappear. Starting in January gives time to:

- complete 240 per-reel records in 15 working days;
- obtain legal sign-off;
- escalate any high-risk reel early enough to do further searches, apply for an orphan-works licence or take a board risk decision;
- publish B in April, leaving May–June for verification and A.

The plan also flags the one ambiguity in the pack: "about three weeks of staff time ... for each reel." If that meant three weeks per reel, the programme would be impossible. The plan records the 15-day total interpretation, confirms it with the Fund in January, and treats a contrary answer as a major escalation.

### 7.6 Why publication is progressive

A June-only publication would put the whole grant condition at risk if the platform, metadata or QA failed in the last week. Progressive publication:

- proves the workflow with a small A sample in February and a first A batch in March;
- lets A, which is public domain, go live while B rights and scanning are still in progress;
- gives B an April publication target, with May–June as buffer;
- produces monthly evidence for the Fund and early warning of metadata or access problems.

The cost is modest — fixed QA/publication support of £2,000 per collection — and the risk reduction is significant.

### 7.7 Why the C gate is 31 March

A C phase would need to isolate and treat 76 mould-affected boxes, scan 950 boxes and publish them. At an indicative 50 boxes/day, scanning alone is 19 days, plus treatment and QA. Starting in April gives about three months; starting later would not finish by 30 June. The 31 March gate is therefore the last responsible moment.

The gate is also a financial control. A C phase would cost about £25,500–£27,500, which is not in the committed budget. It could only be activated with a written funding variation or reprofiling approved by the Fund, and only if it did not put A or B at risk. Without signed permission, there is no lawful publication and no basis for spending the grant.

## Appendix A — Nitrate batch procedure (Northgate)

This procedure applies to every Collection B batch. It is written to satisfy the Partnership's conservation policy and Filmtek's operating requirements.

1. **Pre-batch check.** Confirm the reels for the batch (in practice 2, never more than 40), their condition, and the scanner and room status. Record the batch ID and operator.
2. **Room qualification.** Confirm the inspection room is within 15–20°C and 40–50% relative humidity. Record the readings. If outside, do not start; inform the conservator and store manager.
3. **Remove from cold store.** Move the reels through the pass-through hatch into the inspection room. Start the 30-minute clock at the moment the first reel leaves the cold store. Do not exceed 30 minutes out, including scanning.
4. **Inspect and scan.** Handle reels with clean gloves; do not clean, splice or repair without the conservator. Thread the scanner, capture to the agreed specification, and record the result. If a reel shows damage, stop and quarantine it.
5. **Return to cold store.** Return all reels through the pass-through hatch before the 30-minute limit. Record the time back. If the limit is at risk, stop scanning and return the reels; do not start a reel that cannot be completed within the remaining time.
6. **Environmental log.** Record temperature and humidity at the start and end of the batch, the time out and back, and any excursion. Any condensation, fogging or unusual smell stops the run.
7. **Incident.** If there is an excursion, condensation or damage, isolate the affected reels, inform the conservator, and record the incident. Do not resume until the conservator clears the batch and the room is back in range.
8. **Data transfer.** Copy files to an encrypted drive, generate checksums, and record the drive ID and chain of custody. Carry the drive to CRO; verify checksums before releasing the Northgate copy. Keep a dual copy until verification.
9. **Network.** Do not connect the scanner or the Northgate equipment to a public network. All transfer is by encrypted drive.
10. **No CRO nitrate.** No nitrate is taken to CRO or scanned in the CRO imaging suite at any time.
11. **40 is a ceiling, not a target.** The batch size is limited by the 30-minute clock and the measured per-reel time. Do not plan a batch on the assumption that 40 reels can be scanned in 30 minutes.
12. **Hard stop.** If a single reel cannot be completed within 30 minutes, stop and escalate to the programme board and the conservator. Do not split a reel across batches without a written conservation-policy ruling.

## Appendix B — Collection B per-reel rights record: minimum fields

Each of the 240 records should contain:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Reel ID | Unique identifier linked to the physical reel and the digital files |
| Title / description | As known from the reel, can, labels and catalogue |
| Date / creator | Known or estimated; basis for the estimate |
| Provenance | Donor, transfer, acquisition or custody history |
| Physical description | Gauge, footage, condition, labels |
| Rights status | Orphan works assessment; known rights holders, if any |
| Searches | Sources searched (e.g. Fund guidance, UK IPO orphan works, BFI, DACS, local records, donor records), dates and results |
| Findings | What was found, what was not found, and the gaps |
| Risk rating | Low / medium / high, with the reason |
| Decision | Publish / publish with conditions / further search / exclude / licence |
| Reviewer and date | Rights officer and legal reviewer |
| Publication link | URL and date, once published |
| Review date | When the record should be revisited |

The record is the evidence that the assessment was completed before publication. It is not a substitute for legal advice on a high-risk reel.
