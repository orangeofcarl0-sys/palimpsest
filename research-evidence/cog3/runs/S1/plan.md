### 3.1 Question 1 in detail — the Northgate conditions

The inspection room is the only compliant location in the Partnership’s estate for the Filmtek scanner:

- **Temperature and humidity:** 16°C and 45% RH are comfortably inside the scanner’s 15–20°C and 40–50% RH operating range. The room is adjacent to the cold store and connected by a pass-through hatch, so reels do not need outdoor acclimatisation.
- **Cold store:** 4°C and 35% RH. The scanner cannot operate there, and nitrate must return to cold storage whenever it is not being scanned.
- **CRO:** certified at 18–22°C and 40–55% RH for paper, but not certified for film. The 2024 pilot is decisive evidence against repeating nitrate work there: humidity reached 62% by the afternoon, condensation appeared on 14 frames, and the conservator stopped the run. The programme runs into summer, which makes CRO even less suitable.
- **Batch limit:** the conservation policy allows up to 40 reels to leave the cold store for at most 30 minutes, including scanning time. The 40-reel figure is an upper bound, not a target. The operative constraint is the 30-minute window. At an assumed average of about three minutes per reel at scan speed, a compliant batch is seven reels, giving four batches and 28 reels per day. This is the basis for the Filmtek booking, not a notional six batches of 40.
- **Conditions to confirm before installation:** power, floor loading, door widths and scanner footprint in the inspection room; logger alarms at 15°C and 20°C, and at 40% and 50% RH; a written abort procedure; and a conservator on call during the scanning fortnight.
- **Data transfer:** Northgate has no public network. Files are written to encrypted drives, checksummed, and carried to CRO the same day under a signed chain-of-custody form.

### 3.2 Question 2 in detail — the open-day figure

The 2025 open day drew **2,340 visitors**. This figure is already recorded in the Partnership’s programme notes and should be used as stated in the newsletter. It is not a figure that requires external checking, and the plan does not allocate staff time to re-research it.

### 3.3 Question 3 in detail — the Collection C decision

Collection C cannot be published without written permission from the Harrow estate. The evidence points in two directions:

- The estate’s solicitor wrote in **August 2025** that a decision is **unlikely before autumn 2026**.
- The donor liaison officer recorded a **verbal assurance in September 2025** that the estate “expects to resolve it by spring”.

A verbal assurance is not written permission, and it is contradicted by the solicitor’s written advice. The prudent planning assumption is that written permission will **not** arrive before 30 June 2026. Collection C is therefore excluded from the two-collection commitment. The Partnership will:

1. Send a written permission request in the first week of January, copied to the estate’s solicitor.
2. Follow up monthly and log every response.
3. Commit **no digitisation spend** to C in this grant period.
4. If written permission arrives unexpectedly, treat it as upside and seek a funder-approved variation. It will not be used to displace A or B, and 950 boxes could not be fully digitised in the remaining weeks in any case.

The 8% of C that is mould-affected (about 76 boxes) is a collections-care issue. It is flagged to the conservator and kept out of the digitisation workflow; handling mould-affected material without treatment would create a health and conservation risk.

## 4. Delivery strategy

### 4.1 Collection A — Parish registers (1,800 bound volumes)

**The constraint.** About 12% of the collection — 220 volumes, using the conservator’s estimate — has binding failure and needs stabilisation before scanning. The conservator estimates six weeks for 220 volumes. Scanning must use a book cradle, and spines must never be pressed.

**The approach.** Conservation starts on **Monday 5 January** and runs for 30 working days, finishing on **Friday 13 February**. The 1,580 volumes that do not need stabilisation are queued first, so scanning can start on **Monday 2 February** while conservation finishes the fragile 220. The conservator signs off each batch of stabilised volumes before it enters the scanning queue.

Bindwell is booked for **26 days** at CRO: 25 scanning days plus one setup/buffer day. At a planning rate of 75 volumes per day, 25 days give 1,875 volumes of capacity against 1,800 required — a 4.2% margin. The scanning block runs **2 February – 6 March**, with 20 days in February (1,500 volumes) and five days in March (375 volumes). Collection A scanning is complete by **Friday 6 March**.

Quality assurance and ingest run **23 March – 10 April**, at a planning rate of 120 volumes per day. The first publication tranche goes live on **30 April**, and the full collection is published by **Friday 22 May**.

**Why scan stable volumes first.** Waiting for all 220 volumes to be stabilised would consume six of the programme’s 26 weeks before a single image was captured. Stable-first sequencing keeps the scanner productive, protects the fragile volumes, and still leaves a month of publication float.

### 4.2 Collection B — Wrenfield Film Society (240 nitrate reels)

**The constraint.** Nitrate film must be cold-stored when not being scanned. The scanner can only operate in the Northgate inspection room, and reels can leave the cold store only in batches of up to 40 for at most 30 minutes, including scanning time. The collection is orphan works, so a documented rights risk assessment is required before publication.

**The approach.** The rights assessment runs **12–30 January** (15 staff days, the equivalent of three weeks), producing a per-reel register. The assessment has schedule float to **10 April** before the publication gate, so a slower search does not put the deadline at risk.

Filmtek is booked for the two-week minimum, **9–20 March**. Day one is installation and calibration; the remaining nine days are scanning. At four batches per day of seven reels, and 28 reels per day, nine days give **252 reels of capacity** against 240 required — a 5% margin. Each batch is timed door-to-door and returned to the cold store immediately after scanning. The environmental logger runs continuously, and the conservator can stop the run at any point.

Quality assurance and the per-reel rights cross-check run **13–24 April**. Collection B is published on **Thursday 30 April**, after the rights officer and programme manager sign off every reel.

**Why March, not summer.** The 2024 pilot failed in July at CRO when humidity reached 62%. Scheduling B in March, in the controlled Northgate inspection room, avoids both the summer risk and the CRO environmental failure. It also places B after the A scanning block, so the digital archivist and the in-kind imaging team are not split between CRO and Northgate.

### 4.3 Collection C — Harrow Estate papers (950 boxes)

Collection C is **not a delivery target** in this phase. The donor restriction requires written estate permission before any publication; the written legal advice says a decision is unlikely before autumn 2026. The collection is also large (950 boxes) and 8% mould-affected, so even a late permission could not produce a full digitisation by 30 June. No C digitisation spend is committed. The permission request is kept alive, and any written permission would be handled through a funder variation rather than by silently reallocating A or B resources.

### 4.5 Quality and publication standard

- **Collection A:** 400 ppi, 24-bit colour TIFF masters; JPEG access copies; no OCR (the registers are handwritten); collection-level and item-level metadata derived from the scanning manifest.
- **Collection B:** 2K scan, 16-bit DPX or 10-bit ProRes masters; H.264 access copies; frame-level checks; audio captured where present.
- **All outputs:** SHA-256 checksums at capture, on transfer and before publication; 3-2-1 backup (master at CRO, encrypted drive copy, offline copy); persistent identifiers and stable URLs.
- **Publication:** CRO catalogue and publication server; clear rights statements; Collection B carries an orphan-works notice and a take-down contact; Collection A is marked public domain.
- **Accessibility:** catalogue descriptions and captions where feasible; alt text for representative images.

These are programme standards. They should be checked against the Partnership’s digital preservation policy before the first ingest.

### 6.3 Budget notes and assumptions

- **Filmtek booking:** the two-week minimum is treated as 10 working days, not 14 calendar days. Day one is installation and calibration; nine days are scanning. This is the single most important commercial assumption to confirm before the purchase order.
- **Bindwell:** 26 days at the quoted day rate. The scanner is on site from 1 February; the contract must reserve the two blocks around the Filmtek fortnight.
- **Conservation:** the rate includes labour and stabilisation materials. If the conservator finds more than 220 volumes needing work, additional days come from contingency and the queue is re-prioritised; the 1,580 stable volumes are not held up.
- **Rights assessment:** 15 staff days is the whole-collection estimate, with a documented record for each of the 240 reels. It is not 15 days per reel; that reading would require 720 weeks and is not a deliverable within any realistic grant.
- **Staff:** the grant-funded team is coordination, supervision, QA and publication. The Partnership provides imaging technicians, the CRO imaging suite, the Northgate cold store and inspection room, the publication server, finance and HR support in kind. Routine QA is carried out by the in-kind imaging technicians; the Digital Archivist samples and signs off. These in-kind contributions are not valued in the £84,000 and should be recorded as match funding if the application requires it.
- **VAT:** the budget assumes vendor rates are inclusive of VAT or that VAT is recoverable. Finance should confirm the treatment before contracts are signed. An irrecoverable VAT liability would exceed the contingency and would require a budget revision before commitment.
- **No Collection C costs:** there is no C digitisation, treatment or publication line. This is deliberate and is the main reason the A+B programme fits comfortably within the grant.

### 6.4 Contingency and change control

The £4,220 contingency is 5.0% of the grant. It is held centrally and released only by the Programme Board, in this order of priority:

1. Additional Filmtek days if nitrate throughput falls below 28 reels/day.
2. Additional conservation days if more than 220 A volumes need stabilisation.
3. Additional Bindwell days if A throughput falls below 75 volumes/day.
4. Additional QA/publication days if ingest slips.

It is **not** available for Collection C. Any C work would require written permission and a funder-approved variation, not a quiet reallocation.

Any change over £1,000 or five working days requires a written change request and board approval. Any change to the grant-funded scope requires funder approval.

### 6.5 Value for money

- **Bindwell versus MobileScan:** for 26 days, Bindwell costs £18,200. MobileScan would cost £24,700 in day rates plus £2,700 in item charges for 1,800 volumes — £27,400 — and it handles paper only, not bound volumes. Bindwell is both cheaper and technically required for the book cradle.
- **Filmtek:** the two-week minimum is the only nitrate-capable option in the pack. The plan uses 252 reels of capacity for 240 reels, a 5% margin, rather than paying for a third week.
- **Conservation:** stabilising 220 volumes prevents damage to fragile bindings and avoids re-scanning or conservation treatment after the programme. It is 10.7% of the budget and protects 100% of Collection A.
- **Rights assessment:** at 4.6% of the budget, it unlocks publication of all 240 reels and is a grant condition. Without it, Collection B cannot be published at all.
- **Contingency:** 5.0% is deliberately modest but sufficient to cover the most likely overruns — a few extra scanner days or conservation days. It is cheaper than over-booking vendors in the baseline.

## 9. Governance, assurance and reporting

- **Programme Board:** meets monthly, chaired by the Partnership Director. Members: Curator, Conservator, Finance Manager, Donor Liaison Officer and Programme Manager. The board owns the budget, the contingency and any change request.
- **Delivery huddle:** weekly during January–May, covering scanning progress, environmental logs, rights register and risks. Minutes and a RAG status are circulated to the board.
- **Finance:** monthly reconciliation against the line-item budget; a forecast to 30 June; contingency drawdown approved by the board. The final reconciliation is signed off by Finance before the grant report.
- **Quality assurance:** the Digital Archivist signs off each scanning block; the Conservator signs off Collection A stabilisation and every nitrate batch; the Rights Officer signs off Collection B before publication.
- **Funder reporting:** a short monthly progress note and a final report by 31 July 2026, covering items digitised, items published, spend, rights assessment, nitrate compliance and lessons learned.
- **Evaluation:** publication statistics, user access, and the 2025 open-day figure of 2,340 visitors provide the baseline for the newsletter and final report.
- **Change control:** any change over £1,000 or five working days needs a written request and board approval; any change to grant-funded scope needs funder approval.

## Appendix B — Nitrate batch protocol

1. **Before the batch:** check the inspection room is within 15–20°C and 40–50% RH; record the reading; confirm the scanner is calibrated; prepare a batch list of no more than 40 reels — in practice seven.
2. **Retrieve:** move the batch through the pass-through hatch from the cold store. Start the timer when the first reel leaves the cold store.
3. **Scan:** scan the batch. The clock includes scanning time, not just handling. Target 25 minutes to leave a five-minute margin.
4. **Return:** return every reel to the cold store through the hatch. Stop the timer and record the door-to-door time. The hard limit is 30 minutes.
5. **Abort:** if temperature or humidity goes outside range, or condensation appears on any reel, stop immediately, return the batch to the cold store, quarantine the affected reels and notify the Conservator.
6. **Data:** write files to an encrypted drive, generate SHA-256 checksums, complete the chain-of-custody form and transfer to CRO the same day.
7. **Log:** record batch number, reel IDs, start and end times, environmental readings, scanner operator, conservator sign-off and any incident.

## Appendix C — Collection B rights assessment checklist

For every reel, record:

- Reel identifier, title or working title, date, maker and provenance.
- Rights holders identified and the sources searched (Film Society minutes, donor records, local press, Companies House, the IPO orphan works register, and any other reasonable source).
- Search date, searcher and findings.
- Risk rating and the proposed publication decision (publish, publish with notice, restrict).
- Reviewer sign-off and date.
- Any take-down or contact information.

The register is complete only when all 240 reels have a record. Publication approval requires the Rights Officer and Programme Manager to sign the register and the publication checklist. Collection B carries an orphan-works notice and a take-down contact on the public catalogue.
