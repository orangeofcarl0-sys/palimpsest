# Pilot design — After Hours: Thursday evening opening with a late minibus, capped at 60

**Round:** 2 — revised for the new 60-attendee licensing condition, the revised lift quote and the estate survey
**Pilot core cost:** £24,000 (evening opening £9,600 + late bus £14,400)
**Full plan cost:** £37,500, including the Youth Studio, targeted mailer and training workstreams
**Duration:** 13 weeks, weeks 1–13 of the April–June 2025 quarter
**Venue:** ground floor / main hall only (the hall holds 180 seated, but the licensed evening cap for this pilot is **60 attendees**)
**Target segments:** Northside and Kiln Field residents, public-transport-dependent evening attendees, former Evening Talks attendees who lapsed during the 2024 pause, and the existing evening talks/film audience

---

## 1. The hypothesis

**If** the centre opens on Thursday evenings for 13 weeks, **and** provides a late minibus on the Northside–Kiln Field loop, **and** keeps every evening within the current 60-attendee licensing cap, **then** evening attendance will be at least 50 visits per session and at least half of new evening attendees will come from the two priority estates. **Because** the 2023 pilot showed a 38% evening attendance gain with most new attendees from those estates, and the gain reversed when the minibus was removed.

The pilot is a **capped replication with better measurement**, not a new untested idea. It also tests whether the gain can be delivered within the current staffing model (existing staff extended, no new post), the fixed envelope and the new building-control condition.

**What would disprove the hypothesis:** evening attendance stays at or below the pre-pilot seasonal baseline, or estate attendance does not materialise even when the bus runs, or the responsible-person rota cannot be sustained, or the bus is not used.

**What the cap changes about the test:** the pilot cannot admit more than 60 people to an evening event while the accessible entrance is out. If demand exceeds 60, the pilot will record turnaways and a waitlist rather than admit more. That means the pilot may not be able to replicate the full 2023 uplift; it tests whether the transport-and-programme model still works within the current licensing ceiling. This is a deliberate, constraint-compliant design, not a claim that 60 is the right long-term capacity.

## 3. Programme design

### 3.1 Dates, times and format

- **13 consecutive Thursday evenings**, weeks 1–13.
- **Doors:** 19:00. **Programme:** 19:30–21:15. The 19:30 start is a working assumption, to be confirmed against the operator's journey time from the estates so that the 19:15 departure can arrive before the programme begins. The 21:30 return is the fixed constraint. The 21:15 finish is within the 23:00 premises licence; the binding constraints are access and the 60-attendee condition, not the licence hour.
- **Format:** existing talks and film format, using partner/volunteer content and existing staff. No new programming or artist fee is committed; if new content requires a fee, it is outside this plan and needs a written quote.
- **Relationship to existing Evening Talks:** the 13 Thursday evenings are the quarter's evening programme. The existing twice-monthly Evening Talks format is folded into the weekly slot rather than run in addition, to avoid double-counting and to stay within the 1.5 FTE rota. If the centre keeps separate Evening Talks dates, they count toward the 13 evenings and the 60 cap; no additional evening opening cost is assumed.
- **Capacity:** the main hall holds 180 seated, but the pilot's licensed evening capacity is **60 attendees**. The hall will be laid out for no more than 60; tickets/registrations will be capped at 60; the door count will be reconciled to the booking list. The 180 figure is not used as the operating capacity.
- **Pricing:** no pricing change is proposed. Existing arrangements apply; pricing is not costed in this plan. The estate survey found cost is a stated barrier for 22% of aware residents; the plan does not fund a price subsidy and will not claim to have removed that barrier.

### 3.2 The 60-attendee cap and booking control

- **Hard cap:** no evening event may admit more than **60 attendees** while the accessible entrance is out of service. This is a condition of the council's building control officer, not a target or a soft guide. It applies to the pilot's 13 evenings and to any other evening event at the venue; the events schedule is checked weekly for any over-60 booking.
- **Counting convention:** "attendees" is interpreted as the audience. The counting convention will be confirmed in writing with the council's building control/licensing officer before week 1. If the council counts staff, performers and volunteers within the 60, the ticket allocation will be reduced so that total attendance does not exceed 60.
- **Booking:** a registration list using existing processes (no new ticketing cost is quoted) with a hard stop at 60. The door count is reconciled to the list at every session.
- **Waitlist:** once 60 are registered, further requests go on a waitlist. **Northside and Kiln Field residents are prioritised** for any released places, so the cap does not systematically exclude the priority estates.
- **No walk-ups above 60:** if the registered list is full, staff do not admit additional people. Turnaways are counted and recorded, with a short reason (full / late / other) and, where volunteered, estate.
- **Compliance check:** the responsible person confirms before doors that the layout, booking list and door count are consistent with the cap. Any risk of exceeding 60 is resolved by stopping admissions, not by reclassifying attendees.
- **What happens if the cap cannot be enforced:** the evening is paused or cancelled. Running an over-cap event would be a licensing breach and is not an option.

### 3.3 Transport

- **One minibus on the Northside–Kiln Field–centre loop**, 13 weeks, with **19:15 and 21:30 departures** as quoted.
- **Working assumption:** the 19:15 departure brings residents from the estates to the centre, and the 21:30 departure returns them after the programme. The quote does not state direction or stops, so this must be confirmed with the operator before week 1.
- **To confirm before contract:** exact stops, pick-up points, capacity, wheelchair accessibility, insurance, safeguarding arrangements, driver/operator responsibilities, and what happens if a departure is missed.
- **Capacity risk:** the quote does not state the minibus capacity. If it is below the number of estate residents who want to travel, the estate-share target may be limited by seats rather than demand. Carryings and turnaways by departure are recorded; any extra vehicle or run needs a written quote and cannot be assumed this quarter.
- **No staffed car park after 20:00.** The pilot's transport offer is the bus, not parking. Car users are not the primary target segment, and the plan does not claim to solve parking.

### 3.4 Staffing and responsible person

- A **named responsible person** must be on site for every evening the building is open to the public. This is an operating requirement in the constraints, and the current rota does not staff it.
- The role is filled by **existing staff extended**, funded by the £9,600 line. **No new post is created.**
- A **named backup** must be identified for every evening. If neither the responsible person nor the backup is available, that evening is **paused**, not run without cover.
- The 1.5 FTE front-of-house team covers daytime opening as well. The rota must be checked weekly for hours, rest and cover. If the team cannot sustain 13 evenings, the pilot should reduce frequency rather than run unsafely; the budget line is for 13 weeks, so any reduction is a board decision, not an informal drift.
- The 2-day welcome and access-awareness training is scheduled before the first evening (week 1) for all rostered front-of-house staff. It includes the 60-person cap, the booking/waitlist process and honest access information.

### 3.5 Access

- **Ground floor only.** No public event may use the first floor while the lift is out of service. The pilot does not schedule any first-floor activity.
- **The accessible entrance remains out of service.** The pilot does not restore step-free access. This is a known limitation, not a claim of accessibility.
- **The 60 cap is a licensing condition, not an accessibility solution.** It allows ground-floor evening events to run without the accessible entrance being operable, but it does not make the venue step-free. A disabled visitor who needs step-free entry still cannot be guaranteed access.
- Access information (ground-floor location, entrance in use, bus times, the 60-person cap, who to contact) is included in the mailer and on booking pages. Staff are trained to answer access questions honestly, including saying that the lift is out of service and that step-free entry is not currently available.
- If a participant needs first-floor access, the pilot cannot accommodate it until the lift is repaired. This is stated in the access information rather than discovered on the night.
- If a disabled visitor asks for step-free access and it cannot be provided, staff record the request (without personal details beyond what is needed) and signpost alternatives. That record is part of the access evidence for the capital case, not a substitute for the repair.
- If the lift is repaired and certified during the quarter, the 60 cap may be reviewed only with written confirmation from building control and a board decision. The pilot does not assume this will happen; the lift is not funded in this plan.

### 3.6 Communication and recruitment

- **Targeted mailer to priority-neighbourhood households** (Northside and Kiln Field), with a specific offer: Thursday evenings, talks/film, dates, bus times, how to book, the 60-person cap, and access information. Printing and postage are included in the £4,200 quote.
- **What the new estate survey adds:** 48% of surveyed households had never heard of the centre, so the mailer has a clear awareness job. Among those who had heard, 57% named getting home after an evening event as the main barrier, 22% cost and 11% programme content. The mailer therefore leads with the bus times and the specific evening offer, states existing pricing clearly, and does not assume that awareness alone will produce attendance.
- **Existing channels at no additional quoted cost:** website, email, social media, partner networks, staff word of mouth, community contacts.
- **Lapsed Evening Talks attendees:** use existing consented contact records, where they exist, for a direct email/phone cascade. If no usable records exist, this is a gap to note, not a cost to invent.
- **No borough-wide print.** The 2022 campaign is the reason: it produced no measurable change and the board minuted against repeating it without a specific audience and offer.
- **Mailer timing:** address list and artwork in weeks 1–2; delivery by week 3; pre-mailer awareness baseline in week 1; post-mailer awareness check around week 8 and week 12.
- **Awareness measures:** a short pre/post survey of mailer recipients, asking (a) whether they have heard of the centre, and (b) whether they know about the Thursday evening programme and bus. The general baseline is 52% heard / 48% never heard (estate survey, n=120); the specific-offer baseline is established in week 1. Report n, response rate and wording.

### 3.7 Data collection at every session

- Attendance count at the door, reconciled to the booking/registration count, with a note of any discrepancy.
- **Cap compliance:** booked count, door count, turnaways, waitlist length, and confirmation that no event exceeded 60.
- Postcode capture on arrival or booking, optional and aggregated, with a short privacy notice.
- New vs returning status: "new" means no recorded attendance in the previous 12 months; where status is unknown, record "unknown" rather than guessing.
- Bus carryings by departure: estate vs other, recorded by the operator or a nominated staff member.
- Equalities monitoring as required by the council, using the council's agreed categories where available; aggregate and suppress small cells.
- Incident log and a short staff debrief each evening.
- Participant feedback: a short optional form or QR code; no external fieldwork cost is committed.
- A record of access requests that could not be met because step-free entry was unavailable.

## 4. Target segments and what "success" means for each

| Segment | Why they are targeted | What success looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Northside residents | Priority neighbourhood; two bus changes; last bus 19:10. | At least half of new evening attendees are from the two estates combined; Northside is reported separately. |
| Kiln Field residents | Same access barrier; priority neighbourhood. | Same as above; report separately so one estate does not mask the other. |
| Public-transport-dependent evening attendees | The 19:10 last bus makes after-19:00 events unusable without the minibus. | Bus carryings are recorded on both departures; the 21:30 return service is recorded as operating. |
| Former Evening Talks attendees who lapsed in 2024 | The programme paused without notice; at least one long-standing attendee assumed it had ended. | A measurable reactivation flow, where consented contact records allow; at minimum, the specific offer reaches them through the mailer and existing channels. |
| Existing evening talks/film audience | The pilot should retain them, not just add new people. | Attendance per session at least 50; no loss of the existing audience. |
| Estate residents who cannot get a place because of the 60 cap | The cap may bind; the plan must not hide the resulting exclusion. | Turnaways and waitlist are recorded, with estate where volunteered; the data are used in the capital case for the lift. |

The Youth Studio workstream has a different target group (young people using the Studio) and its own metrics. It is part of the same quarter plan but not part of the evening pilot's target segments.

## 5. Success criteria and decision rule

### 5.1 Primary criteria (week 13)

1. **Average attendance of at least 50 visits per Thursday evening session** across the 13 sessions (about 650 visits in total), compared with 36.5 per session in April–June 2024 and 46.5 in January–March 2025. **No session may exceed 60 attendees.** The average is reported alongside the number of sessions at or near the cap.
2. **At least 50% of new evening attendees resident in Northside or Kiln Field**, with the denominator and the "unknown postcode" share reported.
3. **The bus is used and recorded**: carryings on both departures, with at least half of riders from the two estates, and the 21:30 return service recorded as operating.

### 5.2 Secondary criteria

- Awareness of the Thursday evening programme among mailer recipients improves by at least **10 percentage points** over the pre-mailer baseline; general awareness of the centre in a comparable estate sample moves in the same direction, with n and method reported.
- All rostered front-of-house staff complete the 2-day training by week 4.
- **Zero first-floor public events** while the lift is out of service.
- **Zero evening events above 60 attendees** while the accessible entrance is out of service; every session has a reconciled door count.
- No safety incidents; no evening run without a named responsible person.
- Family Saturdays remains within ±10% of its April–June 2024 level (1,626–1,988 visits).
- Turnaways and waitlist length are recorded for every session. If the cap is reached in more than 10% of sessions, the pilot has hit a demand ceiling; that is evidence for the capital case, not a reason to exceed the cap.

### 5.3 Decision bands

| Result at week 13 | Decision |
|---|---|
| Attendance ≥50/session, no session >60, estate share ≥50%, bus used, no safety/compliance issues | **Continue/scale** the evening + bus model into the next quarter, subject to the new envelope and the 60 cap. Protect the bus if the estate gain is real. |
| Attendance 36.5 to below 50/session, or estate share 25% to below 50%, or bus use weak | **Review and redesign**: test timetable, route, promotion, programme mix or an earlier finish; do not assume the model has failed or succeeded. |
| Attendance below 36.5/session, or estate share below 25%, or bus unused after a full quarter | **Stop** the pilot. Do not continue evening opening without transport; that is the 2023 failure mode. |
| Cap reached in more than 10% of sessions, with recorded turnaways | **Continue within the cap** and use the turnaway evidence to support the lift capital case; do not admit above 60. If the board wants to serve the unmet demand, it must fund the accessible entrance first. |
| Any evening event above 60 attendees, or any attempt to reclassify attendees to avoid the cap | **Stop the event**, investigate, report to the board and the council; this is a licensing and compliance breach, not a performance issue. |
| Responsible-person cover cannot be sustained | **Pause** the affected evenings and review frequency; do not run without a named responsible person. |
| Lift becomes available and is certified | The 60 cap may be reviewed only with written building-control confirmation and a board decision. The pilot does not depend on it. |

### 5.4 What happens when the bus funding ends

The pilot is funded for 13 weeks only. At week 13 the board must decide whether to renew the bus in the next quarter. The 2023 evidence says the estate attendance gain reverses when the bus stops. The plan therefore treats the bus as the **last item to cut**, not the first. If the next envelope cannot cover the bus, the board should either:

- reduce the evening frequency and keep a smaller bus service, if the operator can quote it (not in this pack, so it would need a new quote), or
- stop the evening programme rather than run it without transport and claim the same outcomes.

What the board should **not** do is continue the evening programme without the bus and present the resulting attendance as a success.

## 7. What the pilot does not test

- **Demand above 60.** The cap censors the attendance measure. The pilot cannot say how many people would have come if the accessible entrance were operable; it can only record turnaways and waitlist demand.
- **The full 2023 uplift if the cap binds.** The 2023 result was produced without a 60 cap. If demand exceeds 60, the pilot tests a constrained version of the model, not a full replication.
- **Step-free access or first-floor use.** The lift and accessible entrance remain out of service. The pilot is ground-floor only.
- **Parking.** The survey's 44 parking mentions are a real friction, but there is no quoted parking intervention and the pilot targets public-transport users.
- **Borough-wide awareness.** The mailer is targeted; it is not a test of a borough campaign.
- **Non-attender demand in general.** The new estate survey provides a household baseline, but the pilot does not survey a representative panel of non-attenders; its post-mailer check is a small follow-up sample.
- **New programme/artist demand.** The pilot uses existing formats and does not commit unquoted programming fees.
- **Long-term retention.** Thirteen weeks is enough to test attendance and estate reach, not whether new attendees become regulars over a year.
- **The effect of the 18-session Youth Studio expansion.** That is a separate workstream with its own metrics; the evening pilot does not test it.

These limits are carried into `uncertainties.md`. They are reasons to be careful about the claims, not reasons to avoid the pilot.
