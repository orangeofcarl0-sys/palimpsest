## 3. What each line buys

### 3.1 Evening opening — £9,600

Buys 13 weeks of Thursday evening opening: the named responsible person on site, utilities and cleaning. It does not buy a new post, new programming or artist fees. The cost assumes existing staff are extended, which is the only staffing model the constraints permit. The line also carries the operating consequence of the new licence condition: every evening is hard-capped at 60 attendees, with booking, door-count reconciliation and a compliance log. The £9,600 does not buy additional capacity or an accessible entrance.

### 3.2 Late bus subsidy — £14,400

Buys one minibus on the Northside–Kiln Field loop for 13 weeks, with 19:15 and 21:30 departures. This is the mechanism that made the 2023 pilot work; without it, the 19:10 last public bus makes after-19:00 events inaccessible from the two estates. The new estate survey reinforces the point: among residents who have heard of the centre, 57% name getting home after an evening event as the main barrier.

### 3.3 Additional Youth Studio sessions — £6,500

The quote is for additional sessions at £6,500. The partner has now confirmed in writing that the same sum covers **up to four additional sessions a month** for the quarter (12 additional sessions; 18 in total at six a month), using its waiting list of 40 first. The partner supplies staff; the centre supplies room and materials. This is a supply correction, not a marketing campaign: the data show per-session attendance and unique participants rising even after the session count was halved, and the partner has confirmed that its own funding change caused the reduction. Room availability and the session calendar must be confirmed before commitment.

### 3.4 Targeted mailer — £4,200

Buys printing and postage for a mailer to Northside and Kiln Field households. It is deliberately not the £9,000 borough-wide print campaign: the 2022 evaluation found no measurable change, and the board minuted against repeating that approach without a specific audience and a specific offer. The new estate survey gives the mailer a clear baseline — 48% of 120 surveyed households had never heard of the centre — but the council has not tested whether awareness alone converts, and 57% of aware residents cite transport home as the main barrier. The mailer is therefore paired with the bus and remains a test with a pre/post measure and a kill criterion.

### 3.5 Staff training — £2,800

Buys two days of welcome and access-awareness training for rostered front-of-house staff. It supports honest access information (including the lift being out of service and step-free entry not being available), welcome, confidence with the evening/bus arrangements, and correct operation of the 60-person cap and waitlist. Backfill is not quoted; training should be scheduled on days that do not require additional cover, or the cover gap should be managed within the existing rota.

## 6. Sensitivity: the possible 15% reduction

The council has verbally indicated that the **following** quarter may be reduced by up to 15%. Nothing is confirmed. Two readings matter:

### 6.1 If the 15% applies to the £48,000 envelope

- Reduced envelope: £48,000 × 0.85 = **£40,800**.
- Recommended plan: **£37,500**.
- Headroom: **£3,300**.
- Result: the full plan still fits. No line needs to be stopped on cost grounds.

### 6.2 If the 15% applies to the plan

- Reduced plan: £37,500 × 0.85 = **£31,875**.
- Stop the targeted mailer (£4,200) and the training (£2,800): £37,500 − £7,000 = **£30,500**.
- Headroom against £31,875: **£1,375**.
- Result: the evening + bus + Youth Studio core (the evidence-backed core) survives; the untested mailer and the one-off training are deferred.

### 6.3 Stop/scale order

If a cut is deeper than either scenario, apply this order:

1. **Targeted mailer** — least proven, easiest to stop, free channels remain.
2. **Staff training** — one-off, can be deferred; access information can still be delivered in briefings.
3. **Additional Youth Studio sessions** — revert to the existing two sessions a month; this loses the supply test but does not stop the evening pilot.
4. **Evening opening and late bus** — protect as the core. If the envelope cannot cover both, do **not** continue evening opening without the bus; the 2023 evidence says the estate gain reverses. Either fund both or stop the pilot and say so.

The bus is the last item to cut, not the first. That is the opposite of a conventional "cut the biggest line" approach, and it follows directly from the 2023 evidence.

## 7. Financial controls

- **Monthly budget report** to the finance trustee: committed, invoiced, paid, forecast, and any variance.
- **No virement between capital and revenue.** The lift is outside this budget; if capital is released, it is a separate decision with its own approval.
- **No unquoted commitment.** Any proposal whose cost is not in `resources.md` needs a written quote before it can be committed; it cannot be added to this quarter's plan retrospectively.
- **No overspend.** If a quoted cost rises above the committed line, the stop/scale order applies; the plan does not use the £10,500 headroom to absorb an overrun unless the board explicitly reallocates it to a quoted line (which would still need to fit the envelope).
- **No over-cap admissions.** The 60-attendee cap is a licensing control. Additional admissions above 60 are not a revenue option and are not counted in the budget.
- **Invoice evidence:** bus operator contract/invoice, training provider invoice, mailer supplier invoice, Youth Studio partner invoice, and evening staffing/utility/cleaning records.
- **Quarter-end reconciliation:** report committed vs spent, with an explanation of any unspent balance. Unspent funds are not carried.
