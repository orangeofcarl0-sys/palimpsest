# Quarterly plan: April–June 2025 (13 weeks) — Round 2

**Riversdale Community Arts Centre** (fictional; synthetic pack)
**Goal:** improve participation and service accessibility over the next quarter, within a fixed resource envelope.
**Envelope:** £48,000, committed at the start, no carry-over.
**Committed plan:** £37,500.
**Chosen pilot:** After Hours: Thursday evening opening with a late minibus, capped at 60 attendees.
**Pilot core cost:** £24,000. **Full plan cost:** £37,500, including the Youth Studio, targeted mailer and training workstreams.
**Capital item outside the plan:** lift repair and accessible entrance reinstatement, £27,500, 8-week lead. Not committed.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The plan does four things:

1. **Repeats the one intervention with direct local evidence** — Thursday evening opening plus a late minibus for Northside and Kiln Field — for 13 weeks, with better measurement and an explicit week-13 decision rule. Because the accessible entrance is out and the lift repair is unaffordable alongside this plan within the envelope, every evening is **hard-capped at 60 attendees** under the council's new licensing condition.
2. **Expands the Youth Studio** from two sessions a month to six — four additional sessions a month, 12 additional over the quarter, 18 in total — at the same £6,500 quote. The partner has confirmed its staffing capacity and a waiting list of 40. The fall in visits was a session-count effect caused by the partner's funding change, not evidence that young people lost interest.
3. **Tests a targeted mailer** to priority-neighbourhood households with a specific offer, instead of repeating the borough-wide print campaign that failed to convert in 2022. The new estate survey gives this a baseline: 48% of 120 surveyed households had never heard of the centre; among those who had heard, 57% named getting home after an evening event as the main barrier, 22% cost and 11% programme content. The mailer is paired with the bus, not treated as a stand-alone conversion strategy.
4. **Trains front-of-house staff in welcome and access awareness**, and keeps all activity on the ground floor. The 60-attendee cap is enforced through booking, a waitlist and a reconciled door count. The lift is not funded; it is a separate £27,500 capital decision with an 8-week lead.

The plan deliberately does **not** fund the £27,500 lift, the £9,000 borough print campaign, the £7,500 café refresh (now deferred and unavailable), a new post or unquoted programming. The lift is the main unresolved access issue and needs a separate capital decision; it cannot be absorbed into this revenue plan without cutting deeply into the participation portfolio, and even the old "lift + evening + bus" combination is now £51,500, over the envelope.

**What the board is asked to approve:**

- the plan and the £37,500 commitment;
- the data collection and equalities monitoring in `metrics.md`;
- the **hard cap of 60 attendees** on every evening event, with booking/waitlist control and a compliance log, until the accessible entrance is certified operable;
- the week-13 stop/go rule, including the commitment not to continue evening opening without transport;
- a separate capital conversation about the **£27,500** lift with an **8-week** lead, without assuming the £10,500 headroom can fund it.

## 2. The problem and the theory of change

### 2.1 What the evidence says

- **Participation is concentrated.** Family Saturdays is the volume base (1,807 visits in April–June 2024). The smaller programmes are stable, except Youth Studio, whose visits fell only because sessions were halved.
- **The priority estates are transport-constrained.** Northside and Kiln Field are two bus changes away; the last weekday bus from the centre is 19:10; there is no staffed car park after 20:00. The 2023 evening pilot worked with a late minibus and collapsed when the minibus stopped. The new estate survey reinforces this: among residents who have heard of the centre, 57% name getting home after an evening event as the main barrier.
- **Awareness is not uniform.** The new estate survey found 48% of 120 surveyed households had never heard of the centre and 37% had heard but never attended. The 2022 borough-wide print/mail-drop did not convert, but the current evidence does not support the blanket claim that "awareness exists." A targeted mailer has a real awareness job, but the council has not tested whether awareness alone converts; transport remains the dominant stated barrier among those who know the centre.
- **The Youth Studio is supply-constrained.** The partner has confirmed that its own funding change in June 2024 caused delivery to fall from four sessions a month to two. Visits per session rose from about 49 to 76, monthly unique participants rose from 71 to 115, and the partner holds a waiting list of 40. The same £6,500 now buys up to four additional sessions a month.
- **The communication gap is real.** Evening Talks paused in 2024 without notice; a long-standing attendee assumed the programme had ended. The survey's "didn't know" mentions point the same way, and the estate survey's 48% never-heard figure shows the gap is wider than lapsed attendees alone.
- **The building is not fully accessible, and the constraint has tightened.** The accessible entrance and lift are out; first-floor public events are prohibited. The lift now costs **£27,500** with an **8-week** lead, and the accessible entrance must be operable before **any evening event with more than 60 attendees** is licensed, whether or not the event uses the first floor.

### 2.2 Theory of change

**If** we run a reliable Thursday evening programme with a late minibus, within a hard cap of 60; **and** expand the Youth Studio to six sessions a month using the partner's waiting list; **and** tell the priority neighbourhoods about the specific offer; **and** train staff to welcome and advise on access; **then** participation will rise among the priority estates and the evening audience, without damaging Family Saturdays. **Because** transport is the binding constraint (2023 pilot, estate survey), session frequency is the Youth Studio constraint (attendance data, partner letter), and targeted communication plus welcome address the remaining friction (interviews, survey, 2022 campaign). The 60 cap limits how much evening demand can be served, but it does not change the transport mechanism being tested.

**Key assumptions to test:**

- the 2023 evening gain replicates under current conditions, at least up to the 60 cap;
- the bus is used by estate residents, not just existing car users;
- the 12 additional Youth Studio sessions fill at or near the current per-session rate, using the waiting list of 40;
- the targeted mailer moves awareness more than the 2022 borough campaign did, and some of that awareness converts to attendance;
- the 1.5 FTE front-of-house team can sustain 13 evenings with existing staff extended;
- the partner can staff four additional sessions a month (confirmed) and the room/dates can be scheduled;
- the 60 cap can be enforced at every session.

Each assumption has a metric and a decision point. If an assumption fails, the plan adjusts or stops that element rather than asserting success.

## 9. Decision points and stop/go rule

### 9.1 Week 6 — mid-point

The board reviews:

- average evening attendance per session against 46.5 (current) and 36.5 (same quarter last year);
- cap status and turnaways/waitlist by session;
- estate share and unknown-postcode share;
- bus carryings by departure;
- Youth Studio sessions/visits/unique participants;
- training completion and rota cover;
- spend against budget;
- incidents, over-cap checks and access issues.

**Decision:** continue as designed, adjust route/promotion/programme, or pause the affected element. The board does not wait until week 13 to fix an operational problem.

### 9.2 Week 13 — final

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| Evening ≥50/session, no session >60, estate share ≥50%, bus used, no safety/compliance issues | Continue/scale the evening + bus model next quarter, subject to the new envelope and the 60 cap. Protect the bus if the estate gain is real. |
| Evening 36.5 to below 50/session, or estate share 25% to below 50%, or bus use weak | Review/redesign: test timetable, route, promotion, programme mix or earlier finish. Do not declare success or failure from one quarter. |
| Evening below 36.5/session, or estate share below 25%, or bus unused | Stop. Do not continue evening opening without transport; that is the 2023 failure mode. |
| Cap reached in more than 10% of sessions, with recorded turnaways | Continue within the cap; use the turnaway evidence in the lift capital case; do not admit above 60. If the board wants to serve the unmet demand, it must fund the accessible entrance first. |
| Any evening event above 60, or any first-floor public event while the lift is out | Stop the event, investigate, report to the board and council; compliance issue, not a performance issue. |
| Training incomplete | Treat as an implementation failure; complete training before drawing access conclusions. |
| Family Saturdays outside ±10% of 1,807 | Review cannibalisation; adjust scheduling/promotion before continuing. |

### 9.3 Stop/scale order if money is tight

1. Targeted mailer.
2. Staff training (defer; brief in-house instead).
3. Additional Youth Studio sessions (revert to two a month).
4. Evening opening and late bus — protect as the core; if both cannot be funded, stop the pilot rather than run evening without transport.

The bus is the last item to cut, not the first, because the 2023 evidence says the estate gain reverses without it.

### 10.2 The lift

The lift repair and accessible entrance reinstatement is **not in this quarter's plan**. It is capital, now **£27,500**, has an **8-week** lead time, cannot be phased, and would cost **£65,000** if added to the full portfolio — over the envelope by **£17,000**. The old "lift + evening + bus" fallback is now **£51,500**, over by **£3,500**. The board should:

- decide separately whether to seek a council capital variation or fund it from capital;
- if approved, instruct it in week 1 so it can complete around week 9;
- not schedule any first-floor public event, or any evening event above 60, until the lift and accessible entrance are certified operational;

The board cannot have the lift, the evening opening and the bus within £48,000; that package is £51,500.

## 11. What success and failure look like

**Success at week 13:**

- evening attendance at or above 50 per session, with no session above 60, and at least half of new attendees from Northside and Kiln Field;
- the bus is used on both departures and the 21:30 return service is recorded as operating;
- cap pressure is measured: turnaways and waitlist are recorded, and if the cap is reached in more than 10% of sessions, the unmet demand is documented for the capital case;
- Youth Studio runs 18 sessions with at least 1,200 visits, at least 150 monthly unique participants by the final month, and at least 65 visits/session;
- awareness among mailer recipients improves by at least 10 percentage points on the specific offer, or the mailer is stopped on the evidence; general awareness moves in the same direction;
- all front-of-house staff are trained; zero first-floor breaches; zero over-60 evening events while the accessible entrance is out; no safety incidents;
- Family Saturdays stays within ±10% of its seasonal baseline;
- the quarter is delivered within £37,500 and the £48,000 envelope.

**Failure at week 13:**

- evening attendance stays below the 36.5 seasonal baseline, or estate share stays below 25%, or the bus is unused;
- the responsible-person rota cannot be sustained, or staff hours become unsafe;
- a first-floor event is scheduled while the lift is out of service, or an evening event above 60 is admitted while the accessible entrance is out, or access claims are made that the building cannot support;
- the plan overspends or commits an unquoted cost;
- the board continues evening opening without transport and presents the resulting attendance as success;
- the report presents capped attendance at 60 as proof that demand stopped at 60, without reporting turnaways and waitlist.

Failure is not the same as a null result. A well-measured pilot that shows the 2023 gain does not replicate, or that the cap prevents a full test, is a useful finding; the mistake would be to keep spending without saying so.
