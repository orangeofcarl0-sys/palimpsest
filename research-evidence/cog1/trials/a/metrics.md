# Metrics and measurement plan

**Round:** 2 — revised for the 60-attendee licensing cap, the expanded Youth Studio offer, the estate survey and the revised lift quote

The plan is judged on **participation and access**, not on satisfaction alone. Four measurement rules follow from the assessment:

1. **Measure per session and per segment, not just totals.** Adding 13 evening sessions or 12 additional Youth Studio sessions will raise totals mechanically. A total-visits target on its own would reward running more sessions, not reaching more people.
2. **Do not invent a baseline.** Where the pack has no programme-level neighbourhood, awareness or equalities baseline, the plan says so and establishes one in the first weeks. It does not back-fill a number. The new estate survey is a household baseline, not a programme-level one.
3. **Report the denominator and the unknowns.** Estate share is reported with the "unknown postcode" share; awareness is reported with the sample size and response rate; bus use is reported by departure, not just as a total.
4. **Treat the 60-attendee cap as a censoring limit, not a target.** Attendance cannot exceed 60 by design. Turnaways and waitlist demand are measured separately so the report does not confuse "we filled the cap" with "we met all demand."

Targets below are **planning thresholds**, not forecasts. They are set from the same-quarter 2024 baseline, the 2025 run rate, the 2023 pilot or the new estate survey where relevant. They are deliberately conservative where the evidence is thin.

**Why metric 9 is context, not primary:** a simple sum of the component targets — using the Q2 2024 Family baseline (1,807) and the current Choir/Cinema run rates (749 and 280) plus the evening target (650) and the Youth target (1,200) — is about **4,686 visits**. A total target is useful for the board summary, but it can be met by adding sessions while missing the estate or per-session goals. The primary metrics are 1–4.

**Why the 60 cap matters for metric 1:** current evening attendance is 46.5/session. If the 2023 38% uplift applied to that current base, demand would be about **64/session**, above the 60 cap. The pilot may therefore hit the ceiling. A 50/session target is still a meaningful test against the 36.5 same-quarter baseline, but the report must show how often the cap bound and how many people were turned away. It must not present capped attendance as evidence that demand stopped at 60.

## 2. Definitions and data rules

| Term | Definition | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Visit** | One attendance at a session, as recorded at the door or on the booking system. | Use one counting method per session; reconcile bookings and door counts; do not mix methods without explanation. |
| **Attendee (for the 60 cap)** | A person admitted to the evening event as audience. | The counting convention is confirmed in writing with building control/licensing before week 1. If staff/performers are counted within the 60, reduce the ticket allocation so total attendance does not exceed 60. |
| **Hard cap** | The maximum number of attendees licensed for an evening event while the accessible entrance is out of service: **60**. | No walk-ups above 60. No reclassification to avoid the cap. A breach is a compliance incident. |
| **Turnaway** | A person who sought admission but was not admitted because the cap was reached. | Count at the door or booking point; record reason and, where volunteered, estate. Do not count a turnaway as a visit. |
| **Waitlist** | People who asked for a place after the 60 were registered but before the session. | Record length and whether estate residents were prioritised; do not add waitlist numbers to attendance. |
| **Unique participant** | A person attending at least once in a month, where the register allows de-duplication. | Monthly unique counts are **not additive** across months; do not sum them into a quarterly "unique" figure unless de-duplicated. |
| **New evening attendee** | No recorded attendance at the centre in the previous 12 months. | If status is unknown, record "unknown" separately; do not guess. Report new attendees as a count and as a share of known-status attendees. |
| **Priority neighbourhood** | Northside or Kiln Field, mapped from postcode. | Report each estate separately; do not let one mask the other. |
| **Estate share** | Estate residents ÷ attendees with a known postcode. | Report the unknown-postcode share alongside it; do not impute postcodes. |
| **Bus rider** | A person boarding the minibus at a recorded stop. | Count by departure (19:15 / 21:30) and by estate/other; do not double-count a return trip as a new attendee. |
| **Awareness (general)** | A respondent correctly saying they have heard of the centre. | Ask the same question pre and post; report n, response rate and wording. The estate survey baseline is 52% heard / 48% never heard (n=120). |
| **Awareness (specific offer)** | A respondent correctly identifying that the centre runs a Thursday evening programme with a late bus. | Ask the same question pre and post; report n, response rate and wording. Do not infer specific-offer awareness from general awareness. |
| **Training completion** | A rostered front-of-house staff member attended both training days. | Keep a register; do not count partial attendance as complete without board agreement. |
| **Over-cap event** | An evening event at which more than 60 attendees are admitted while the accessible entrance is out. | Zero tolerance. Stop admissions, investigate and report; this is a licensing breach, not a performance issue. |

### 2.1 Data collection methods

- **Booking and door count:** a registration list capped at 60; door count reconciled at the start and, where practical, during the evening. Record the booked count, door count and any discrepancy. The responsible person signs off the count.
- **Turnaways/waitlist:** a simple tally at the door/booking point, with reason and (where volunteered) estate. No personal data beyond what is needed.
- **Postcode:** collected at booking or arrival, optional, with a short privacy notice. Paper form or tablet; aggregated before reporting. If a visitor declines, record "unknown," not a guessed estate.
- **Bus carryings:** operator or nominated staff member counts boardings by departure and by estate/other. No personal data.
- **Youth Studio:** partner register records sessions, visits, unique participants and waiting list. The centre does not duplicate the partner's safeguarding data. With up to 18 sessions, report per-session averages and the monthly unique count separately; do not sum monthly uniques.
- **Awareness:** a short 3–4 question survey by mail, phone or online to a sample of mailer recipients. Pre-mailer in week 1; post around weeks 8 and 12. Ask both the general "heard of the centre" question and the specific Thursday evening/bus question. Report the sample frame, n and response rate. If the sample is too small to support a percentage-point estimate, report the raw counts and say so.
- **Training:** register plus a short post-training access-knowledge check (not a formal qualification).
- **Compliance:** a weekly check of the events schedule for any first-floor booking or any event advertised above 60; zero tolerance while the lift is out. The responsible person's session sheet records the cap check.
- **Qualitative:** staff debrief notes, participant feedback and an incident log. These are not statistically representative; use them to explain the numbers, not to replace them.
- **Access requests:** a record of requests for step-free or first-floor access that could not be met. This is evidence for the capital case, not a substitute for the repair.

### 2.2 Equalities monitoring

The council requires one quarterly report with attendance and equalities monitoring for the priority neighbourhoods. The plan will:

- collect postcode and the council's agreed equalities categories where available;
- report attendance and estate share by programme;
- aggregate data and suppress small cells (for example, where a group is so small that an individual could be identified);
- avoid publishing personal data or combining postcode with other identifying fields;
- state where data are missing rather than inferring them;
- report the estate survey baseline (48% never heard; 37% heard but never attended; 15% attended) alongside the quarter's own programme-level data, clearly labelled as a household survey, not a programme attendance measure.

The exact equalities categories and the small-cell threshold should be confirmed with the council before collection. If the council has no standard, use its existing reporting template rather than inventing one.

## 4. Interpretation cautions

These cautions should appear in the quarterly report, not just in this file:

- **No control group.** The comparison is pre/post and same-quarter-last-year. Seasonality, weather, school holidays and the 2025 recovery in evening attendance could all move the numbers. The 2023 pilot is supportive, not a guarantee.
- **The 2025 evening uptick may regress.** Evening Talks per session rose from 44.0 in January to 49.5 in March 2025; some of that may be recovery from the 2024 pause rather than a trend. The April–June 2024 baseline (36.5) is the more conservative comparator.
- **The 60 cap censors demand.** Attendance above 60 cannot be observed. A full house at 60 is not proof that demand is exactly 60; turnaways and waitlist data are the only evidence of unmet demand. Conversely, a below-50 average may reflect the cap only if sessions were full; check the cap status before interpreting.
- **The 2023 uplift applied to the current base would exceed the cap.** 46.5 × 1.38 ≈ 64.2. If demand behaves like 2023, the pilot will turn people away. The report must not compare capped attendance with an uncapped 2023 percentage without saying so.
- **Survey coverage bias.** The 92% satisfaction figure is not a population estimate. Do not use it as a baseline for the pilot or as evidence that non-attenders are satisfied.
- **The estate survey is a small doorstep sample.** The 48% never-heard and 57% transport figures are indicative, not precise; the barrier percentages rest on about 62 aware households. Report the base and do not over-read small movements.
- **Awareness surveys self-select.** People who respond to a mailer survey may be more engaged than non-respondents. Report response rate and treat the result as indicative. General awareness and specific-offer awareness are different measures; do not substitute one for the other.
- **Bus carryings measure use, not new attendance.** Some riders may have driven before or may be existing attenders. Pair carryings with postcode and new/returning status.
- **Youth per-session attendance may fall as sessions expand.** The current 74/session is based on two sessions a month; 12 additional sessions may attract less frequent attenders. The ≥65/session target allows for that. A fall to 60 would not necessarily mean the extra sessions failed, but it would need explanation. The partner's waiting list of 40 is a supply-side signal, not a guarantee that all 12 sessions fill.
- **Estate share excludes unknown postcodes.** If many attendees decline to give a postcode, the estate share could be biased in either direction. Report the unknown share and do not impute.
- **Small numbers.** Thirteen evenings at around 50–60 people, split by two estates and new/returning status, will produce small cells. Use counts and ranges, not false precision.
- **Attribution.** The plan combines five lines. If attendance rises, the bus, the mailer, the training and the youth sessions may all have contributed. The design can show whether the package worked; it cannot isolate each line's causal effect. That is acceptable for a quarterly operational plan, but it should be stated.
- **Evening programme definition.** The 13 Thursday evenings are the quarter's evening programme; existing Evening Talks dates are folded into the weekly slot rather than counted in addition. If separate dates are kept, they count toward the 13 sessions and the 60 cap, and the 650-visit target is not a second, additive total.
- **Cost remains a barrier for some.** The estate survey found 22% of aware residents name cost as the main barrier. The plan does not fund a price subsidy; it can report whether cost appears in feedback, but it cannot claim to have removed the barrier.

## 5. Decision rule

| Result at week 13 | Decision |
|---|---|
| Metrics 1–3 met (≥50/session, ≥50% estate share, bus used), no session >60, and no safety/compliance issues | Continue/scale the evening + bus model next quarter, subject to the new envelope and the 60 cap. Protect the bus if the estate gain is real. |
| Metric 1 or 2 in the middle band (36.5 to below 50/session; 25% to below 50% estate share), or bus use weak | Review and redesign: test timetable, route, promotion, programme mix or an earlier finish. Do not declare success or failure from one quarter. |
| Metric 1 below 36.5/session, or metric 2 below 25%, or bus unused after a full quarter | Stop the pilot. Do not continue evening opening without transport; that is the 2023 failure mode. |
| Metric 10 shows the cap reached in more than 10% of sessions, with turnaways | Continue within the cap; use the turnaway evidence to support the lift capital case. Do not admit above 60. If the board wants to serve the unmet demand, it must fund the accessible entrance first. |
| Metric 6 not met (training incomplete) | Treat as an implementation failure; complete training before drawing conclusions about access. |
| Metric 7 breached (any first-floor event, or any evening event above 60, while the lift/entrance is out) | Stop the event, investigate, and report to the board and the council; this is a compliance issue, not a performance issue. |
| Metric 8 outside the ±10% guardrail | Review whether the pilot is cannibalising Family Saturdays; adjust scheduling/promotion before continuing. |

## 6. What is not measured this quarter

- **Demand above 60.** The cap censors it. Turnaways and waitlist demand are recorded, but the plan cannot say how many people would have attended an uncapped evening.
- **Step-free access and first-floor use.** The lift and accessible entrance remain out; the plan records unmet access requests but does not deliver access.
- **Parking.** The survey's 44 mentions are noted, but there is no quoted parking intervention.
- **Borough-wide awareness.** The mailer is targeted; it does not test a borough campaign.
- **Non-attender demand in general.** The estate survey gives a household baseline, but the quarter's follow-up is a small mailer sample, not a representative panel.
- **Long-term retention.** Thirteen weeks is enough to test attendance and estate reach, not a year of retention.
- **Economic or social impact.** Not in the pack and not costed.
- **The causal effect of each individual line.** The package is tested as a package.

These are limits on what the plan can claim, not reasons to avoid measuring what it can.
