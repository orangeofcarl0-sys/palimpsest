# Options appraisal

**Round:** 2 — revised for the estate survey, the revised lift quote, the Youth Studio clarification and the café notice
**Envelope:** £48,000 for the 13-week quarter, committed at the start, no carry-over

All options are costed only from the quoted lines in `sources/resources.md`, as updated by `updates/U2-lift-quote-revised.md` and `updates/U3-youth-clarification.md`. No option invents a cost, a post or a capital/revenue transfer. The lift is now quoted at **£27,500 with an 8-week lead time**, and the accessible entrance must be operable before any evening event with more than 60 attendees is licensed. Those two changes remove the round-1 "lift + evening + bus" fallback, which is now £51,500 and over the envelope.

The appraisal criteria are deliberately evidence-first rather than a weighted score:

- **Participation impact** — does it reach the priority estates and add visits, not just rearrange them?
- **Access impact** — does it remove a measured barrier (transport, step-free access, communication, welcome)?
- **Strength of evidence** — has this or something close to it been tested here?
- **Affordability and resilience** — does it fit £48,000, and would it survive the possible 15% reduction next quarter?
- **Deliverability** — can it run for 13 weeks within the staffing, building and season constraints?

## 2. Option A — recommended: capped After Hours + Youth + targeted comms + training

### What it buys

| Line | Cost | What it does |
|---|---:|---|
| Evening opening, 13 weeks (responsible person + utilities + cleaning) | £9,600 | Opens the building on Thursday evenings for 13 weeks, using existing staff extended, not a new post. The audience is hard-capped at 60 to meet the new licensing condition. |
| Late bus subsidy: one minibus on the Northside–Kiln Field loop, 13 weeks | £14,400 | Provides 19:15 and 21:30 departures so estate residents can get to and from the evening programme. |
| Additional Youth Studio sessions (quoted at 2 per month; partner confirms up to 4 extra per month at the same £6,500) | £6,500 | Delivers up to 12 additional sessions over the quarter (18 in total, six a month), with the partner supplying staff and the centre supplying room and materials; the waiting list of 40 is used first. |
| Targeted mailer to priority-neighbourhood households | £4,200 | A specific offer to a specific audience, with printing and postage included. The new estate survey gives a general awareness baseline: 48% had never heard of the centre. |
| Staff training: welcome and access awareness, 2 days | £2,800 | Builds front-of-house confidence on access, welcome, the 60-person cap and the bus/evening arrangements. |
| **Total** | **£37,500** | **£10,500 headroom** |

### Why it is the best fit

- It funds the **only positive participation intervention in the pack with direct, local, before-and-after evidence**: evening opening plus a late minibus produced a 38% evening attendance gain, with most new attendees from the two estates, and the gain reversed when the bus was removed.
- It addresses the **dominant stated barrier**. The new estate survey finds that, among residents who have heard of the centre, 57% name getting home after an evening event as the main barrier. The 19:10 last bus is the concrete manifestation of that barrier, and the late minibus is the only quoted transport fix.
- It responds to the **awareness gap without repeating the 2022 mistake**. The new survey finds that 48% of surveyed estate households have never heard of the centre, so a targeted mailer is better justified than round 1 assumed. But the council has not tested whether awareness alone converts, and the 2022 borough-wide print/mail-drop did not move first-time attendance. The mailer is therefore paired with the bus and a specific offer, not treated as a stand-alone conversion strategy.
- It responds to the **Youth Studio supply constraint**, which the partner has now confirmed in writing. The same £6,500 buys up to four additional sessions a month, not two, and the partner has a waiting list of 40.
- It keeps the **1.5 FTE front-of-house** and no-new-post constraints intact. The evening line explicitly assumes existing staff are extended.
- It **complies with the new licensing condition** by hard-capping evening attendance at 60. It does not pretend the full 180-seat hall is available while the accessible entrance is out.
- It is **resilient to the possible 15% cut**: £37,500 fits inside a 15% reduction of the £48,000 envelope (£40,800), with £3,300 to spare. The lift-plus-evening-plus-bus package at £51,500 does not fit at all.

### Trade-offs and risks

- **The lift is not fixed.** All activity is ground-floor; first-floor public events remain prohibited. The plan does not claim to restore step-free access. It also cannot serve more than 60 evening attendees, so the 2023 model can be tested only in a capped form. This is the main cost of choosing A.
- **The cap may bind.** Current Evening Talks attendance is 46.5 per session. If the 2023 38% uplift applied to that current base, demand would be about 64 per session — above the 60 cap. The pilot may therefore turn people away. Turnaways and waitlist numbers are recorded as evidence of unmet demand and as part of the capital case for the lift. The plan does not claim the full 2023 uplift can be served.
- **The mailer is untested.** The 2022 package included a mail-drop and failed to convert. The targeted mailer is different, but it needs a baseline, a post-test and a kill criterion.
- **Thirteen evenings stretch 1.5 FTE.** The rota needs a named responsible person and a backup; if cover fails, that evening is paused rather than run unsafely.
- **The bus quote is not a full operating spec.** Direction, stops, capacity, insurance and accessibility must be confirmed before week 1.
- **The extra Youth Studio sessions depend on room and scheduling capacity**, even though the partner's staffing capacity is now confirmed.
- **Cost is a stated barrier for 22% of aware estate residents.** The plan does not include a price subsidy and does not change prices. It can state existing pricing clearly, but it cannot claim to remove the cost barrier this quarter.

## 3. Option B — access-first, no evening or bus

### What it buys

Lift repair and accessible entrance reinstatement (£27,500) + Youth Studio extras (£6,500) + targeted mailer (£4,200) + training (£2,800) = **£41,000**, leaving £7,000.

### Strengths

- It restores the **accessible entrance and passenger lift**. After the 8-week lead time, it would remove the first-floor prohibition and the 60-person evening condition, provided the work is certified.
- It funds the expanded Youth Studio, the targeted mailer and staff training.
- It is the coherent affordable package if the board makes **step-free access the overriding precondition** for new programming. It does not pretend to run an evening programme it cannot make accessible.

### Why it is not recommended this quarter

- **It does not fund the evening pilot or the late bus.** The estates' dominant stated barrier — getting home after an evening event — is left untouched, and the strongest local participation evidence is not used.
- **Within this package, the £7,000 cannot buy either missing element without dropping another line**: adding the bus (£14,400) or the evening opening (£9,600) would push the package over the envelope, and adding both would cost £65,000.
- **It is £200 short of a 15% reduced envelope**: £41,000 versus £40,800. It is close, but not resilient without dropping a line.
- **It is an access-and-audience quarter, not a participation quarter.** That may be the right choice if the board's priority is step-free access, but it should be labelled honestly.

**When B would be right:** if the board decides that restoring step-free access is the overriding precondition for any new programming, and accepts that the evening programme and the bus are deferred. That is a legitimate choice, but it should be made explicitly: it trades the best-evidenced estate transport intervention for building access. It should not be described as equivalent to Option A.

## 4. Option C — lift + evening + bus: no longer affordable

### What it buys

Lift (£27,500) + evening opening (£9,600) + late bus (£14,400) = **£51,500**.

### Why it is not available

This was the round-1 fallback at £46,000, when the lift was quoted at £22,000 with a six-week lead. The revised quote and lead time change the arithmetic:

- £51,500 exceeds the £48,000 envelope by **£3,500**.
- The lift cannot be phased or part-funded, and capital and revenue cannot be swapped without a council variation.
- Dropping the Youth Studio, mailer and training would not close the gap while keeping both the evening opening and the bus; the three-line package is already over.

Option C is therefore **not a valid option within the current envelope**. If the board wants lift plus evening plus bus, it needs a council capital variation or additional capital, not a re-cut of this revenue plan.

## 5. Option D — core only: evening + bus

### What it buys

Evening opening (£9,600) + late bus (£14,400) = **£24,000**, leaving £24,000.

### Assessment

This is the safest, most evidence-backed core. It would run the 2023 pilot again, capped at 60, with better measurement. But it leaves the Youth Studio supply problem unaddressed, does nothing targeted about the communication gap, and leaves half the envelope uncommitted. The unspent £24,000 cannot be carried forward, and there is no quoted item that usefully fills it without either repeating the print mistake or buying unrelated café equipment.

**When D would be right:** under a severe cut, or if the board wants a minimal, single-intervention test before committing to the wider plan. It is the fallback if the partner cannot deliver additional Youth Studio sessions and the board does not want the mailer/training.

## 6. Option E — lift + evening + Youth + training, no bus

### What it buys

Lift (£27,500) + evening opening (£9,600) + Youth Studio extras (£6,500) + training (£2,800) = **£46,400**, leaving £1,600.

### Why it is rejected

- **It removes the late bus.** The 2023 pilot is explicit: when the minibus stopped, attendance fell back and the new estate attendees stopped coming. Funding evening opening without transport would repeat the known failure mode.
- **It is a sequencing trap if step-free access is the precondition.** The lift takes eight weeks from instruction. If the board requires step-free access before new programming, the evening cannot start until around week 9, so most of the 13-week evening line is unusable. If the evening runs before the lift is certified, it contradicts the precondition.
- **It leaves only £1,600 headroom**, so it is not resilient to the possible 15% cut; a 15% cut to the envelope (£40,800) would leave it £5,600 short.

It is not the same as Option B: B drops the evening to avoid the sequencing trap. E keeps the evening but cannot sequence it honestly.

---

## 7. Option F — print-led: borough print + evening + bus + Youth Studio

### What it buys

Borough print and poster campaign (£9,000) + evening (£9,600) + bus (£14,400) + Youth Studio extras (£6,500) = **£39,500**, leaving £8,500.

### Why it is rejected

The 2022 campaign is the clearest negative evidence in the pack: £9,000 on borough-wide posters and leaflets plus a mail-drop produced attendance within normal seasonal variation and no measurable change in first-time attendance from the priority estates. Word of mouth remained dominant at 64%. The board minuted: *"Do not repeat the poster spend without a specific audience and a specific offer."* A borough-wide print campaign is the opposite of that condition.

The new estate survey's 48% never-heard finding does not rescue the borough print option. It shows that awareness is uneven, but the 2022 campaign already tested a mass print/mail-drop and did not convert. The targeted mailer in Option A is the smaller, specific-audience alternative. It is still a test, not a proven success, but it does not repeat the 2022 design and it is paired with the transport fix.

---

## 8. Option G — status quo

### Why it is rejected

The barriers are already documented: the estates are two bus changes away, the last bus is 19:10, half of surveyed estate households have never heard of the centre, the Youth Studio is running at half its previous frequency despite a waiting list of 40, the lift and accessible entrance are out, and lapsed evening attendees were not told the programme had returned. Doing nothing leaves all of that in place and forgoes the quarter's only opportunity to test the 2023 transport finding under current conditions.

## 9. The café equipment refresh

The café equipment refresh (£7,500) is **not an available option this quarter**. The supplier notice says it has been deferred to the next financial year. The café's coffee supplier is changing from Bean & Co to Harbour Roasters, and prices to visitors are unchanged; the notice says this does not change the programming budget. Round 1 already excluded the café refresh as unrelated to programming. The new notice confirms that exclusion and removes the line from the option set entirely.

## 10. Recommendation

**Choose Option A.** It is the only option that combines:

- the strongest local evidence (evening + late bus, 2023), operated within the current 60-person licensing cap;
- the clearest supply correction (Youth Studio frequency), now confirmed by the partner and expanded to up to four extra sessions a month at the same price;
- a targeted, testable communications intervention (mailer, not borough print), now informed by a 48% never-heard baseline;
- a low-cost access enabler (training);
- full compliance with the £48,000 envelope, the no-new-post rule, the ground-floor restriction, the 60-person evening cap and the 13-week season;
- enough headroom to survive a 15% reduction of the envelope next quarter.

The recommendation is not "do everything." It deliberately excludes the lift, borough print, café equipment, new posts and unquoted programming. The lift is the main unresolved access issue, now £27,500 with an 8-week lead, and it should be handled as a separate capital decision rather than smuggled into a revenue plan.

**Decision requested from the board:**

1. Approve Option A and commit £37,500 at the start of the quarter.
2. Note the £10,500 uncommitted balance and confirm it is not a carry-over reserve.
3. Approve the **hard cap of 60 attendees** on every evening event until the accessible entrance is operable, with booking/waitlist control and a compliance log. This is a licensing condition, not a discretionary choice.
4. Authorise a separate capital conversation about the **£27,500** lift repair and accessible entrance, with an **8-week** lead time and a council variation if capital is not already available. Do not assume the £10,500 headroom can be used for it; it is £17,000 short.
5. Approve the data collection and equalities monitoring in `metrics.md`.
6. Agree the week-13 stop/go rule in `plan.md` so the bus decision is made on evidence, not by default.

**If the board's overriding priority is step-free access, choose Option B instead — but do so explicitly, and accept that the evening programme and the bus are deferred.** The one thing the board should not do is add the lift to Option A: that costs £65,000 and breaches the envelope by £17,000. Nor should it assume the round-1 £46,000 lift-plus-evening-plus-bus option still exists; it is now £51,500 and unaffordable.
