# Uncertainties

Ranked by **consequence if wrong × likelihood of being wrong**. Each entry states what
would resolve it, by when, and how the plan behaves in the meantime. Nothing here is
resolved by assertion; where the pack is silent, the entry says so.

**A note on IDs.** These are prefixed **UNC-** so they cannot be confused with the round-2
update files (`updates/U1`–`U4`), which are evidence, not uncertainties. Where an update
resolves an uncertainty, the entry says so.

---

## Tier 1 — could stop the plan or a major strand

### UNC-1. Can 1.5 FTE front-of-house actually cover thirteen Thursday evenings?
**What we do not know.** `constraints.md` states the centre has 1.5 FTE across the whole
week, has no events manager post, and that "evening opening requires a named responsible
person on site, which the current rota does not staff." Yet `resources.md` prices evening
opening at £9,600 on the assumption that "existing staff [are] extended, not a new post."
Those two statements are in tension. Thirteen evenings add roughly 50 staffed hours
across the quarter — only around 7% of a 1.5 FTE establishment, but concentrated into a
weekly late shift that the current rota does not contain, on top of the existing weekday
and Saturday programme. The problem is the shape of the hours, not their total.

**Why it matters.** Without a named responsible person the building cannot lawfully open
in the evening. This is a precondition, not a risk to be managed during delivery.

**What resolves it.** A written rota naming the responsible person for all 13 dates,
agreed before the quarter starts (week 0, G0).

**Meanwhile.** The plan treats it as a hard gate: if not confirmed, the evening strand is
**not committed**, the fallback package (Youth Studio + training, £9,300) is committed
instead, and £38,700 is returned. The mailer is not part of the fallback because it is
built around two dated pilot evenings. No attempt is made to run the evening thinly.

**Residual.** Even if the rota is confirmed, sustained evening working by a small team
carries a burnout and sickness risk that no line in the budget addresses. There is no
quoted cost for backfill or overtime, so it cannot be bought this quarter. Recorded, not
solved.

---

### UNC-2. Does the 2023 result reproduce — and does it survive the quarter?
**What we do not know.** The 2023 pilot is a single observation, in a single quarter, at
one venue, with no control group. It may have benefited from novelty; it may have
benefited from a particular programme; the +38% may not be repeatable. And the reversal
after the minibus stopped suggests the effect is **contingent on continuing spend**, not a
permanent change in behaviour.

**Why it matters.** It is the entire evidential basis for the largest line in the budget
(£24,000, 50% of the envelope).

**What resolves it.** The quarter itself, read against pre-registered thresholds
(M1, M2, durability window weeks 9–13) with the Choir and Family Saturdays as controls.

**Meanwhile.** The target is set *below* the observed 2023 effect (+34% vs +38%), and the
plan is explicit that a volume gain without an estate-share gain counts as failure.

**What this plan cannot resolve.** Whether the effect persists beyond a quarter. Nothing
in a 13-week window can answer that, and the 2023 evidence points the pessimistic way. The
60-attendee cap also means this quarter cannot reproduce the 2023 comparison exactly: 2023
was uncapped, this quarter is capped, so a volume shortfall may be a cap artefact rather
than a weaker effect. The turnaway log is the correction.

---

### UNC-3. Does the 60-attendee licensing condition catch the Youth Studio and other non-Talks sessions?
**What we do not know.** The new condition says the accessible entrance must be operable
before "any evening event with more than 60 attendees is licensed", whether or not the
event uses the first floor (`updates/U2`). The pilot's evening events are plainly covered.
The Youth Studio is a partner-run youth session, currently drawing about 74 visits per
session, and it is not obviously a "licensed evening event" — but the pack does not define
the term. If the condition catches it, the Studio is already over 60 and every extra
session would be capped at 60.

**Why it matters.** It could cut the Studio's hard ceiling from 18 × ~74 to 18 × 60 =
1,080 visits, and it could mean the existing programme is in breach today. Family
Saturdays draw 110–120 per session but are daytime events, so the condition should not
catch them; that should be confirmed rather than assumed.

**What resolves it.** Written confirmation from the council's building control officer in
week 0, before the first session, recorded against the licence.

**Meanwhile.** The M4 target is set at **1,000 visits**, which is achievable under either
reading (55.6/session against a 60 cap leaves 4.4 per session of headroom). The plan does
not assume the cap is inapplicable. If it applies, sessions run at 60 or below and the
shortfall against 1,000 is reported; if it does not, the upside is reported.

**Residual.** If the existing Studio is caught and is already above 60, the centre needs a
compliance plan before week 1 — cap the room or change the session time — and no cost for
either is quoted. That would be a board decision, not an operational workaround.

## Tier 2 — shapes delivery

### UNC-4. Can the partner deliver the confirmed block, and can the room absorb six sessions a month?
**What we do not know — and what is now resolved.** Round 1 did not know whether the
partner could staff a third session a month. `updates/U3` resolves that: the partner has
confirmed in writing that it can supply staff for up to **four extra sessions a month** for
the quarter at the same £6,500, using its 40-name waiting list first. The remaining
uncertainty is delivery: whether the room is on the ground floor (no first-floor public
event is permitted while the lift is out), whether it can absorb six sessions a month,
whether the waitlist converts, and whether the partner's own funding stays stable through
the quarter.

**Why it matters.** This is the best-value line in the plan (£14 per additional visit,
roughly one-tenth the cost of an evening visit) and it rests on the strongest demand signal
in the pack — 115 monthly unique participants and a 40-name waitlist.

**What resolves it.** The partner's written block confirmation (held), a room-capacity check
before week 1, and monthly registers against M4/M5.

**Meanwhile.** If the partner delivers fewer than four extra sessions, the plan runs what
can be staffed and reports the shortfall (S6). The £6,500 is a block price, not a
per-session rate; it is returned only if the block cannot run at all. It is not reallocated
to an unquoted item.

---

### UNC-5. What does "19:15 and 21:30 departures" actually mean?
**What we do not know.** The operator quote does not say whether these depart from the
centre or from the estates. If 19:15 leaves the estates, arrival is ~19:35 and the
programme cannot start before 19:40. If 19:15 is an early return trip from the centre,
the inbound pattern is unconstrained.

**Why it matters.** It determines the start time, and therefore which films can be shown
and how long a talk can run. Get it wrong and the audience is stranded at 21:30 or
arrives for the second half.

**What resolves it.** The operator's written timetable, week 0.

**Meanwhile.** The plan defaults to the conservative reading (19:40 start, 20:55 hard
finish, films capped at 70 minutes), which works under either interpretation.

---

### UNC-6. How many people from Northside and Kiln Field actually attend now?
**What we do not know.** At programme level, nothing. `attendance.csv` holds no postcode,
neighbourhood, age or disability field. There is no baseline for the council's equalities
monitoring and no way to say which programme currently reaches the priority estates
(`open-questions.md` §5). `updates/U1` gives a household-level figure — 15% of estate
households have attended at least once, 48% have never heard of the centre, 37% have heard
but not attended — but it cannot say which programmes they use or how often they come.

**Why it matters.** It is the council's stated reporting requirement and the pilot's
primary outcome. Without it, "reach improved" is an unfalsifiable claim.

**What resolves it.** Postcode capture from week 1; first reading by week 6; 80% capture
target.

**Meanwhile.** The plan reports the first figure as **a baseline, not an improvement**.
That distinction is written into `metrics.md` §5 and must survive into the council report.
`updates/U1`'s 15% is not a substitute for it and must not be presented as one.

---

### UNC-7. How large is the latent evening demand from the estates — and how much of it can the cap see?
**What we do not know.** The 2023 record says "the majority of new evening attendees came
from the two priority estates" but gives no counts. No passenger numbers from the 2023
minibus survived into this pack. So the plan knows the direction of the effect and not
its size, which makes the 35% target a reasoned translation rather than a measurement.
The 60-attendee cap now adds a second unknown: demand above 60 cannot be observed, only
inferred from turnaways, and people who see a full sign and leave are not counted. A
single minibus may also bind before the cap does.

**Why it matters.** It determines whether £14,400 a quarter for a minibus is proportionate
or whether a smaller, differently-shaped service would do.

**What resolves it.** Passenger counts per trip from week 1 (M3), a door count and turnaway
log every evening (M13), and the estate-share reading from week 3 (M2). By week 4 there will
be more hard data on minibus demand than existed after the entire 2023 pilot.

**Meanwhile.** The report gives attendance plus turnaways as a **demand floor**, not a
demand estimate, and says so.

---

### UNC-8. What is the cost of deferring the lift repair?
**What we do not know.** How many people the broken accessible entrance and lift exclude,
because the centre has no disability data either. The barrier is certain
(`constraints.md`, `updates/U2`); its size is invisible.

**Why it matters.** The plan defers a **£27,500** capital item that addresses a real and
current exclusion and now also caps every evening event at 60. That is a defensible
programme-budget decision and an uncomfortable human one, and it should not be softened in
the telling.

**What resolves it.** A separate capital bid at £27,500, instructed as early as the capital
process allows. An 8-week lead means an instruction in week 0 completes around week 8,
allowing events above 60 in weeks 9–13; an instruction in week 1 of next quarter completes
around week 9 of that quarter. Plus: begin recording access requirements at the door
(already part of the capture form) so the next bid has a number attached.

**Meanwhile.** All programming is ground floor, every evening is capped at 60, and the
exclusion and the cap are recorded as standing equalities and capacity findings in the
week-6 and week-13 council reports rather than passed over.

**If the board overrules this.** There is no longer a coherent revenue package that
contains the lift. Lift + evening + bus is £51,500, £3,500 over, with no Studio, training
or mailer. Lift + evening + bus + Studio is £58,000, £10,000 over. To fund the lift from
the £48,000 the board would have to drop the bus — which is the 2023 failure mode — or drop
the evening programme, which leaves the lift with nothing to open on to. The coherent route
is a capital bid outside the revenue envelope.

### UNC-9. Will the council supply the priority-neighbourhood postcode list?
**What we do not know.** The postcode list that defines Northside and Kiln Field is not in
this pack. The council requires reporting *by* those neighbourhoods but the definition is
not held here.

**Why it matters.** Without it, postcodes can be captured but not mapped to estates, and
M2 cannot be reported.

**What resolves it.** Request in week 0; escalation at week 3 if not received.

**Meanwhile.** Report "postcodes captured, not yet mapped" and say so plainly. Do not
infer estate membership from assumption. `updates/U1`'s estate-level figures are the council's own
and do not substitute for the list.

---

### UNC-10. Can anything be done about cost as a barrier?
**What we do not know.** Whether the 22% of aware estate households who name cost as the
main barrier (`updates/U1`) would attend if tickets or fares were subsidised. The survey
measures a stated barrier, not a willingness to pay.

**Why it matters.** It is the second-largest stated barrier, ahead of programme content
(11%), and the plan does not address it at all. A report that claims to have improved
access while leaving cost untouched would be overclaiming.

**What resolves it.** A written quote for a ticket or fare subsidy — not on the current
menu, so it cannot be bought this quarter — and a small test next quarter if the quote is
affordable.

**Meanwhile.** The plan records cost as an unaddressed barrier in every report, and does
not present the mailer or the minibus as a cost remedy. The free-entry Family Saturdays
are the only existing zero-price offer and they are daytime, so they do not answer the
evening barrier.

## Tier 3 — known gaps that limit what the plan can claim

### UNC-11. Is £147 per additional evening visit good value?
**What we do not know.** The pack holds no programme cost data, so neither the Youth
Studio's £14 nor the pilot's £147 can be benchmarked against what existing programmes cost
per visit. The comparison between the two interventions is valid; the comparison to
"business as usual" is not possible.

**What resolves it.** Unit cost data for existing programmes — not in this pack, not
costed here, and not invented.

---

### UNC-12. Will the council cut the budget, and by how much?
**What we do not know.** "Verbal indication… up to 15%… nothing confirmed and no figure
agreed" (`resources.md`).

**Why it matters.** It determines whether the evening strand can become recurrent.

**Meanwhile.** Treated as a stress scenario, not a number. The committed plan is £37,500;
it already fits a 15%-reduced envelope (£40,800) with £3,300 to spare, and the cut ladder
is pre-agreed (`budget.md` §5) so the decision is not improvised.

---

### UNC-13. Will the targeted mailer do anything?
**What we do not know.** Prior evidence is negative: the 2022 package *included a
mail-drop* and produced no measurable change in first-time attendance from the priority
estates. `updates/U1` changes the picture by showing a real awareness gap — 48% of estate
households have never heard of the centre — but the equalities team explicitly says it has
not tested whether awareness alone converts. The mailer's defence is that it has a specific
audience, two dated offers, a booking route and transport behind it; that is an argument,
not a result.

**Why it matters.** £4,200, and more importantly the risk of concluding "we told them and
they still didn't come" when the real problem was cost (22%), transport (57%) or something
else.

**What resolves it.** Week-4 response count against the ≥30 threshold.

**Meanwhile.** It is the first line cut, it is a single wave with two dates, and there is
no second wave this quarter even if it succeeds, because a second wave was not committed at
the start and would pre-empt the result. It is the first call on next quarter if wave one
clears 30.

---

### UNC-14. Will the pilot displace existing activity?
**What we do not know.** Whether stretching 1.5 FTE across thirteen extra evenings degrades
the weekday and Saturday programme. Guardrail metrics (M7, M8) will show it if it happens,
but a 10% floor may be too loose to catch early erosion.

**What resolves it.** Weekly dashboard read alongside the evening numbers, not after them.

---

### UNC-15. What is the Youth Studio's room capacity?
**What we do not know.** `constraints.md` gives the main hall at 180 seated but says
nothing about the Studio room. At 74 visits/session the Studio may already be near its
ceiling, in which case adding sessions adds less than the linear projection assumes — and
the plan now proposes six sessions a month, up from two.

**Why it matters.** The +476 visit target assumes the 18 sessions average 55.6. If the room
caps at, say, 60, that is close to the target and leaves little headroom; if it caps lower,
the target is unreachable. If the 60-attendee licensing condition also applies (UNC-3), the
hard ceiling is 1,080 and the room is not the binding constraint.

**Meanwhile.** The target already discounts current performance by 25% for dilution and
cap risk. If capacity turns out to be the binding constraint rather than frequency, the
finding is recorded — it would mean the Studio needs a bigger room, not more sessions.

---

### UNC-16. Safeguarding on a late minibus
**What we do not know.** Whether unaccompanied young people travelling home at 21:30 fall
within the partner's existing safeguarding policy or need a separate arrangement. No
policy document is in the pack and no cost for an escort is quoted.

**Why it matters.** A serious incident would end the pilot and damage the partnership.

**What resolves it.** Written confirmation from the partner and the operator before week 1.

## What this plan deliberately leaves open

Not everything unresolved is a failure. These are left open on purpose, to be decided
with data rather than guessed now:

- **Whether the evening programme runs weekly or fortnightly.** Weekly is assumed
  (13 openings), but if the rota or the audience suggests otherwise, fortnightly is a
  legitimate in-quarter change — it does not alter the budget, only the session count and
  the cap arithmetic.
- **Which films and talks fill the slots.** Constrained only by the 70-minute cap and the
  20:55 finish. Programme selection is an operational matter, not a planning one.
- **Whether a second mailer wave runs.** Not this quarter: it was not committed at the
  start and would pre-empt the week-4 result. It is the first call on next quarter.
- **Whether the 19:40 start is necessary.** Depends on UNC-5.
- **What replaces the pilot if transport cannot be funded recurrently.** The zero-cost
  fallback (earlier start so the 19:10 bus is usable) is identified but not designed,
  because designing it now would pre-empt the week-10 decision.
- **Whether the lift bid is approved this quarter or next.** Depends on the capital
  process, not on the revenue plan. The earlier it is instructed, the sooner the 60 cap
  can be lifted.
