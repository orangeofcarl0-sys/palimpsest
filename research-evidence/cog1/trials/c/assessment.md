# Evidence assessment — Riversdale Community Arts Centre

**Purpose.** Establish what the pack actually supports before any money is committed,
and mark clearly where it supports nothing. Every claim below is tied to a file in
`sources/` or `updates/`. Where a source speaks confidently beyond its own method, that
is recorded as a limitation rather than repeated as fact.

**Round 2 note.** Four items arrived between rounds. They are treated as evidence, not as
instructions, and they are numbered separately from the round-1 uncertainties: `updates/U1`
(the estates doorstep survey), `updates/U2` (the lift re-quote and the new building-control
condition), `updates/U3` (the partner's written clarification of the Youth Studio), and
`updates/U4` (the café notice). Their evidence entries are E23–E26. Uncertainty IDs in
this plan are prefixed **UNC-** so that they cannot be confused with the update files.

**Headline judgement.** The centre does not have a demand problem among the people it
reaches, nor a satisfaction problem among attenders. It has three access problems —
transport and timing, session frequency and physical access — an awareness problem that
is **uneven rather than universal**, and a measurement problem: it cannot say who it
reaches, because it holds no equalities data at all. The strongest single piece of
evidence remains the accidental natural experiment in `prior-interventions.md` §1, which
isolates transport as the binding constraint on evening attendance. The new estates survey
sharpens that finding rather than replacing it: among estate households that have heard of
the centre, getting home after an evening event is the dominant stated barrier (57%), but
almost half of estate households have never heard of the centre at all (48%).

## 2. What the evidence actually establishes

**(a) Satisfaction is high, and irrelevant to the goal.** 92% is a real number about
a real audience. It is also the audience that already comes, on the day that already
works, in the mode (car, with children) that already works. `open-questions.md` §1 asks
whether 92% means the centre can stop worrying about audience development. The survey's
own author answers it: the survey covers "the people who come on a Saturday". High
satisfaction among attenders and low reach among non-attenders are not in tension —
they are the standard shape of an access problem. Nothing in the pack measures a single
non-attender's behaviour, although E23 now measures non-attenders' **stated** reasons.

**(b) The Youth Studio decline is a supply artefact, and the partner now says so.** This
was the clearest analytical result in round 1 and it is no longer an inference. The
partner has confirmed in writing that its own funding cut halved delivery from four
sessions a month to two in June 2024, that this is the whole reason the visit figure fell,
that unique participants rose from 71 to 115 over the same period, and that it now holds a
waiting list of 40 (`updates/U3`, E25). Visits per session rose 50.8 → 67.8; monthly
unique participants rose 62%; visits fell 27% only because sessions halved (E6). A demand
collapse cannot produce that pattern. `open-questions.md` §2 — "are young people losing
interest?" — can be answered **no**, and the speculation that they had "moved on" has been
withdrawn by the organisation that made it. The remaining question is not demand but
delivery: can the partner staff four extra sessions a month, and can the room absorb them
(UNC-4, UNC-15)?

**(c) Transport gates evening attendance.** Three independent sources converge: the
front-of-house account (E8), the hard 19:10 last bus (E10), and 71% car travel in the
survey (E9). The natural experiment then settles it: the same programme, staff and
building drew +38% with a minibus and fell back to pre-pilot level within six weeks of the
minibus stopping (E11). `updates/U1` adds a fourth, independent signal: among estate
households that have heard of the centre, 57% name getting home after an evening event as
the main barrier — far ahead of cost (22%) and programme content (11%) (E23). That is a
stated barrier, not a measured behaviour, but it points the same way as the 2023 reversal.
`open-questions.md` §3 — "the 2023 pilot worked, why did it stop working?" — the answer is
that the transport subsidy, not the programme, was doing the work, and it was withdrawn
abruptly enough that the new audience was lost rather than retained at a lower level.

**(d) Awareness is uneven, not absent.** Round 1 concluded from the 2022 null that
"awareness is not the binding constraint". That was too strong for the priority estates.
E23 finds that 48% of estate households have never heard of the centre, 37% have heard of
it but never attended, and only 15% have attended at least once. Awareness is therefore a
real gap for about half the estates, and a plausible barrier — not a proven one, because
nobody has tested whether awareness converts. This **qualifies** the 2022 result rather than
overturning it: the 2022 campaign was borough-wide, had no specific offer, and did not
measure estate awareness; what it established is that generic print did not convert. The
board's minute — *"do not repeat the poster spend without a specific audience and a
specific offer"* — still rules out another borough-wide campaign. It does not rule out a
targeted approach to a named audience with a dated offer, which is what the mailer is.
The honest position is: **there is an awareness gap for about half the estates, transport
is the dominant stated barrier for those who know the centre, and nobody has tested
whether awareness alone converts** (E23 covering note; UNC-13).

**(e) Reach is unmeasured, and that is a finding, not a gap to be papered over.**
There is no postcode or equalities field anywhere in `attendance.csv`. The council
requires quarterly equalities monitoring for the two priority estates
(`constraints.md` Reporting). The centre currently cannot produce it. Any plan that
claims to improve reach must first build the instrument that can see reach — otherwise
the claim is unfalsifiable and the report to the council is unevidenced. E23 can say how
many estate households have attended **at least once**, but it cannot say which
programmes they use, or how many visits that represents.

**(f) Physical access now gates scale, not only first-floor use.** The new building-control
condition bars any evening event above 60 attendees until the accessible entrance is
operable, whether or not the event uses the first floor (E24). The lift is out of service
and the repair is capital at £27,500 with an 8-week lead. So the evening programme can run
this quarter only under a hard 60-attendee cap, and the 180-seat hall stops being the
binding capacity. This makes the lift a growth constraint as well as an equalities one —
and it remains unaffordable inside this revenue envelope (see `options.md` §4). The
condition cuts both ways: it strengthens the case for the capital bid, and it weakens what
the revenue pilot can demonstrate about demand above 60.

**(g) Cost is a secondary barrier the plan cannot address.** Among estate households that
have heard of the centre, 22% name cost as the main barrier (E23). The quoted menu
contains no ticket subsidy, no free-ticket line and no transport fare subsidy; anything not
on the menu needs a written quote and cannot be committed this quarter (E19). The plan
records this as an unaddressed barrier (UNC-10) rather than pretending the mailer or the
minibus answers it. It is also a reason to commission a quote for next quarter.

## 3. What actually binds

| Constraint | Type | Binds what | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Last bus 19:10; no staffed car park after 20:00 | Access | Any evening activity that finishes after ~18:45 for bus users | E8, E10 |
| **No evening event above 60 attendees until the accessible entrance is operable** | Licensing / physical access | The size of every evening event, on any floor; the 180-seat hall is no longer the binding capacity | E16, E24 |
| Lift out of service; first floor prohibited; repair capital at £27,500 with an 8-week lead | Physical access / capital | Any first-floor use; a whole class of disabled visitors, currently uncounted; any event above 60 | E16, E24 |
| No named responsible person on the evening rota; 1.5 FTE FOH | Staffing | Evening opening at all — a safety and licensing precondition, not a preference | E17 |
| Partner organisation supplies Youth Studio staff | Delivery | Whether the confirmed four-session block can actually be delivered in the room; £6,500 buys a block of sessions, **not** unlimited partner capacity | E7, E19, E25 |
| £48,000 fixed, no carry-over, closed cost menu | Financial | Everything; no new posts, no commissioned evaluation, no ticket subsidy, no unquoted item | E18, E19 |
| No equalities data | Measurement | Any credible claim about priority-neighbourhood reach | E20, E23 |
| Cost named as main barrier by 22% of aware estate households | Access | Any price-based route to attendance; no quoted subsidy exists | E23, E19 |

The financial envelope is **still not** the binding constraint: the plan commits £37,500
and leaves £10,500. The binding constraints are the rota, the bus timetable, the
60-attendee licensing cap and the closed menu. This matters for how the plan is built: it
is a scheduling, staffing and licensing problem with a budget attached, not a budget
problem with activities attached.

## 4. What the pack cannot tell us

1. **How many people from Northside and Kiln Field currently attend, or which
   programmes they use.** E23 gives a household-level "ever attended" share (15%), but no
   postcode data exists at the door. (Q5 remains unanswerable at programme level.)
2. **How many disabled visitors the centre has, or has lost,** since the accessible
   entrance failed. No disability data exists.
3. **Whether four extra Youth Studio sessions a month will fill.** The partner can staff
   them and has a waitlist of 40; the pack does not give the room's capacity or the rate at
   which waitlisted young people attend.
4. **The size of the evening market.** Nothing measures people who would come in the
   evening if transport existed; the 2023 +38% is the only estimate and it is a
   single-quarter, single-site observation. The 60-attendee cap now censors observation
   above 60, so the quarter cannot measure the top of the demand curve.
5. **Whether the 2023 effect persists beyond two quarters,** since it was never tested
   beyond the point where funding stopped.
6. **What a 15% reduction would actually be,** since the council has said nothing
   confirmed and agreed no figure.
7. **Whether minibus demand is sufficient to justify a 13-week contract,** since no
   passenger counts from 2023 survived into this pack.
8. **Whether awareness converts.** E23 measures stated barriers and "ever attended"; it
   does not test whether a household that has never heard of the centre would attend if it
   did. The mailer is the test, and it is a single wave.
9. **Whether the 60-attendee condition catches the Youth Studio and other non-Talks
   sessions.** The condition says "evening event … licensed"; the Studio is a partner-run
   youth session and is not obviously a licensed evening event, but the pack does not
   define the term (UNC-3).

These absences shape the plan directly: the pilot is designed so that its central
question can be answered with data the centre will own by week 6, and the measurement
work is sequenced *before* the spend that depends on it.
