# The pilot — Evening Access Pilot

**One line.** Open the building on Thursday evenings for thirteen weeks *and* run the
Northside–Kiln Field minibus on the same thirteen evenings, as a single coupled
intervention, so that the centre can find out whether the 2023 result reproduces — and
whether it can be held rather than lost when the money stops. Every evening is capped at
**60 attendees** until the accessible entrance is operable; the pilot is designed to work
inside that cap, not to pretend it is not there.

**Cost.** £24,000 of the £37,500 package (£9,600 evening opening + £14,400 minibus).
**Duration.** 13 weeks, weeks 1–13 of the quarter.
**Owner.** Front-of-house coordinator (delivery), finance trustee (budget), board
(decision gates at weeks 0, 4 and 10).

---

## 1. Why this is the pilot and not one of the others

Three reasons, in order of strength:

1. **It is the only intervention in the pack that has already been tested here, and it
   was tested in the most informative way available.** The 2023 pilot ran with a
   minibus, drew +38% evening attendance year-on-year with most new attenders from the
   two estates, then continued unchanged without the minibus and fell back to
   pre-pilot level within six weeks, with the new estate attendees gone
   (`prior-interventions.md` §1). Same building, same programme, same staff, weeks
   apart. That is as close to a controlled comparison as this venue will ever get, and it
   isolates transport rather than programme.
2. **It targets the stated priority neighbourhoods directly**, which the council's
   equalities reporting requires and which no current programme can demonstrate
   (`constraints.md` Reporting; `assessment.md` E20). `updates/U1` sharpens the case:
   among estate households that have heard of the centre, 57% name getting home after an
   evening event as the main barrier, and 48% have never heard of it at all — which is
   why the pilot pairs transport with a targeted, dated offer rather than transport alone.
3. **It is the only proposal that fails informatively.** If attendance does not move
   with transport provided, the transport hypothesis is wrong, and that is worth
   knowing before the centre commits to a recurrent £14,400-a-quarter revenue line.
   A pilot whose failure teaches nothing is a purchase, not a test.

**The counter-argument, stated fairly.** This is the least durable item in the budget.
The 2023 gain did not survive the money stopping. £24,000 buys thirteen weeks of access,
not a permanently accessible venue, and there is a real possibility that the honest
conclusion in week 13 is "this works and we cannot afford it". The pilot is designed to
surface that at week 10, while there is still time to choose a managed ending rather than
repeat 2023's abrupt one. The new 60-attendee cap adds a second limit: even if demand is
there, the building cannot lawfully hold more than 60 a night this quarter, so the pilot
cannot demonstrate the top of the demand curve.

## 2. Logic model

| Inputs | Activities | Outputs | Short-term outcomes (weeks 1–13) | Medium-term (next quarter) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| £9,600 evening opening | Building open 13 Thursday evenings; named responsible person on site; utilities, cleaning | 13 openings, 12–13 programmed sessions, each capped at 60 | Evening attendance ≥ 640 visits (baseline 477), or a documented cap-limited result | Evening offer established as a standing part of the week |
| £14,400 minibus | Loop service Northside–Kiln Field ↔ centre, 19:15 and 21:30 departures, 13 weeks | ~2 trips × 13 weeks; passenger counts per trip | ≥ 35% of evening attenders resident in the two estates | Transport either recurrently funded or a managed alternative in place |
| £4,200 targeted mailer | One wave to Northside and Kiln Field households, **two dated evenings**, booking route, minibus times printed | Household reach; registrations; attendance on the two mailed dates | ≥ 30 attributable registrations by week 4 | Estate households know a specific, dated, reachable offer exists |
| £2,800 training | 2 days, welcome and access awareness, all FOH + volunteers, including cap and turnaway handling | Trained front-of-house team before first evening | Consistent welcome; equalities data captured at the door; no one turned away badly | Practice embedded in induction |
| Existing programme | Evening Talks and Access Cinema re-timed onto Thursdays, finishing by 20:55 | 12–13 sessions | No loss of existing evening audience | — |

**The 60-attendee cap is a constraint on every row of this table, not an input.** It is
not bought; it is complied with. The pilot's success is therefore measured on attendance
and reach **within** the cap, with turnaways recorded as a separate demand signal.

**The causal chain the pilot is testing:** transport removes the 19:10 barrier → estate
residents attend → the offer is specific and dated so they know when → the welcome is
good so they return. Each link has a measurement attached (§6). If attendance rises but
the estate share does not, the chain broke at link two and the volume number is
misleading.

## 3. The offer, and how the timetable and the licence constrain it

The pilot does **not** commission new programming — the quoted menu has no programming
line, and an unquoted item cannot be committed this quarter (`resources.md`). The
evening offer is the centre's existing Evening Talks and Access Cinema cadence
(2 talks + 2 films per month ≈ 12 sessions) re-timed onto Thursday evenings, with the
start time fixed by the minibus timetable.

### 3.1 The timetable tension

The operator quote gives "19:15 and 21:30 departures" without saying from where
(`resources.md`). Two readings are possible and they produce different programmes:

| Reading | Consequence | Programme shape |
|---|---|---|
| 19:15 departs the **estates**, arrives ~19:35 | Event cannot start before 19:40 | Doors 19:20 · start 19:40 · **hard finish 20:55** · return 21:30 |
| 19:15 departs the **centre** (an early return trip) | Inbound arrival is earlier and unconstrained | Doors 18:45 · start 19:00 · **hard finish 20:55** · return 21:30 |

**Resolution.** The operator timetable is confirmed in writing before the contract is
signed (week 0, gate G0). The plan defaults to the **conservative reading**
(19:40 start) because it is the one that still works if the other reading is wrong.
Under that default:

- every evening event finishes by **20:55**, giving 35 minutes' buffer to the 21:30
  return — this is a hard operating rule, not a target;
- film slots are restricted to titles of **70 minutes or less** while the 19:15-inbound
  pattern applies (19:40 start, 20:50 finish). A 110-minute film cannot be shown without
  stranding the audience, and an 80-minute one finishes five minutes past the rule;
- talk slots are 60 minutes plus 15 minutes' questions.

That last point is the kind of detail that decides whether a transport pilot works: a
programme that routinely overruns by twenty minutes delivers people to a bus that has
gone, and they do not come back a second time.

### 3.2 The 60-attendee condition (new in round 2)

The council's building control officer has added a condition: **the accessible entrance
must be operable before any evening event with more than 60 attendees is licensed**,
whether or not the event uses the first floor (`updates/U2`). The entrance is not
operable, and the lift repair is capital at £27,500 with an 8-week lead. Therefore:

- **every pilot evening is capped at 60 attendees** until the entrance is operable;
- the 180-seat hall is no longer the binding capacity — 60 is;
- no event is licensed above 60, and the door stops entry at 60;
- compliance is operational, not a budget line: a pre-booked allocation, a door count,
  a hard stop, and a turnaway log. It costs nothing to comply, and there is no revenue
  line that could lift the cap.

**The cap sits just above the effect the pilot is trying to reproduce.** The 2023 +38%
is about 54.9 visits per session; the cap is 60. The 640 target is 49.2 per opening across
13 openings (82% of the cap) or 53.3 across 12 programmed sessions (89% of the cap). If the
cap binds, the volume test is **censored, not failed**: the report will give attendance
plus recorded turnaways as a demand floor, and will say that the true demand is at
least that figure.

**Protecting the priority audience from the cap.** A cap that turns away the people the
pilot exists to reach would be a perverse result, so the design does three things:

1. the mailer carries **two dated evenings** rather than one, spreading demand;
2. **half of each mailed date's places are held for Northside and Kiln Field bookings
   until 48 hours before the event**, then released;
3. every turnaway is recorded (count, and postcode where voluntarily offered), so the
   report can show whether the cap fell disproportionately on estate residents.

**A second sitting was considered and rejected.** A 19:40 start and a 20:55 finish leave
no room for two sittings, and the single 21:30 return cannot serve both. The only lawful
way to exceed 60 this quarter is the capital repair, which is outside this envelope
(`options.md` §4).

### 3.3 Access rules that follow from `constraints.md`

- All activity on the **ground floor**. The lift is out of service and no first-floor
  public event is permitted (E16). This is a design constraint on the whole quarter,
  not just the pilot.
- **No event admits more than 60** until the accessible entrance is operable (E24).
- No event is scheduled to finish after **20:55**: the car park is unstaffed after 20:00
  and, for anyone not on the minibus, the last scheduled service leaves at 19:10.
- The minibus is the only supported route home for estate residents. Its 21:30
  departure is published on every piece of pilot material, including the mailer.

## 4. Target segments

| Segment | Why them | How they are reached | Baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Northside estate residents** | Named priority neighbourhood; two bus changes from the centre | Minibus + targeted mailer | **Unknown at programme level** — no postcode data. `updates/U1`: 15% of estate households have ever attended, 48% have never heard of the centre |
| **Kiln Field estate residents** | Named priority neighbourhood; same | Minibus + targeted mailer | **Unknown at programme level** — same survey, same limits |
| **Estate households that have never heard of the centre** | 48% of the two estates (`updates/U1`); the largest single segment and the one the 2022 campaign never measured | Targeted mailer with two dated evenings and a booking route | 48% never heard, 37% heard but never attended, 15% attended at least once |
| **Lapsed Evening Talks attendees** | Attended before the Jul–Aug 2024 stoppage and did not return; one is on record as never told it restarted (E14) | Existing contact list where held; programme leaflet at the door | Partly inferable from monthly unique counts (Q1 2024: 52–58; Q1 2025: 58–63) — the audience largely returned, so this segment is smaller than the interview implies |
| **Public-transport-dependent working adults** | The 19:10 last bus excludes anyone whose evening runs past ~18:45 | Minibus; not directly mailed — reached through the programme leaflet and word of mouth | **Unknown — not measured** |

Four of five baselines are blank or household-level only. That is the honest position and
it is why the measurement work described in §6 is sequenced from week 1, before the
pilot's effect can be assessed. The pilot is designed to create its own baseline rather
than wait for one.

**A capacity interaction worth naming.** 35% of 640 is 224 estate visits, or about 17 per
evening across 13 evenings. If the minibus is a single vehicle, its capacity may bind
before the 60-attendee cap does. Passenger counts (M3) will show it; the plan does not
assume a seat count the pack does not give.

## 5. Operating model

**Evening responsible person.** A named individual on site for every opening, per
`constraints.md`. The £9,600 line "assumes existing staff extended, not a new post" —
so this is drawn from the existing 1.5 FTE front-of-house, which is the single largest
delivery risk in the plan (§8, R1). **This is confirmed in week 0, before the minibus
contract is signed.** If it cannot be confirmed, the evening strand is not committed.

**Front of house.** Two people on the door on event evenings where possible: one
welcoming and capturing postcode data, one on the minibus manifest, the door count and
returns. With 1.5 FTE this is only achievable with volunteer support; the two training
days are timed to fall before the first evening so volunteers are briefed, not improvised.

**The capped door.** The responsible person holds the count. At 55 on the door, staff
prepare to close entry; at 60, entry stops. Pre-booked guests are admitted first; the
estate reservation is honoured until 48 hours before, then released. Turnaways are logged
with a tally counter and, where offered, a postcode. No one is turned away without being
told the next date and how to book it.

**Minibus.** Operator-run under the quoted contract. Centre responsibilities: publish
times, hold a manifest, ensure the 20:55 finish, and count passengers boarding at each
end. A named centre contact is on call for every trip.

**Partner (Youth Studio).** Runs in parallel, not inside the pilot: **up to 4 additional
sessions per month** on top of the existing 2, taking the Studio from 2 to 6 sessions a
month and from 6 to 18 sessions in the quarter. The partner has confirmed in writing that
it can staff this block at the same £6,500 and will use the 40-name waiting list first
(`updates/U3`). The centre supplies room and materials. The target is **1,000 visits**
across the 18 sessions (M4). **The room's location is confirmed in week 0**: no
first-floor public event is permitted while the lift is out, so a first-floor Studio would
stop the strand, not merely cap it.

**Data.** Postcode captured at every event from week 1, by the door volunteer, with a
one-question form (postcode + first visit y/n + how heard). No personal data beyond
that; aggregated to estate level for reporting. Turnaways are counted separately.

## 6. Evaluation design

**Question.** Does providing transport and evening opening increase evening attendance
*and* the share of attenders from the two priority estates — and does the increase hold
across the quarter, within the 60-attendee cap?

**Design.** Difference-in-differences against two comparators:

1. **Same quarter last year** (Q2 2024: 477 evening visits over 12 sessions) — controls
   for the programme and the season.
2. **Non-transport programmes in the same quarter** (Community Choir, Family
   Saturdays) — controls for borough-wide or weather effects that would lift everything.
   If the Choir and Family Saturdays are flat while evenings rise, the rise is
   attributable to the pilot rather than to a good spring.

This is not a randomised trial and the report will not call it one. It is stronger than
a before-and-after because it has a within-quarter control group, and it is honest about
the residual confound: the pilot also changes *when* events happen, not only how people
reach them. The 2023 comparison was uncapped; this quarter's is capped at 60, so if the
cap binds, the like-for-like volume comparison is biased **against** the pilot, and the
report must say so.

**Pre-registered success thresholds** (fixed now, before any data arrives, so the
result cannot be interpreted after the fact):

| Test | Threshold | Reading if met | Reading if missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volume | ≥ 640 evening visits (baseline 477, +34%), or attendance plus recorded turnaways ≥ 640 | Transport hypothesis holds | Mechanism failed, or the cap censored the result — distinguish using the turnaway count |
| Reach | ≥ 35% of evening attenders from the two estates | The gain is going to the priority neighbourhoods | **Volume without reach is a failure of purpose**, even if the volume target is met |
| Durability | Weeks 9–13 mean attendance ≥ weeks 1–4 mean | Not a novelty spike | A spike that decayed — the 2023 pattern recurring inside the pilot |
| Control | Choir and Family Saturdays within ±10% of Q2 2024 | No borough-wide confound | Result is not attributable to the pilot |
| Cap compliance | **0 events counted above 60** | Lawful operation | A licensing incident, reported as such — not a metric miss to be explained away |

**The cap arithmetic, stated in advance.** 640 visits is 49.2 per opening across 13
openings (82% of the 60 cap), or 53.3 across 12 programmed sessions (89% of the cap). The
2023 effect of +38% would be about 54.9 per session, or 91.4% of the cap. The target is
therefore feasible but close to the ceiling; a shortfall of a few dozen visits could be a
cap artefact rather than a transport failure. The turnaway log is what separates the two,
and it is why turnaways are a primary measure (M13), not an anecdote.

**The falsification that matters most.** If evening attendance rises 40% but the estate
share stays near zero, the pilot has increased attendance among people who were already
coming by car, and it has not improved access at all. That outcome is recorded as a
failure in the report, not spun as a success on volume. This is written down here, in
advance, precisely because it will be tempting to spin later.

**And the honest limit of the design.** Thirteen weeks cannot tell the board whether the
effect is durable beyond a quarter. The 2023 evidence suggests it is not durable without
the transport. The week-13 report will say so. Nor can a capped quarter tell the board how
much demand exists above 60; it can only say that demand was at least what was admitted
plus what was turned away.

### Stop rules (S1–S7)

Agreed in advance so that no one has to invent a decision under pressure. S1–S5 and S7
apply to the pilot; S6 applies to the Youth Studio strand.

| ID | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | No named responsible person available for an evening opening | **That evening is cancelled, not run.** Two consecutive failures suspend the evening strand; referred to the board within 5 working days |
| **S2** | Any safety incident at an evening event or on the minibus | Suspend the affected strand immediately, pending review. No exceptions |
| **S3** | Mean fewer than **6 passengers per return trip** over 4 consecutive weeks | Renegotiate route and times with the operator **before** continuing. If no improvement by week 10, recommend not renewing |
| **S4** | Estate share **below 15% by week 8** (under half the linear path to the 35% target) | The mechanism is not working. Stop the mailer; refer the evening strand to the board at G2 rather than waiting for week 13 |
| **S5** | Fewer than **30 attributable registrations by the end of week 4** | Do not repeat the mailer. No second wave |
| **S6** | Partner delivers fewer than the confirmed block of 4 extra sessions a month | Run the sessions that can be staffed and report the shortfall. The £6,500 is a **block price**, not a per-session rate; if the block cannot run at all, return it and report underspend |
| **S7** | **Three or more evenings at 60 with recorded turnaways before week 8** | Report to the board at G2 with the turnaway evidence; recommend instructing the capital bid. Do **not** raise the cap and do **not** add a second mailer wave |
| **C1** | Any event counted above 60 | Licensing incident: entry stops immediately, the responsible person records it, and it is reported to the board and the council within 5 working days; the board reviews whether to suspend the strand, and a second breach suspends it automatically. It is not a target to be missed; it is a breach |

## 7. Risks

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **The 1.5 FTE front-of-house cannot cover 13 evenings on top of weekday duties** — the £9,600 assumes extended existing staff, and the rota does not currently staff evenings at all | High | High — no responsible person, no event | Confirm named responsible person and full rota in week 0, before signing the bus; training days scheduled first; hard stop rule S1 | **Not fully mitigable within the envelope.** If cover fails, the correct response is to cancel the strand, not to run it thin |
| R2 | Partner cannot deliver the confirmed block, or the Studio room cannot absorb 6 sessions a month | Medium | Medium — £6,500 delivers less than planned | Written confirmation in week 0 (already held, `updates/U3`); room capacity checked before week 1; S6 | Medium |
| R3 | Minibus runs under-used | Medium | Medium — £14,400 for few passengers | Week-4 and week-8 utilisation checks; renegotiate route/times with operator (no extra cost assumed); stop rule S3 | Medium |
| R4 | Events overrun and strand the audience | Medium | High — destroys trust with exactly the new attenders being courted | Hard 20:55 finish; 70-minute film cap; front-of-house empowered to end on time | Low |
| R5 | Low postcode capture at a busy door | High | Medium — the reach claim becomes unevidenced | Dedicated door role, one-question form, weekly capture-rate reporting against an 80% target | Low |
| R6 | Unstaffed car park after 20:00 deters car-driving attenders | Medium | Low–Medium | Outside the envelope; communicated in advance; minibus prioritised | **Unmitigated — recorded as a standing risk** |
| R7 | Abrupt stop at week 13 repeats 2023 and loses the new audience | High if unfunded | High | Week-10 gate decides between recurrent funding and a *managed* ending with an announced replacement offer | Medium |
| R8 | Safeguarding on the minibus (unaccompanied young people, late returns) | Low–Medium | High | Named contact on call; manifest; existing partner safeguarding policy applies to Studio attendees | Low–Medium |
| R9 | **The 60 cap turns away estate residents** after they have made the journey | Medium | High — the pilot would exclude the people it exists to reach | Two dated offers; half of each mailed date's places held for estate bookings until 48 hours before; turnaway log; S7 | Medium — the cap cannot be lifted this quarter |
| R10 | **The cap censors the volume result**, making a successful pilot look weak | High if demand is strong | Medium — the board could misread a cap artefact as failure | Pre-registered reading rule (attendance + turnaways); M13; report the cap explicitly | Low — the rule is agreed before data arrives |

## 9. What this pilot deliberately does not test

Recorded so that absence is not later mistaken for evidence:

- whether **new** evening programming (as opposed to the existing cadence) would draw
  better — no programming line is quoted, so it cannot be bought this quarter;
- whether the lift repair would change attendance — deferred, capital at £27,500 with an
  8-week lead (`options.md` §4);
- **whether demand exists above 60** — the licence caps every evening at 60, so the quarter
  cannot observe it; turnaways are the only estimate;
- whether **daytime** access is a barrier for the estates — the minibus is an evening
  service only; a daytime shuttle is not quoted;
- whether transport would work for **Family Saturdays** — those already draw 110–120
  per session and are not access-constrained in the same way;
- whether a **second evening a week** would work — the £9,600 buys one opening a week, and
  a second would need a new quote.
