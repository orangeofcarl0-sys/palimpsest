# Budget

**Envelope: £48,000 for the quarter**, committed at the start, no carry-over
(`resources.md`). **Committed: £37,500.** **Uncommitted: £10,500**, which lapses.

Every line below is a quoted cost from `sources/resources.md`, as updated by
`updates/U3` (the same £6,500 now buys up to four additional Youth Studio sessions a
month rather than two). Nothing is estimated, extrapolated or invented; where the pack has
no price, the item is not in the budget and is named in §4 instead.

**Arithmetic check:** 9,600 + 14,400 + 6,500 + 2,800 + 4,200 = **37,500**. Under the
envelope by 10,500. No line exceeds its quote; no line is a proportion of a quote. The
Youth Studio line is the same £6,500 block price as round 1 and now buys **twelve**
additional sessions instead of six, because the partner's written clarification offers up
to four a month at that price (`updates/U3`). That is a better use of the same quoted
line, not a new cost.

**Lines 1 and 2 are one decision.** They are costed separately because the supplier
costs are separate, but they are approved together or not at all — see `options.md` §3.
Committing £9,600 without £14,400 buys the six weeks of the 2023 pilot that did not work.

**The 60-attendee cap has no budget line.** It is a licensing condition, not a purchase
(`updates/U2`). Compliance is operational — pre-booking, a door count, a hard stop at 60
— and there is no quoted item that could lift it. The only route above 60 is the £27,500
capital repair, which is outside this envelope (§4).

## 3. Cost-effectiveness

Comparing cost per additional visit, on the targets set in `metrics.md`:

| Intervention | Cost | Target additional visits | Cost per additional visit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Youth Studio extra sessions | £6,500 | +476 (524 → 1,000) | **£14** |
| Evening access (opening + bus) | £24,000 | +163 (477 → 640) | **£147** |
| Blended, all committed spend | £37,500 | +639 | **£59** |
| *2022 borough print (for contrast)* | *£9,000* | *no detectable change* | *not computable — the denominator was zero* |

**The Youth Studio is now roughly ten times better value per visit than the evening
pilot.** If the only objective were volume, the plan would put everything into the Studio.
It does not, for two reasons: the partner can supply at most four extra sessions a month
and the room may bind before that (UNC-15), and volume is not the only objective — the
evening pilot is the only instrument that reaches the priority estates by construction,
because the minibus goes to them and nowhere else.

**The Studio figure improved because the same money now buys twice the block, not because
the arithmetic was flattered.** Round 1 bought 6 extra sessions for £6,500 and set a
target of 780 visits (£25 per additional visit). The partner's clarification offers 12
extra sessions for the same £6,500. The target is **not** doubled, because the marginal
sessions are harder to fill: the first six draw on a 40-name waiting list, the second six
have to fill from the same base. 1,000 visits is 55.6 per session, a 25% discount to the
current 74 per session. That is the honest assumption, and it still produces £14 per
additional visit.

**£147 per additional evening visit is expensive, and the report will say so.** It is
unchanged from round 1: the cost and the target are unchanged, so the ratio is unchanged.
The defence is not that the number is good; it is that the number is the wrong unit. The
right unit is cost per additional *priority-neighbourhood* attender, and that cannot be
computed until the postcode baseline exists — which is week 6. If, at week 10, the estate
share is on target and the volume is on target, £24,000 for ~160 additional visits, with
at least 35% of the audience from the two estates, is defensible. If the estate share is
not on target, the same £24,000 is buying attendance from people who were already coming
by car, and the pilot has failed on its own terms regardless of the volume figure.

**The 60 cap limits how much the evening ratio can improve.** A capped event cannot
convert excess demand into visits, so a successful pilot may record 640 visits plus
turnaways and still show £147 per counted visit. If every one of the 13 openings fills to
60, attendance is 780 and the ratio falls to about £79 at best — but it cannot go below
that this quarter. That is why the report gives cost per counted visit and the turnaway
count side by side, and why it does not use the evening ratio alone to judge the pilot.

**No comparison to existing programme cost per visit is possible.** The pack contains no
programme cost data, so the £14 and £147 figures cannot be benchmarked against what the
Choir or Family Saturdays cost to run. That is a real gap and it is recorded in
`uncertainties.md` UNC-11 rather than filled with an estimate.

## 5. Stress test: the indicated 15% reduction

The council has *verbally indicated* a reduction of up to 15% next quarter, with
"nothing confirmed and no figure agreed" (`resources.md`). It is therefore treated as a
stress scenario, not a budget.

**A 15% reduction takes the envelope to £40,800.** The committed plan is £37,500 and
**fits with £3,300 to spare. No strand needs to be cancelled.** This is a deliberate
design property: the plan was sized so that the indicated cut does not force a choice
between access and volume. The Youth Studio block doubling does not change this, because
it costs the same £6,500.

**If the cut is deeper than 15%, the cut ladder is fixed in advance** — weakest evidence
first, so the decision is not made under pressure:

| Step | Action | Saving | Running total | What is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stop the targeted mailer | £4,200 | £33,300 | Demand-side support; the offer still exists but is less known |
| 2 | Stop the training days | £2,800 | £30,500 | Welcome quality for new attenders, including capped-door handling; no measured attendance effect |
| 3 | Stop the extra Youth Studio sessions | £6,500 | £24,000 | 476 visits — the best value in the plan, cut last of the discretionary items |
| 4 | Stop the evening strand **as a unit** | £24,000 | £0 | Everything the pilot was for |

**Step 4 is never taken halfway.** Cutting the bus but keeping the opening is the 2023
failure mode; cutting the opening but keeping the bus is a bus to a closed door. If the
board cannot fund both, it must fund neither and say so.

## 6. Commitment, variation and reporting rules

- **All five lines are committed in week 0**, before the quarter starts, per
  `constraints.md`. Nothing is held back as in-quarter contingency.
- **G0 pre-condition.** Lines 1 and 2 are signed only after the responsible person and
  the evening rota are confirmed. If they cannot be, the fallback package is committed
  instead (lines 3 and 4 = £9,300; £2,800 if the Studio room is not ground floor) and
  the balance is returned. The mailer is **not** part of the fallback: it is built around
  two dated pilot evenings, and without the pilot there is no offer for it to carry.
  Committing £24,000 to an evening programme with no lawful responsible person would be
  committing to something that cannot legally run.
- **Cap compliance is a condition of the evening line, not an extra.** No event above 60
  is licensed until the accessible entrance is operable (`updates/U2`). If the door cannot
  be controlled, the evening does not run; the money is not reallocated to a larger event.
- **No virement between capital and revenue** without a council variation
  (`resources.md`). No capital is in this budget, so the constraint does not bite this
  quarter — which is part of why the lift is deferred.
- **Any new item needs a written quote** and cannot be added mid-quarter. The £10,500
  cannot be quietly reallocated to an unquoted idea at week 8.
- **Any change greater than 10% of a line** requires a board resolution and is recorded
  in the quarterly report.
- **Underspend is reported as underspend.** If the partner cannot run the confirmed
  Studio block, the £6,500 is returned and reported, not absorbed into other lines.
