# Options appraisal

Eight quoted items (seven still available), one £48,000 envelope, one 13-week quarter,
and — new in round 2 — a hard licensing cap of 60 attendees on every evening event until
the accessible entrance is operable. This file builds the option set, tests each against
the evidence in `assessment.md`, and shows why the chosen package is the only one that is
simultaneously **lawful**, **evidenced**, **feasible** and **internally coherent**. The
three traps it avoids are the ones the pack is built around: spending on awareness because
awareness is easy to buy, spending on capital because accessibility is in the goal
statement, and spending on a bigger evening programme than the building can lawfully hold.

---

## 1. Appraisal criteria

| Criterion | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **E0 Lawful operation** | Pass/fail | Can the option run under the current licence? Since `updates/U2`, no evening event above 60 attendees may be licensed until the accessible entrance is operable. An option that assumes a larger evening audience is not a worse option; it is not available. |
| **E1 Evidential support** | High | Does the pack contain a test, a mechanism, or a quantified signal that this will work here? |
| **E2 Reach to Northside / Kiln Field** | High | The stated priority neighbourhoods and the council's equalities duty. |
| **E3 Affordability & headroom** | High | Must fit £48,000 with no carry-over, from the quoted menu only. |
| **E4 Deliverability in 13 weeks** | High | Rota, lead times, partner capacity, licensing. |
| **E5 Reversibility** | Medium | The council has signalled a possible 15% cut; stoppable strands are worth more than indivisible ones. |
| **E6 Durability of the gain** | Medium | Does the benefit survive the money stopping? |

## 3. The two coupled pairs

Two of the menu items are worthless — worse than worthless — on their own.

**Evening opening without transport (O2).** This is not a hypothetical failure. It is
the 2023 pilot's second act: the programme continued unchanged for six weeks after the
minibus stopped, attendance returned to pre-pilot level, and the new estate attendees
stopped coming (`prior-interventions.md` §1). Buying £9,600 of evening opening without
£14,400 of transport is buying the six weeks that did not work. The programme was never
the active ingredient. `updates/U1` reinforces the point: 57% of estate households that
have heard of the centre name getting home after an evening event as the main barrier.

**Transport without evening opening (O3).** A minibus running a 19:15 and a 21:30
departure to a building whose evening rota is unstaffed delivers people to a closed
door. It is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one.

**Therefore evening opening and the late minibus are a single £24,000 decision.**
They are costed separately and must be approved together or not at all. This coupling
is the single most important structural fact in the budget.

**The new cap makes the pair more fragile, not less.** The £24,000 buys thirteen evenings
of at most 60 people each, not thirteen evenings of 180. That is still the only instrument
in the menu that reaches the two estates by construction. But it means the pair cannot
grow its way to a better cost per visit this quarter: volume is capped by licence, and the
only lawful route above 60 is the capital repair, which is outside this envelope. The
evening pilot is therefore justified on **reach**, not on cost per visit, and the report
must say so.

The Youth Studio is a weaker version of the same logic: extra sessions need partner
staff, and the partner's own written clarification says its funding cut is what reduced
frequency (`updates/U3`). The £6,500 buys a block of sessions, not unlimited capacity. The
partner has confirmed it can staff up to four extra sessions a month; that confirmation is
now in hand, so the delivery question has moved from "will they?" to "will the room and
the waitlist support a tripled cadence?" (UNC-4, UNC-15).

## 4. Why the lift is deferred rather than funded

The lift is the most emotionally compelling line on the menu and the worst value in a
13-week participation envelope. The reasoning, stated plainly so the board can overturn
it if it disagrees:

1. **It is capital, and it cannot be phased** (`resources.md`). At **£27,500** it is 57%
   of the entire revenue envelope in one indivisible commitment — and capital and revenue
   cannot be swapped without a council variation.
2. **It has an 8-week lead time** (`updates/U2`). Instructed in week 0, it completes
   around week 8 — leaving five weeks of the quarter in which the entrance is operable.
   Instructed in week 1 of next quarter, it completes around week 9 of that quarter.
3. **There is no costed first-floor programme to put in those weeks.** The quoted menu
   contains no programming line for it. So the lift delivers access to a floor with nothing
   scheduled on it inside this quarter. Its first-floor participation return in-quarter is
   close to zero; its cap-lifting return is real but limited to the weeks after completion,
   and its larger return falls in the *next* financial year.
4. **It is unaffordable in any coherent package.** Lift + evening + bus is **£51,500**,
   £3,500 over the envelope, and that package has no Studio, no training and no mailer.
   Lift + evening + bus + Studio is £58,000, £10,000 over. Round 1 could at least describe
   a lift-plus-evening-plus-bus package that fitted by deleting the Studio; the re-quote
   removes that option. Including the lift now means dropping the bus, which is the 2023
   failure mode, or dropping the evening programme, which leaves the lift with nothing to
   open on to.
5. **The new condition changes the case in both directions.** It makes the lift more
   important, because it now caps *every* evening event at 60, not only first-floor use.
   It also makes the lift more expensive and slower. The correct response is not to squeeze
   it into the revenue plan; it is to fund it as capital and to stop pretending the
   revenue plan can solve it.

**This is a deferral, not a dismissal, and it carries a real cost that must be
recorded.** The accessible entrance is out of service *now*. Every quarter it stays
broken is a quarter in which disabled visitors are excluded and the centre cannot even
count how many, and every evening event is capped at 60. The mitigation available within
this envelope is to keep **all** programming on the ground floor (which the pilot does),
to hold every evening at or below 60 (which the pilot does), and to record the exclusion
and the cap as standing equalities and capacity findings in the council report rather than
let them pass unremarked.

**The recommendation is a separate capital bid at £27,500**, approved by the board and
instructed as early as the capital process allows — not a reallocation from this plan.
Approved and instructed in week 0, the 8-week lead completes around week 8, so weeks 9–13
could license events above 60. Approved later, it lands in the following quarter. Either
way it is outside the £48,000 and must not be netted against it. `uncertainties.md` UNC-8
carries the position if the board overrules this.

## 5. Why print is excluded and the mailer is only conditionally included

The 2022 campaign is the only clean null result in the pack: £9,000 of borough print
plus a mail-drop, attendance within normal seasonal variation, word of mouth still
dominant at 64%, and no measurable change in first-time attendance from the priority
estates (`prior-interventions.md` §2). The board's minute — *"do not repeat the poster
spend without a specific audience and a specific offer"* — is a governance constraint,
not a suggestion. O8 fails it on both counts: "across the borough" is not a specific
audience, and a generic poster has no specific offer.

**`updates/U1` changes the mailer's standing, not the print verdict.** Round 1 treated the
mailer as the weakest line in the budget because the 2022 package had included a mail-drop
and still produced nothing. `updates/U1` shows why that analogy is incomplete: 48% of estate
households have never heard of the centre, 37% have heard but never attended, and only 15%
have attended at least once. There is a measured awareness gap in the specific audience the
mailer reaches. That does not prove a mailer will close it — the equalities team explicitly
says it has not tested whether awareness converts — but it gives the line a specific
audience and a measured gap, which is more than round 1 could say.

The **targeted mailer (£4,200)** is therefore included as the demand-side match to a
supply-side fix that is already evidenced, on probation:
- it goes only to Northside and Kiln Field households (specific audience);
- it carries **two dated evenings** and a booking route, with the return minibus times
  printed on it (specific offer), because a single date under a 60-attendee cap risks
  turning away the estate residents the pilot exists to reach;
- it carries a pre-registered stop rule at week 4 (S5: fewer than 30 attributable
  registrations means no repeat).

**A second wave is affordable and deliberately not committed.** Another £4,200 would fit
inside the £10,500 headroom (O13, £41,700), and 48% unawareness is a real argument for it.
It is not committed for two reasons: the quarter's budget must be committed at the start,
so a second wave would have to be bought before the first wave's result exists; and the
2022 mail-drop is a direct warning that sending more paper is not the same as converting.
If wave one clears 30 registrations, a second wave is the first call on next quarter and
needs a fresh quote. If it does not, the headroom lapses and the board has learned
something for £4,200 rather than £8,400.

## 6. The chosen package

**O10 — Evening Access Pilot, with three supporting strands.**

| Item | Cost | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Evening opening, 13 weeks | £9,600 | Supply: the building is open and legally staffed, capped at 60 a night |
| Late bus subsidy, 13 weeks | £14,400 | Access: the binding constraint, per the natural experiment |
| Additional Youth Studio sessions (up to 4/month, 12 for the quarter) | £6,500 | Volume: relieves a measured capacity constraint with a 40-name waitlist |
| Staff training: welcome and access awareness | £2,800 | Capability: the welcome new attenders receive, including how to run a capped door kindly |
| Targeted mailer to priority-neighbourhood households | £4,200 | Demand: specific audience, two specific dates, probationary |
| **Total** | **£37,500** | **£10,500 under the £48,000 envelope** |

**Why this is the coherent plan and not a list.** It attacks one access failure per
strand, each with a different mechanism, and it never funds a demand-side instrument
(mailer) without the supply-side condition that makes the demand actionable (transport +
opening). It touches the constraints in `assessment.md` §3 that money can reach:
timing/transport, session frequency, and welcome. It complies with the constraint money
cannot reach this quarter — the 60-attendee cap — by design rather than by hoping. It
leaves the two constraints money cannot reach at all — the unstaffed evening rota and the
partner's room capacity — as explicitly managed risks rather than assumed away.

**Why £10,500 is left unallocated.** Not modesty, and not slack. The available menu
contains no further item the evidence supports: the only remaining menu items are print
(contraindicated, O8) and the café (no longer available, O9). The lift is capital and
unaffordable in any coherent revenue package. A second mailer wave is the one credible
use of headroom and is deliberately deferred to the week-4 result (O13). Spending the
remainder on print or on a pre-emptive second wave would be spending to fill a number.

The headroom also does real work: at £37,500 the plan already fits inside a 15%-reduced
envelope of £40,800, so a cut of the indicated size requires **no strand to be cancelled**.
That is a deliberate design property, not an accident — see `budget.md` §5.
