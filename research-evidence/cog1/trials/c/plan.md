# Quarterly plan — Riversdale Community Arts Centre

- **Quarter:** 13 weeks from the first week of April (Q2 2025).
- **Resource envelope:** £48,000, committed at the start, no carry-over.
- **Committed:** £37,500.
- **Goal:** improve participation and service accessibility within the fixed envelope, the
  current licensing constraints and the current quoted menu.

---

## 1. The plan on one page

| | |
|---|---|
| **What we are doing** | An Evening Access Pilot (Thursday opening + Northside–Kiln Field minibus, 13 weeks, every evening capped at 60) as the centrepiece, with **twelve** additional Youth Studio sessions (up to four a month), two days of welcome and access training, and one targeted mailer to the two priority estates carrying two dated evenings. |
| **Why** | The pack contains an accidental natural experiment showing that transport — not programme — is what gates evening attendance; a written partner clarification showing that the Youth Studio decline is a supply cut, not lost interest; and a new estates survey showing that awareness is uneven (48% have never heard of the centre) while transport remains the dominant barrier among those who have. |
| **What it costs** | £37,500 of £48,000. £10,500 uncommitted and lapsed. |
| **What success looks like** | ≥640 evening visits (baseline 477), or attendance plus recorded turnaways ≥640, with ≥35% from the two estates; ≥1,000 Youth Studio visits (baseline 524) and ≥140 monthly unique participants; zero evening events above 60; a postcode baseline that exists for the first time. |
| **What we are deliberately not doing** | Borough-wide print (tested and failed in 2022, banned by board minute); lift repair (capital, now £27,500 with an 8-week lead, and unaffordable in any coherent revenue package); café equipment (no longer available this quarter); any new post or unquoted item. |
| **The decision the board must take** | By week 10: does transport become a recurrent revenue commitment, or does the centre plan a managed ending? Separately, the £27,500 capital bid: approve it at G0 if the board wants the cap lifted in weeks 9–13, or by week 10 for the following quarter. |

**The objectives are deliberately few.** Every one of them is a response to something
the pack evidences. There is no objective for "raise satisfaction", because satisfaction
is already 92% among attenders and the pack contains no evidence of a problem there —
only evidence that the measurement of it is unrepresentative (`assessment.md` E1–E4).

## 3. What the plan is built on

Five findings, each with a different consequence:

1. **Transport gates evening attendance** (`prior-interventions.md` §1, triangulated by
   `constraints.md`, `survey.md` and now `updates/U1`: 57% of aware estate households name
   getting home as the main barrier). Consequence: opening and transport are funded and
   approved as one unit, never separately.
2. **The Youth Studio is capacity-constrained, not demand-constrained**
   (`attendance.csv`: visits/session +33%, unique participants +62% on half the sessions;
   confirmed in writing by the partner in `updates/U3`, with a 40-name waiting list).
   Consequence: buy sessions, not marketing — and buy the full block the same £6,500 now
   offers.
3. **Awareness is uneven, not absent** (`updates/U1`: 48% of estate households have never
   heard of the centre; 37% have heard but never attended). The 2022 result still rules out
   borough-wide print, because it had no specific audience or offer; it does not rule out a
   targeted mailer to a named audience with dated offers. Consequence: one targeted wave,
   two dates, a stop rule — not another poster campaign.
4. **Reach is unmeasured** (`attendance.csv` has no equalities field). Consequence:
   measurement is a workstream with a budget-adjacent cost in staff time, sequenced
   before the pilot's effect can be judged.
5. **Physical access now gates scale, not only first-floor use** (`updates/U2`: no evening
   event above 60 until the accessible entrance is operable; the lift is £27,500 with an
   8-week lead). Consequence: the pilot runs at 60 a night, records turnaways, and the lift
   becomes a separate capital bid rather than a revenue squeeze.

## 4. Workstreams

### WS1 — Evening Access Pilot (mobilisation week 0; delivery weeks 1–13) · £24,000
Thirteen Thursday openings with a named responsible person, hosting the existing
Evening Talks and Access Cinema cadence re-timed to finish by 20:55, with the
Northside–Kiln Field minibus running on the same evenings (19:15 and 21:30 departures).
**Every evening is capped at 60 attendees** until the accessible entrance is operable;
entry stops at 60 and turnaways are logged. Full design, logic model, evaluation and stop
rules: `pilot.md`.

### WS2 — Youth Studio additional sessions (weeks 2–13) · £6,500
**Twelve** additional sessions (up to 4 per month) on top of the existing cadence, taking
the Studio from 2 to 6 sessions a month. The partner has confirmed in writing that it can
staff this block at the same £6,500 and will use the 40-name waiting list first
(`updates/U3`). The centre supplies room and materials. If the partner cannot run the
block, the sessions do not run and the money is returned — it is not absorbed elsewhere.

### WS3 — Measurement and equalities (weeks 1–13) · staff time, no incremental cost
Postcode and first-visit capture at every event, a door count and turnaway log at every
evening, weekly dashboard, week-6 interim equalities report, week-13 quarterly report.
This is the workstream that makes every other claim in the plan checkable. Full
definitions: `metrics.md`.

### WS4 — Welcome and access training (week 1) · £2,800
Two days for all front-of-house staff and volunteers, completed before the first
Thursday opening. Covers welcome, access awareness, the hard 20:55 finish, how to capture
postcode data without making it feel like surveillance, and how to run a capped door and
turn people away kindly with the next date.

### WS5 — Targeted demand (weeks 2–4) · £4,200
One mailer wave to Northside and Kiln Field households carrying **two dated evenings**,
the return minibus times, the 60-seat limit and a booking route. Half of each mailed
date's places are held for estate bookings until 48 hours before. No second wave without a
week-4 result.

### WS6 — Governance and reporting (weeks 0, 4, 6, 8, 10, 13) · no cost
Gates, council reporting, the cap review and the week-10 funding decision.

### WS7 — Capital case for the lift (weeks 0–10) · no cost, outside the envelope
A separate bid for **£27,500** to repair the lift and reinstate the accessible entrance,
with an **8-week** lead time. Approved and instructed early, it completes around week 8
and would allow events above 60 in weeks 9–13; approved later, it lands next quarter. It
is not funded from the £48,000 and must not be netted against it. Full reasoning:
`options.md` §4.

## 8. Constraints carried into every activity

These are not preferences; they come from `constraints.md` and `updates/U2` and apply to
all workstreams:

- **Ground floor only.** The accessible entrance and lift are out of service; no public
  event may use the first floor. Nothing in this plan does, subject to the week-0
  confirmation that the Youth Studio room is on the ground floor (D2).
- **60-attendee cap on every evening event** until the accessible entrance is operable,
  whether or not the event uses the first floor. Entry stops at 60; turnaways are logged;
  no event is licensed above 60. A breach is a licensing incident, not a metric miss.
- **Hard 20:55 finish** for every evening event. The 21:30 minibus is the only supported
  route home for estate residents; an overrun strands them.
- **No staffed car park after 20:00.** Attendees arriving by car are told in advance.
  This is a residual risk the envelope cannot fix.
- **Named responsible person on site** for every evening opening, without exception.
- **Main hall capacity 180 seated** — no longer the binding capacity; the 60-attendee
  licence binds first. The 180 figure is relevant only to the capital case.
- **One quarterly report to the council** with attendance and equalities monitoring.

## 9. What the plan will not do

- It will not run a borough-wide print or poster campaign. That was tested in 2022 and
  produced no detectable effect; the board has minuted the condition for revisiting it, and
  this plan does not meet that condition. `updates/U1` shows awareness is uneven, but
  "across the borough" still fails the specific-audience test; the targeted mailer is the
  compliant response.
- It will not fund the lift repair from the programme envelope. It is capital, now
  £27,500 with an 8-week lead, cannot be phased, and has no costed first-floor programme
  to open on to inside the quarter; its only in-quarter benefit would be lifting the 60
  cap after completion. Lift + evening + bus alone is £51,500, £3,500 over, so it cannot
  be squeezed in by trimming; it is recommended as a separate capital bid
  (`options.md` §4).
- It will not create a new post, commission new programming, subsidise tickets, or buy
  anything not on the quoted menu — those need written quotes and so cannot be part of this
  quarter.
- It will not claim that 92% satisfaction means audience development is unnecessary, nor
  that it is a problem. It is a well-measured fact about one audience on one weekend.
- It will not present the £10,500 headroom as a reserve. It lapses at quarter end.

## 10. End of quarter: exit and handover

The 2023 pilot ended because funding ran out mid-programme, and the audience was lost
rather than retained at a lower level. **The single most important design choice in this
plan is that it does not repeat that.** By week 13 the board will hold:

1. **A result against pre-registered thresholds** — volume, reach, durability, control,
   cap compliance — reported as met or not met, without reframing (`metrics.md` §4). If
   the cap bound, the volume result is reported with the turnaway count as a demand floor.
2. **A recommendation on transport**: recurrent revenue commitment, or a managed ending
   announced at the week-10 decision — three weeks before the final opening, so the
   audience is told rather than discovering it, which is what happened in 2023 and in
   the summer 2024 stoppage — with a replacement offer (the zero-cost fallback is to
   move the evening start earlier so the 19:10 last bus becomes usable; it recovers
   part of the access gain and costs nothing).
3. **A capital bid for the lift at £27,500 with an 8-week lead**, instructed as early as
   the capital process allows, so the work does not consume a programme quarter and the
   60-attendee cap can eventually be lifted.
4. **Written quotes for the four things this plan needed and could not buy**: a second
   Youth Studio block, a daytime or weekend shuttle, an evening duty officer post, and a
   ticket or fare subsidy for estate residents (the barrier 22% of aware households name).
   Commissioning those quotes now is what stops next quarter being constrained to the
   same menu.
5. **A first postcode baseline**, which is the only durable asset the quarter creates: it
   survives the money and makes every future reach claim checkable.

**The honest summary for the board.** This quarter buys thirteen weeks of access — capped
at 60 a night — and a measurement capability the centre has never had. It does not buy a
permanently accessible venue, and the evidence says the evening gain will not outlive the
transport subsidy unless the board chooses to make that subsidy recurrent. It also does not
buy the right to fill the hall: the licence now ties any audience above 60 to a £27,500
capital repair. Week 10 is where the transport choice has to be made; the capital bid is
where the scale choice has to be made.
