# Metrics and evaluation framework (round 2)

## 1. Principles

- **Measure the plan that is funded.** The core metrics cover the transport-linked evening pilot, the Youth Studio expansion, the targeted mailer, the access training, the 60-attendee licensing cap, and the equalities reporting requirement. They do not invent outcomes for activities that are not in the budget.
- **Use the baseline that exists, and say when one does not.** The pack has monthly programme attendance but no neighbourhood, disability, or equalities breakdown. The new doorstep survey gives an estate-level awareness and barrier baseline, but not centre attendance. "Not measured" is a valid baseline; a fabricated number is not.
- **Report counts and denominators, not just percentages.** A 50% estate share means something different at 20 attendees and at 200.
- **Do not overclaim causality.** This is a single-site, 13-week, self-selected pilot with no control group. The evaluation can describe change and test whether the pre-agreed thresholds were met; it cannot prove that the pilot alone caused the change.
- **Report the 60 cap.** The licensing condition limits evening events to 60 attendees until the accessible entrance is operable. Attendance is therefore partly supply-constrained. Cap hits and turn-aways must be reported alongside attendance, or a capped event will look like a demand ceiling when it is a licensing ceiling.
- **Protect personal data.** Postcode/estate and access needs are personal data. Collect the minimum, explain the purpose, store securely, and suppress small cells (fewer than five) in reporting.

The plan commits **£37,500** across five revenue lines (evening opening, late bus, Youth Studio expansion, targeted mailer, and training) against the **£48,000** envelope, leaving **£10,500** uncommitted; the metrics below cover each line, plus the 60-attendee access condition.

## 2. Core metrics

### M1. Evening attendance per session

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Definition | Total visits to pilot evening events divided by the number of pilot evening sessions. Exclude staff, volunteers, and contractors. Report door count and registration count separately if they differ. Report cap hits and turn-aways separately. |
| Baseline | **36.5 visits per session** for Evening Talks in April–June 2024 (219 visits over 6 sessions). The current 2025 Q1 figure is **46.5** (279 visits over 6 sessions). |
| Target | **≥50 visits per session by week 6, sustained to week 13.** This is approximately the 2023 pilot's +38% effect applied to the 36.5 baseline (50/36.5 = +37%). The 60-attendee cap is above the target but below the hall's 180-seat capacity. |
| Source / frequency | Door count and registration; every event; weekly dashboard. |
| Why this target | It is anchored to the only prior evening result in the pack, not to an arbitrary aspiration. It is also above the current 2025 Q1 level, so it is a real test rather than a forecast of the status quo. |
| Cap treatment | If an event reaches 60, record the number admitted, the number turned away, and whether pre-registration exceeded 60. A run of capped events is evidence of unmet demand, not a reason to raise the cap without the accessible entrance. |

### M2. Priority-neighbourhood share of new evening attendees

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Definition | New evening attendees resident in Northside or Kiln Field divided by all new evening attendees. "New" means first-ever attendance at a centre evening event, self-reported at registration and checked against the registration record where possible. Estate is recorded by postcode or self-identification. |
| Baseline | **Not measured by the centre.** The pack has no neighbourhood breakdown. The 2023 pilot recorded that the **majority of new evening attendees** came from the two estates, but gives no count or denominator. The new doorstep survey gives a household baseline (15% have attended at least once), not a programme baseline. |
| Target | **≥50% of new evening attendees from Northside or Kiln Field**, with counts and denominators reported. |
| Source / frequency | Registration form; every event; weekly dashboard. |
| Why this target | It mirrors the 2023 qualitative result ("majority") and is the participation outcome that matters most for the priority-neighbourhood commitment. It is a planning target, not a measured baseline. |

### M3. Late minibus use

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Definition | Return boardings on the 21:30 service divided by priority-estate attendees. Also report outward boardings and total boardings per run. A "stranded attendee" is anyone who intended to use the bus and could not get home on it. |
| Baseline | **0** — there is no late service. The last regular bus leaves at **19:10**. |
| Target | **≥50% of priority-estate attendees use the 21:30 return; zero reported stranded attendees.** |
| Source / frequency | Driver manifest/boarding count; event registration; every run. |
| Why this target | The bus is the pilot's defining input. If estate attendees come but do not use it, either the route/timing is wrong or the audience is not the intended one; both need investigation. |

### M4. Evening event end time

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Definition | Percentage of pilot events that end by **21:00**, leaving 30 minutes for the 21:30 return. Record scheduled and actual end time. |
| Baseline | **Not recorded** in the pack. Existing Evening Talks end times are not stated. |
| Target | **100% of pilot events end by 21:00.** |
| Source / frequency | Programme schedule; responsible person's event log; every event. |
| Why this target | A 21:30 bus is useless if the event runs to 21:30. This is a design discipline, not a nice-to-have. |

### M5. Access compliance and the 60-attendee cap

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Definition | Three things: (a) percentage of evening events with **60 or fewer attendees** until the accessible entrance is operable; (b) number of first-floor public events (target zero); (c) week-1 confirmation that the ground floor has an independent step-free route and accessible toilet. Record any licensing breach, turn-away, or access complaint. |
| Baseline | **Accessible entrance and lift out of service; first-floor public events prohibited; no current evening programme.** The new condition requires the accessible entrance before any evening event with more than 60 attendees is licensed, whether or not it uses the first floor. |
| Target | **100% of evening events at or below 60; zero first-floor public events; ground-floor step-free route and accessible toilet confirmed in week 1.** If the ground floor is not independently accessible, the pilot does not start. |
| Source / frequency | Ticketing/door count; licensing records; week-1 access check; event log; every event; weekly dashboard. |
| Why this target | This is the current operating constraint. It is a compliance metric, not an attendance target. It must be reported separately so a capped or inaccessible event is not hidden inside a participation story. |
| Lift note | The lift is **not funded in this plan**. It is a separate £27,500 capital bid with an eight-week lead. Track the bid as context (submitted / approved / instructed / completed), but do not report it as a plan milestone. If a separate route completes and the accessible entrance is operable, the cap can be lifted only after handover, an independent access check, and licensing confirmation. |

### M6. Equalities monitoring completeness

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Definition | Percentage of pilot registrations with postcode/estate and access needs recorded. "Access needs" can be "none"; the field must be offered. Report the number who decline. |
| Baseline | **No neighbourhood or equalities breakdown** in `attendance.csv` or the survey. The council's required report cannot currently show priority-neighbourhood reach. The new doorstep survey is estate-level, not centre-attendance-level. |
| Target | **≥95% of pilot registrations record postcode/estate and access needs; the quarterly report includes attendance and equalities breakdowns for Northside and Kiln Field by week 13.** |
| Source / frequency | Registration form; weekly completeness check; quarterly report. |
| Why this target | The council requires it, and the board's fifth open question cannot be answered without it. It is also the foundation for future audience development. |

### M7. Pilot attendee experience

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Definition | Percentage of pilot attendees satisfied or very satisfied; percentage rating welcome and access as good; free-text themes. Report separately for priority-estate residents, disabled attendees/people with access needs, and first-time attendees. |
| Baseline | **92% overall satisfaction (285/310)** in the 2025 survey, but that sample is Family Saturdays free-entry weekend only (71% car, 78% with under-12s); there is no estate, disability, or evening breakdown. It is context, not a comparable baseline. |
| Target | **≥90% satisfied or very satisfied; results reported by segment; investigate any segment below 80%.** Target n ≥100 completed short surveys across the pilot. |
| Source / frequency | Short survey at weeks 6 and 13; registration; free-text. |
| Why this target | 90% is a floor, not a stretch: the existing figure is 92% for a different audience, and the pilot's value is whether new attendees feel welcome, not whether the already-satisfied core remains satisfied. |

### M8. Youth Studio expansion (funded)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Definition | Additional sessions delivered per month; total sessions per month; visits per session; monthly unique participants; waiting-list size and movement; whether the waitlist was used first. |
| Baseline | **76 visits per session, 2 sessions per month, 115 monthly unique participants** (March 2025); **waiting list of 40**. The partner's written clarification (U3) confirms supply fell, not interest, and that it can supply up to four extra sessions a month for the same £6,500. |
| Target | **Up to 4 additional sessions per month (6 total), waitlist first; ≥70 visits per session; waitlist reduced or cleared; report actual delivery.** If room or materials limit delivery to 2 extra sessions/month, deliver the originally quoted 2 and report the constraint. |
| Source / frequency | Monthly attendance return from the partner youth organisation; room booking records; waitlist log. |
| Why this target | The expansion is now funded, so it is no longer a guardrail. The target reflects what the partner says it can supply, with a delivery floor of the originally quoted two sessions if the room cannot support four. |

### M9. Targeted mailer reach and attribution

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Definition | Whether the mailer was dispatched to Northside and Kiln Field households in weeks 1–2; the number of registrations that cite the mailer; the share of **new estate attendees** attributed to the mailer; the cost per new estate attendee attributable to the mailer; and a comparison with partner/estate-network and word-of-mouth sources. |
| Baseline | **No mailer this quarter; no centre-level estate source data.** The new doorstep survey gives a household awareness baseline (48% never heard; 37% heard but never attended; 15% attended at least once). |
| Target | **Mailer dispatched weeks 1–2; source data allow every new estate registration to be attributed to a channel; report the mailer's incremental reach and cost per new estate attendee.** No arbitrary percentage target: the point is to test whether a specific mailer adds reach beyond existing networks. |
| Source / frequency | Mailer dispatch record; response code/QR; registration "how did you hear?"; weekly dashboard; final report. |
| Why this target | The equalities team explicitly says it has not tested whether awareness alone converts. The mailer is a test, not a proven fix. If it does not add measurable new estate reach, the plan should not repeat it. |

### M10. Welcome and access training

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Definition | Percentage of front-of-house staff and volunteers who complete the two-day welcome and access awareness training before the first event; a short pre/post confidence check; the access actions agreed (for example, how to record needs, how to manage the 60 cap, how to handle a request for step-free access). |
| Baseline | **No training this quarter.** The pack does not record previous training. |
| Target | **100% of front-of-house staff and volunteers trained before the first event; actions logged and reviewed at week 6.** |
| Source / frequency | Training register; pre/post check; event log; week-6 review. |
| Why this target | The 60 cap, the accessible entrance still being out, and the need to record access needs all depend on front-of-house confidence. The training is small but directly supports the access and welcome metrics. |

## 3. Secondary and contextual measures

These are reported but are not pass/fail targets:

- **Total evening visits** across the 13 weeks, alongside per-session attendance. Total visits will rise simply because there are more sessions; do not compare totals with the 6-session 2024 quarter without saying so.
- **New versus returning attendees.** A weekly programme may attract the same people more often; the estate-share metric should distinguish new attendees from returning ones.
- **Travel mode split** (minibus, regular bus, car, walk, other). This tests whether the bus is actually the access route, not just a gesture.
- **Source of hearing about the event.** Tests whether the mailer, partner/estate networks, or word of mouth is doing the work.
- **Cap hits and turn-aways.** The number of events at 60, the number of people turned away, and the pre-registration oversubscription rate.
- **Incidents and near-misses.** Zero serious incidents is the standard; minor incidents are logged and reviewed.
- **Cancellations and late finishes.** Delivery quality; a cancelled event is not a zero-attendance data point.
- **Access barriers reported.** Free-text and direct feedback; use to fix practical issues during the pilot, not only at the end.
- **Youth waitlist movement.** The number of young people moved off the waiting list into sessions, and the number still waiting.
- **Spend against the five committed lines.** Weekly tracker; report any line above quote and the reason.

## 4. Decision thresholds

These are the same thresholds as `pilot.md`; they are repeated here so the metrics and the pilot cannot drift apart.

| Status | Evening attendance | Estate share of new attendees | Bus use | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Green** | ≥50/session | ≥50% | ≥50% | Continue to week 13; prepare a continuation case subject to funding |
| **Amber** | 40–49/session | 35–49% | 30–49% | Continue to week 13; review content, timing, and outreach; no extra spend |
| **Red** | <40/session, or <36.5 by week 6 | <35% | <30% | Complete the committed quarter; do not recommend continuation; document why |

Additional rules:

- **60-cap breach:** an event admitting more than 60 is a compliance breach, not an attendance success. Stop admitting, log it, and report it to the board. The cap is not a target to beat.
- **Ground-floor access failure:** pause public events and do not start the pilot if the ground floor has no independent step-free route. Do not run an inaccessible pilot to protect the attendance number.
- **Lift delay (separate capital bid):** does not change the participation thresholds, but it means the accessible entrance remains out and the first-floor and >60 restrictions remain. Report it as an access delay, not a pilot result.
- **Youth delivery of 2–3 extra sessions/month:** amber, with a dated recovery plan; **below 2 extra sessions/month** is red against the quoted scope.
- **Mailer no measurable incremental reach:** report it and do not repeat; do not add a print campaign to compensate.
- **Serious safety incident:** triggers an immediate review; the responsible person can close an event.
- **Week-1 baseline check:** if it reveals a materially different starting point (for example, the 2024 comparison is not the right seasonal match), recalibrate **once**, document the reason, and do not move the goalposts after week 6.

## 5. Reporting

| Audience | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot lead / centre manager | Weekly dashboard: attendance, cap hits, estate share, bus boardings, mailer attribution, youth sessions, data completeness, incidents, spend | Weekly |
| Board / finance trustee | Monthly summary: progress against thresholds, access compliance, lift capital bid status, spend vs committed lines, risks | Monthly |
| Council | Quarterly report: attendance and equalities monitoring for Northside and Kiln Field, pilot outcomes, access position, spend | Week 13 |
| Participants / partners | Short plain-language summary of what was learned | After week 13 |

The council report should include:

- total visits and unique attendees, with the counting method stated;
- attendance by event and by month;
- cap hits and turn-aways;
- estate share with counts and denominators;
- travel mode and bus boardings;
- mailer and other source attribution;
- access needs and barriers reported;
- satisfaction by segment;
- Youth Studio sessions, visits per session, and waitlist movement;
- access status: ground-floor route, first-floor closure, lift capital bid, any licensing breach;
- what changed, what did not, and what the centre will do next.

## 6. Limitations of this evaluation

- **No control group.** The comparison is same-weeks prior year and the 2025 Q1 evening baseline. Seasonality, weather, novelty, and other events cannot be fully separated.
- **The 60 cap truncates attendance.** A capped event cannot show whether demand was 60 or 160. Report cap hits and turn-aways; do not interpret a capped series as the demand ceiling.
- **Small numbers.** A weekly evening programme may attract tens, not hundreds, per session. Percentages based on small denominators will be unstable; report counts alongside them.
- **Self-selection.** Attendees who complete the survey are not a random sample of attendees, and attendees are not a random sample of the estates.
- **No non-attendee survey at the centre.** The new doorstep survey is a small estate-level baseline, not a centre non-visitor survey. The pilot can say who came; it cannot fully say why others did not.
- **Undercounting on the bus.** Driver manifests may miss boardings; use registration and door count to triangulate.
- **Access-needs disclosure is voluntary.** A 95% completeness target applies to the field being offered and recorded, not to everyone disclosing a need.
- **The 92% baseline is not comparable.** It is a different audience, season, and survey method. Do not present a before/after satisfaction change as if it were the same population.
- **The lift is a separate capital bid, not a plan milestone.** Its benefit is access and compliance; this evaluation does not attempt a cost-benefit analysis of the capital spend.
- **Mailer attribution is self-reported.** A registration source is a claim, not a randomised exposure. The comparison with network-attributed registrations is descriptive.

These limitations do not make the pilot unmeasurable. They define what the centre can honestly claim at week 13.
