# Evidence assessment (round 2)

## Bottom line

The pack now supports five conclusions strongly enough to plan around:

1. **Transport is the binding constraint for estate residents who already know the centre.** The 2023 evening pilot worked while the minibus ran and collapsed when it stopped. The new doorstep survey sharpens this: among residents who have heard of the centre, **57% name "getting home after an evening event" as the main barrier**, against 22% for cost and 11% for "nothing there interests me".
2. **Awareness is not uniform, so the board's 2022 conclusion needs qualifying.** The same survey finds **48% of Northside and Kiln Field households have never heard of the centre**, 37% have heard but never attended, and only 15% have attended at least once. "Awareness exists" was too broad. The right response is a *targeted* approach to a *specific* audience with a *specific offer*, not a repeat of the 2022 borough-wide poster campaign.
3. **The Youth Studio is supply-constrained, not demand-constrained — now confirmed in writing.** Sessions halved in June 2024; monthly visits fell, but visits per session and unique participants rose. The partner's written clarification adds a **waiting list of 40**, confirms the cause was its own funding change, and offers **up to four extra sessions a month for the same £6,500**. The interview speculation that young people had "moved on" is withdrawn.
4. **The 92% satisfaction figure is still real for the people surveyed but cannot be generalised.** It remains a self-selected, in-person, free-entry Family Saturday sample: 310 forms, 71% car-borne, 78% with under-12s. The new doorstep survey helps with estate barriers but does not turn the satisfaction survey into a borough-wide picture.
5. **The lift is now £27,500 with an eight-week lead, and a new licensing condition applies.** The round-1 three-line plan (lift + evening opening + minibus) would now cost **£51,500**, which is **£3,500 over the £48,000 envelope**. The new condition also requires the accessible entrance to be operable before **any evening event with more than 60 attendees is licensed**, whether or not it uses the first floor. The plan therefore **defers the lift to a separate capital bid, keeps the transport-linked evening pilot, and caps evening events at 60**. This is the most important change from round 1.

The plan still has to **buy access to the programme and build the measurement that is currently missing**. It can no longer buy the building access fix and the transport fix together within this envelope.

## 1. What the attendance data show

`attendance.csv` covers January 2024 to March 2025, by month and programme. The important patterns:

| Programme | Pattern in the data | Reading |

Two cautions about the data:

- **"Unique participants" is monthly, not annual.** Summing twelve months would double-count people. The pack does not contain a deduplicated annual participant count.
- **There is no neighbourhood, postcode, disability, or equalities field.** The data can describe programmes and months, but cannot answer the board's fifth question — which programmes actually reach Northside and Kiln Field. The plan has to create that baseline.

### The Youth Studio question is now settled

Round 1 found a contradiction in the youth worker's interview: the worker said numbers were down because young people had "moved on", but also said sessions ran less often because of staffing and filled when they ran. **U3 resolves it in writing.** The partner organisation confirms:

- its own funding changed in June 2024 and it cut delivery from four sessions a month to two;
- that is the whole reason the monthly *visit* figure fell;
- unique participants rose from 71 in January 2024 to 115 in March 2025;
- it now holds a **waiting list of 40**;
- it can supply staff for **up to four extra sessions a month** in the coming quarter for the same **£6,500**;
- the interview speculation is withdrawn.

The data and the partner now agree. The only remaining gap is that "unique participants" is monthly and not deduplicated, so the plan still cannot state an annual unduplicated count. The waiting list is the best available measure of unmet demand, and it is large enough to justify the expansion.

## 2. The two surveys

### 2.1 The 2025 Visitor Satisfaction Survey

**What it says**

- 310 completed forms, handed to visitors at Family Saturdays across the free-entry weekend of 1–2 March 2025.
- 92% satisfied or very satisfied (285/310); 89% would recommend; 87% say the programme meets their needs.
- 64% heard about the centre by word of mouth; 71% travelled by car; 78% attended with children under 12.
- Free-text themes: friendly staff (81), good value (57), parking (44), more evening provision (12), café (9), "didn't know you ran other things" (6).

**What it supports**

It supports a claim about **that specific audience on that weekend**: they are satisfied, they value the staff and the price, they are largely car-borne families, and they hear about the centre by word of mouth. It is useful for service quality and for understanding the Family Saturdays audience.

**What it does not support**

The summary's sentence — "This gives us a robust picture of how our audiences feel about the centre" — is not supported by the method. The marketing volunteer's own note at the bottom is the correct caveat:

> "It covers the people who come on a Saturday. We did not survey people who do not come, and the weekday and evening audiences were not in the room because the free-entry weekend was a family event."

Specific limitations:

- **Selection bias:** forms were handed out in person at one event type on one free-entry weekend. Respondents self-selected. There is no sampling frame, no denominator, and no non-response analysis.
- **Coverage bias:** no non-visitors, no weekday audience, no evening audience, no priority-estate quota, no disabled visitors, no equalities breakdown.
- **Framing bias:** a free-entry family weekend is not a neutral setting for questions about price, evening demand, or barriers to attendance.
- **The 71% car figure is a description of the respondents, not of the borough or the centre's whole audience.** It is consistent with the front-of-house coordinator's "most of our regulars drive", but neither source measures estate residents' travel.
- **The free-text counts are mentions, not priorities.** Parking (44) probably reflects the car-heavy sample; evening provision (12) and "didn't know you ran other things" (6) are weak signals, not demand estimates.

**Consequence:** 92% cannot be used to conclude that audience development is no longer needed, or that the centre's offer meets the needs of people who do not currently attend. The board's first open question should still be answered "no".

### 2.2 The new doorstep survey of Northside and Kiln Field

**What it says**

A doorstep survey of **120 households** across the two priority estates, completed last month by the council's equalities team:

| Finding | Share |
|---|---:|
| Have never heard of the centre | **48%** |
| Have heard of it but never attended | **37%** |
| Have attended at least once | **15%** |
| Of those who have heard: "getting home after an evening event" is the main barrier | **57%** |
| Of those who have heard: cost is the main barrier | **22%** |
| Of those who have heard: "nothing there interests me" | **11%** |

The equalities team's covering note is important:

> "Awareness is not uniform. Half of the estates have never heard of you. Among those who have, transport home is the dominant stated barrier, not programme content. We have not tested whether awareness alone would convert to attendance."

**What it supports**

- **Awareness is a real barrier for about half the estates.** The 2022 board minute — "awareness exists; the barrier is not awareness" — was drawn from a borough-wide poster campaign and is too broad. Half of these households have never heard of the centre.
- **Among residents who know the centre, transport dominates.** Fifty-seven per cent name getting home, against 22% cost and 11% content. This is consistent with the 2023 pilot and the front-of-house coordinator's account of the 19:10 last bus.
- **A targeted mailer is now defensible.** The 2022 minute rules out repeating a broad poster spend "without a specific audience and a specific offer". A mailer to Northside and Kiln Field households, leading with the weekly evening programme and the late minibus, has both. It is not the same intervention as the 2022 borough-wide campaign.

**What it does not support**

- **It is a small doorstep survey (120 households), not a representative borough survey.** The percentages have wide margins of error, and the 57/22/11 split is among the roughly half who have heard of the centre, so the base is small.
- **It measures stated barriers, not behaviour.** "Getting home is the main barrier" is a self-report; the 2023 pilot is the behavioural evidence that transport actually changes attendance.
- **It does not test whether awareness alone converts.** The equalities team says so explicitly. A mailer may raise awareness without producing attendance; the pilot's registration source data will show whether it did.
- **It is household-level, not programme-level.** It cannot say which of the centre's programmes reach the estates. The board's fifth question still needs the centre's own registration data.
- **It does not say whether the 15% who have attended still attend, or what they attended.** "Attended at least once" is not current participation.

**Synthesis:** the two barriers are sequential, not alternatives. For the **unaware half**, awareness is the first barrier. For the **aware half**, transport is the dominant barrier. A plan that only buys a bus will under-reach the unaware; a plan that only buys awareness will tell people about an evening they cannot get home from. The current plan buys both, but it buys a *targeted* mailer, not a borough-wide campaign.

## 3. The two prior interventions

### 2023 evening opening pilot (12 weeks)

The centre opened on Thursday evenings for talks and film and ran a paid late minibus from Northside and Kiln Field, returning after the event.

- Evening attendance was **38% higher** than the same weeks the previous year.
- The **majority of new evening attendees came from the two priority estates**.
- Staff reported no incidents.
- When the minibus funding ran out, the centre continued the evening programme for six more weeks without it. Attendance fell back to roughly the pre-pilot level, and the new estate attendees stopped coming. The programme was then suspended.

This is the strongest causal signal in the pack. The programme did not fail when the offer changed; it failed when the transport was withdrawn. The front-of-house coordinator's note — "The evenings worked. They stopped working when the bus stopped. We tested that by accident." — is a fair summary.

Limitations to keep visible:

- Single site, single 12-week autumn pilot, no contemporaneous control group.
- The +38% is a year-on-year comparison, so seasonality, weather, novelty, and other changes are not separated.
- The pack gives no exact attendance counts, no estate denominators, and no demographic detail.
- The reversal after withdrawal is stronger evidence than the +38% itself, but it is still one natural experiment.

### 2022 borough-wide print and poster campaign

- £9,000 on posters and leaflets across the borough, plus a mail-drop.
- Total attendance for the quarter was within normal seasonal variation.
- Word of mouth remained the dominant channel (64% of post-campaign survey respondents).
- No measurable change in first-time attendance from the priority estates.
- The board minuted: *"Do not repeat the poster spend without a specific audience and a specific offer."*

This is direct evidence against repeating a borough-wide print campaign. It is not evidence that every targeted communication is useless. The new doorstep survey shows why: the 2022 campaign was broad and had no specific offer, while half the estates still had not heard of the centre. The board's condition — specific audience, specific offer — is the right test, and the current mailer is designed to meet it.

## 4. Operating constraints that shape what is possible

- **Physical access and licensing.** The accessible entrance and passenger lift are out of service. The lift repair is **capital**, now quoted at **£27,500** with an **eight-week lead** from instruction to completion, and it **cannot be phased**. The new building-control condition requires the accessible entrance to be operable before **any evening event with more than 60 attendees is licensed**, whether or not the event uses the first floor. Two consequences follow: first-floor public use would be possible only from about **week 9** of a 13-week quarter, and only if the lift were separately funded and instructed in week 1; and a ground-floor evening programme can run without the lift only if it is **capped at 60 attendees**. The first floor stays closed under this plan.
- **Transport.** Northside and Kiln Field are two bus changes from the centre (the front-of-house interview describes "a bus change and a walk"; the plan uses the conservative constraint figure). The last regular service back leaves at **19:10** on weekdays. There is **no staffed car park after 20:00**. Any evening event that finishes after about 19:00 is effectively unreachable by regular bus; the 2023 minibus was the workaround.
- **Staffing.** 1.5 FTE front-of-house across the week. There is no events manager post. Evening opening requires a **named responsible person on site**, which the current rota does not staff. The £9,600 evening opening quote assumes existing staff are extended, not a new post.
- **Season and money.** One quarter, 13 weeks, beginning the first week of April. The budget is committed at the start; unspent funds are not carried. The council requires one quarterly report with attendance and equalities monitoring for the priority neighbourhoods.
- **Capital/revenue.** Capital and revenue cannot be swapped without a council variation. The pack gives a single £48,000 envelope but does not state its capital/revenue split. This plan commits **only revenue lines** (£37,500) and keeps the lift out of the envelope, so it does not require a capital/revenue swap. The lift needs a separate capital route.

## 5. What the pack cannot tell us

These gaps are load-bearing, not footnotes:

- **No neighbourhood-level attendance or equalities data for the centre's programmes**, so the board's question "which programmes reach Northside and Kiln Field?" cannot be answered today. The new doorstep survey gives estate-level awareness and barriers, not programme attendance. The 2023 pilot's "majority of new attendees" is a statement without a count or a denominator.
- **No non-visitor research beyond the 120-household doorstep survey.** The survey is useful but small, and it measures stated barriers, not behaviour.
- **No test of whether awareness alone converts.** The equalities team says this explicitly. The mailer is a test, not a proven fix.
- **No deduplicated annual Youth Studio participant count.** The waiting list of 40 is the best available measure of unmet demand, but it is not a full demand curve.
- **No costed first-floor programme.** The lift repair restores a lawful/accessible route, but the pack quotes no activity to put on the first floor. The plan does not invent one.
- **No capital/revenue split** of the £48,000, and no confirmation that the lift can be charged to this envelope. The plan avoids the question by funding only revenue lines and deferring the lift.
- **No confirmed next-quarter budget.** The council "verbally indicated" a possible reduction of up to 15%; nothing is agreed. Recurring commitments therefore carry real risk.
- **No operator detail** on route stops, minibus capacity, wheelchair accessibility, driver arrangements, or reliability.
- **No control group** for the pilot, and no historical data by neighbourhood. Evaluation will be descriptive, not causal in a strict sense.
- **No confirmation that the ground floor has an independent step-free route.** If it does not, the pilot cannot run without the lift, and the plan must be re-planned.

## 6. Implications for the quarterly plan

The evidence points to a focused plan rather than a broad one:

1. **Fund the transport-linked evening pilot, with a hard 60-attendee cap.** It replicates the only intervention in the pack with a positive participation result for the priority estates, and it is time-limited. The cap is the lawful way to run before the accessible entrance is restored; the first floor stays closed.
2. **Fund the Youth Studio expansion.** The partner's written clarification removes the contradiction, adds a 40-name waiting list, and offers up to four extra sessions a month for the same £6,500. This is the best-evidenced supply fix in the pack and it is affordable once the lift is deferred.
3. **Fund the targeted mailer.** The new doorstep survey shows 48% of estate households have never heard of the centre. A mailer to those households, with the specific evening-plus-minibus offer, meets the board's 2022 test in a way the borough-wide poster campaign did not.
4. **Fund the welcome and access training.** The 60 cap, the accessible entrance still being out, and the need to record access needs all depend on front-of-house confidence. It is a quoted line and it fits.
5. **Defer the lift to a separate capital bid.** It cannot be funded alongside the transport-linked evening pilot inside £48,000: £27,500 + £9,600 + £14,400 = £51,500, which is £3,500 over. It cannot be phased, a part-share of the quote would not buy a usable repair, and capital cannot be swapped with revenue. The plan is explicit that this is an access compromise, not a claim that access is solved.
6. **Do not fund the borough-wide print campaign.** The 2022 result and the board minute rule it out.
7. **Do not fund the café refresh.** U4 makes it unavailable this quarter and it has no programming rationale.
8. **Build the missing baseline.** Collect postcode/estate, access needs, travel mode, and first-visit status at pilot registration, and extend the same fields to the other programmes where feasible. That is how the council report gets its equalities monitoring and how the board eventually gets an answer to question 5.

The resulting plan commits **£37,500** against the £48,000 envelope, leaving **£10,500** uncommitted. The next documents translate this into options, a pilot design, a budget, metrics, a delivery plan, and an explicit uncertainty register.
