# Transport-linked evening opening pilot (round 2)

**Working title:** Thursday Lates
**Duration:** 13 weeks, beginning the first week of April
**Pilot cost:** **£24,000** (evening opening £9,600 + late minibus £14,400)
**Complementary funded workstreams:** Youth Studio expansion £6,500; targeted mailer £4,200; staff training £2,800
**Whole-plan cost:** **£37,500** against a £48,000 envelope; £10,500 uncommitted
**Access position:** lift **deferred** to a separate capital bid at £27,500 (eight-week lead); first floor closed; all evening events **capped at 60 attendees** until the accessible entrance is operable

## 1. What the pilot is

A weekly evening programme in the ground-floor main hall for 13 weeks, paired with a subsidised minibus on the Northside–Kiln Field loop:

- **Outward:** 19:15 departure from the two estates to the centre.
- **Return:** 21:30 departure from the centre.
- **Programme:** a mix of talks, film, and participatory sessions, using the existing Evening Talks format and partner/community input. Every event ends by **21:00** so the return can run.
- **Venue:** ground-floor main hall, capped at **60 attendees** per evening until the accessible entrance is operable. The hall seats 180, but the licensing condition, not the room, sets the cap.
- **Staffing:** a named responsible person on site every evening, plus existing front-of-house cover. No new post.

The lift is **not part of this pilot or this quarter's budget**. The new quote is £27,500 with an eight-week lead, and the three-line plan (lift + evening + bus) would cost £51,500 — £3,500 over the envelope. The pilot therefore runs on the ground floor under the new 60-attendee licensing condition. The first floor stays closed. The lift is a separate capital bid, and the pilot's success is judged on participation and transport, not on whether a capital project finishes.

Keeping the access position explicit matters. The plan does **not** claim the building's access problem is solved. It claims that a 60-capped ground-floor programme is lawful under the current condition, and that the transport-linked evening model is the best-evidenced participation intervention available inside the envelope.

## 2. Why this pilot

The evidence is unusually direct for a community arts centre:

- The 2023 evening pilot produced **38% higher evening attendance** than the same weeks the previous year, and the **majority of new evening attendees came from Northside and Kiln Field**.
- When the minibus funding ended, the centre kept the evening programme for six more weeks. Attendance **fell back to roughly the pre-pilot level**, and the new estate attendees **stopped coming**. The programme did not suddenly become bad; the transport disappeared.
- The front-of-house coordinator describes the same barrier today: the estates are two bus changes away, the last regular bus is **19:10**, and "anything that finishes after seven is no use to them."
- The new doorstep survey of 120 estate households finds that, among residents who have heard of the centre, **57% name getting home after an evening event as the main barrier** — against 22% for cost and 11% for content.
- The same survey finds **48% of estate households have never heard of the centre**. That is why the plan pairs the pilot with a **targeted mailer** to those households, not a borough-wide poster campaign.

The pilot is therefore a **replication with a designed ending**, not a new experiment. It tests whether the 2023 model still works in the current season and with the current audience, while the mailer tests whether targeted awareness adds reach and the youth expansion uses the waiting list the partner has identified.

## 3. Hypothesis

> If the centre runs a weekly evening programme with a late return minibus, then evening attendance will reach at least **50 visits per session** — about 37% above the April–June 2024 baseline of 36.5 and approximately the 2023 +38% effect — and at least **half of new evening attendees will come from Northside or Kiln Field**, because the 2023 gains disappeared when the transport was withdrawn.

The hypothesis is supported if the attendance and estate-share thresholds are met by week 6 and sustained to week 13. It is not supported if attendance stays at or below the 2024 baseline or if estate residents do not use the service, even with the bus.

**The 60 cap is a constraint on the test, not part of the hypothesis.** If events repeatedly hit 60, the pilot has more demand than it can license; that is a finding to report, not a reason to raise the cap without the accessible entrance.

## 4. Target segments

**Primary**

- Residents of **Northside** and **Kiln Field**, the centre's stated priority neighbourhoods.
- People in those estates who do not have a car or cannot rely on the last regular bus.
- Adults and young adults looking for evening cultural activity; families where the content is suitable.

**Secondary**

- People without a car from elsewhere in the borough who can reach the route.
- Evening and weekend workers who cannot attend daytime or Family Saturday provision.
- Existing and lapsed Evening Talks attendees — the long-standing member's account shows that people who used to come may not know the programme is back.
- Disabled visitors and people with access needs. The ground-floor hall is the pilot venue; the accessible entrance and lift remain out, so the plan caps attendance at 60 and requires the ground-floor step-free route to be confirmed in week 1. Disabled visitors are not a secondary audience for access purposes: if the ground floor is not independently accessible, the pilot does not run.

**Not targeted**

- A borough-wide general audience. There is no paid borough print campaign; the 2022 evidence does not support it.
- First-floor audiences. No first-floor public event is planned this quarter.

## 5. Design detail

### 5.1 Programme

- One evening a week for 13 weeks. Thursday is the recommended slot; confirm against the rota and operator availability in week 1.
- A mixed programme of talks, film, and participatory sessions. Use the existing Evening Talks format as the anchor, with partner/community input into at least part of the programme.
- Events start at a time that allows the 19:15 minibus arrival and end by **21:00**.
- **Hard cap: 60 attendees per evening** until the accessible entrance is operable and the licensing position is confirmed. This is a condition of the current building-control advice, not a target. Count door admissions and pre-registrations; stop admitting once 60 are admitted; record turn-aways. If the cap is hit, offer turn-aways priority booking for the next event so the cap does not systematically exclude the priority estates.
- No first-floor public event at all this quarter. If a separate capital route later restores the lift, first-floor activity would still need its own costed programme and risk assessment; this plan does not commit one.

### 5.2 Transport

- One minibus on the Northside–Kiln Field loop, 13 weeks, 19:15 outward and 21:30 return, per the operator quote.
- Booking or registration for the return journey so the operator and centre know expected numbers.
- Capacity, wheelchair accessibility, driver arrangements, safeguarding, and route stops must be confirmed with the operator before the first run. These are not specified in the pack and are a week-1 condition.
- A named person at the centre is responsible for the departure: no event ends without confirming the bus has left and no one is stranded.
- A simple fallback (taxi/refund/credit) must be agreed with the operator for a breakdown or no-show. Any cost must be quoted and approved by the board before commitment; it is not assumed in this budget.

### 5.3 Staffing and rota

- A **named responsible person** on site for every evening. This is the constraint the pack flags: the centre has no events manager post and the current rota does not staff evening opening.
- The £9,600 assumes existing staff are extended, not a new post. Overtime, TOIL, and cover arrangements are not costed separately; they must be agreed within the 1.5 FTE envelope before the first event.
- If no named responsible person can be rostered for the full 13 weeks, **the pilot does not start**. A bus without an event is not a pilot.

### 5.4 Communications

The plan funds a **targeted mailer to Northside and Kiln Field households** (£4,200), plus no-cost channels:

- one mailer to priority-neighbourhood households, dispatched in **weeks 1–2**, leading with the specific offer: a weekly evening programme and the late minibus home;
- a response code or registration source so the pilot can see whether the mailer added reach;
- the partner youth organisation's estate networks;
- estate community groups, noticeboards, and existing local networks;
- the centre's existing email/social channels and word of mouth;
- direct invitation to lapsed Evening Talks attendees where records exist.

This is deliberately **not** the 2022 borough-wide poster campaign. The board's minute ruled out repeating that spend "without a specific audience and a specific offer". The mailer has both: the two priority estates, and the evening-plus-minibus offer. It is still a test — the equalities team has not shown that awareness alone converts — so the registration source data decides whether it is worth repeating. Design and distribution management are assumed to be within existing staff time; the £4,200 quote covers printing and postage, and no additional design cost is assumed.

If the mailer does not add reach beyond partner/estate networks, the plan should say so and not repeat it. The fallback is the no-cost network approach, not a bigger print spend.

### 5.5 Access

- The pilot venue is the ground-floor main hall. **Confirm in week 1 that the ground floor has an independent step-free route and accessible toilet.** If it does not, the pilot does not start; the board must re-plan (see `options.md`, Options B and C).
- **Cap every evening event at 60 attendees.** The new building-control condition requires the accessible entrance to be operable before any evening event with more than 60 attendees is licensed, whether or not it uses the first floor.
- The lift/accessible-entrance repair is **not funded in this plan**. It is a separate capital bid at £27,500 with an eight-week lead. If a separate route is approved and instructed in week 1, completion would be at end week 8 and public use from week 9 at the earliest; this plan does not assume it.
- No first-floor public event before the lift is commissioned and independently checked. Under this plan, that means no first-floor public event this quarter.
- Access needs are recorded at registration so the centre can act on them, not just count them.

### 5.6 Data and consent

At registration or first visit, collect:

- postcode or estate (Northside / Kiln Field / other);
- access needs (voluntary, with a clear purpose statement);
- travel mode (minibus / regular bus / car / walk / other);
- how they heard about the event (including the mailer code);
- whether this is their first visit to an evening event;
- consent to be contacted for a short follow-up survey.

Also keep: door counts per event, turn-aways when the 60 cap is reached, bus boardings per run, incident log, staff debrief notes. Data should be minimised, stored securely, and reported in aggregate with small cells suppressed (fewer than five). The pack is synthetic and contains no personal data; the real pilot would need a privacy notice and retention period before week 1.

## 8. Success criteria and decision rules

| Criterion | Green | Amber | Red | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Evening attendance per session | ≥50 (about +37% on the 36.5 Apr–Jun 2024 baseline; approximately the 2023 +38% effect) | 40–49 | <40, or <36.5 by week 6 | Weekly; formal at week 6 and 13 |
| Share of new evening attendees from Northside/Kiln Field | ≥50% | 35–49% | <35% | Weekly; formal at week 6 and 13 |
| Priority-estate attendees using the 21:30 return | ≥50% | 30–49% | <30% | Weekly |
| Events ending by 21:00 | 100% | One late finish, corrected | Repeated late finishes | Every event |
| Evening events at or below 60 attendees | 100% at or below 60; every cap hit and turn-away logged | One event briefly above 60, corrected immediately and reported | Any event admitted above 60 and not corrected, or cap not enforced | Every event |
| Ground-floor step-free route and accessible toilet | Confirmed week 1 | Confirmed with a documented temporary arrangement and a dated remedy; any cost needs a written quote and board approval | Not available | Week 1 |
| Equalities data completeness | ≥95% of registrations | 85–94% | <85% | Weekly; final week 13 |
| Serious incidents | Zero | Minor incidents, managed | Serious incident | Every event |
| Participant satisfaction | ≥90% | 80–89% | <80% | Weeks 6 and 13 |

**Complementary workstreams** are reported separately so they cannot be used to obscure a weak pilot result:

| Workstream | Green | Amber | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Youth Studio expansion | Up to 4 extra sessions/month delivered; waitlist reduced; ≥70 visits/session | 2–3 extra sessions/month; waitlist stable | Fewer than 2 extra sessions/month, or visits/session <70 |
| Targeted mailer | Mailer dispatched weeks 1–2; source data show it adds new estate registrations beyond partner/estate networks | Dispatched, but attribution unclear | Not dispatched, or no measurable new estate reach |
| Staff training | 100% of front-of-house/volunteers trained before first event | Most trained; catch-up dated | Not delivered before first event |

**Decision rules**

- **Green at week 6:** continue unchanged to week 13; prepare a continuation case (subject to funding) for the board.
- **Amber at week 6:** continue to week 13, but review content, timing, and outreach. No additional spend. Record what changed.
- **Red at week 6:** the costs are already committed for 13 weeks, so the pilot runs to the end of the quarter unless there is a safety or access failure. Do not recommend continuation; document why. Do not add activity to rescue it.
- **Cap red:** if an event admits more than 60, stop admitting immediately, log the breach, and report it to the board. The cap is a licensing condition, not an attendance target.
- **Ground-floor access red:** if the main hall does not have an independent step-free route, pause public events and do not start the pilot. Do not run an inaccessible pilot.
- **Lift red (separate capital bid):** first-floor public events remain prohibited. The ground-floor pilot can continue if the hall is accessible. A lift delay is an access failure, not a reason to overclaim the pilot.
- **Mailer red:** if the mailer does not add measurable new estate reach, do not repeat it; rely on partner/estate networks and say so in the report.

## 10. Evaluation and reporting

The evaluation is **descriptive, not a controlled trial**. It compares the pilot with the same weeks in 2024 and with the 2025 Q1 evening baseline, and it reports:

- attendance per session, total visits, and unique attendees;
- cap hits and turn-aways;
- new versus returning evening attendees;
- estate share, with counts and denominators;
- bus boardings and travel mode;
- mailer attribution and other sources;
- access needs and any barriers reported;
- satisfaction and free-text themes;
- incidents and delivery issues;
- data completeness;
- Youth Studio sessions delivered, visits per session, and waitlist movement.

The council requires one quarterly report with attendance and equalities monitoring for the priority neighbourhoods. That report is the formal output at week 13. The board gets a shorter decision note: what happened, what it cost, whether the hypothesis held, and what to do next.

The main threats to interpretation are **seasonality and novelty**: April–June is a different season from the 2023 autumn pilot, and a new weekly offer may attract curiosity. The same-weeks comparison and the per-session baseline reduce, but do not eliminate, this. The **60 cap** is a third threat: attendance may be supply-constrained by the licensing condition rather than by demand. The report should state how often the cap was hit and how many people were turned away.

## 11. Exit and continuation

At week 13:

- The bus and evening opening stop unless continuation funding is identified. There is no carry-over.
- The youth expansion, mailer, and training are one-off commitments for this quarter; they do not create a new post.
- The lift remains a separate capital bid. Any future first-floor programme needs its own costed plan and a completed accessible entrance.
- If the pilot is green, the board can seek a council variation, a next-quarter allocation, partner sponsorship, or a lower-frequency model under a new quote. It should not assume the council's verbal 15% reduction is either confirmed or avoided.
- If the pilot is red or amber, the honest recommendation is to stop or redesign, not to rebrand the same activity. The 2023 evidence says that an evening programme without transport will not hold the estate audience. The new evidence adds a second warning: a transport offer without reach will under-fill, which is why the mailer must be judged on attribution, not on dispatch.
