# Options considered (round 2)

## Decision rule

Every option had to satisfy six tests:

1. **Evidence of participation effect** — does it address a barrier the pack actually identifies, rather than a plausible-sounding one?
2. **Service accessibility** — does it improve access to the building or to the programme for people currently excluded, and does it comply with the current licensing condition?
3. **Deliverability in 13 weeks** — can it be contracted, staffed, and run within the quarter, including the lift's **eight-week** lead if the lift is included?
4. **Affordability** — is it within **£48,000** using only quoted costs, with no invented, pro-rated, or negotiated figures?
5. **Financial and legal risk** — does it avoid an unfunded recurring commitment before an unconfirmed next-quarter cut, and does it avoid a capital/revenue swap?
6. **Reversibility** — if it does not work, can it be stopped at the end of the quarter without a new post or a stranded capital commitment?

All costs below are taken directly from `sources/resources.md` and the round-2 updates. The lift is now **£27,500** (U2), not £22,000. No cost has been pro-rated, negotiated, or invented.

## The binding arithmetic

The round-1 plan was lift + evening opening + minibus:

```
Lift (old quote)                    £22,000
Evening opening, 13 weeks            £9,600
Late bus subsidy, 13 weeks          £14,400
                                   ---------
Round-1 total                       £46,000   (within £48,000)
```

With the revised lift quote:

```
Lift (new quote)                    £27,500
Evening opening, 13 weeks            £9,600
Late bus subsidy, 13 weeks          £14,400
                                   ---------
Same plan today                     £51,500   (£3,500 over the envelope)
```

The lift cannot be phased, so a part-share would not buy a usable repair, and it cannot be swapped between capital and revenue without a council variation. So the real choice is **the lift or the transport-linked evening pilot**, not both. The rest of this document compares the alternatives.

## Option A — Transport-led participation package (recommended)

**What it buys**

| Item | Quoted cost |
|---|---:|
| Evening opening, 13 weeks (responsible person + utilities + cleaning) | £9,600 |
| Late bus subsidy: one minibus on the Northside–Kiln Field loop, 13 weeks | £14,400 |
| Additional Youth Studio sessions (partner can supply up to 4 extra sessions a month at the quoted price) | £6,500 |
| Targeted mailer to priority-neighbourhood households | £4,200 |
| Staff training: welcome and access awareness, 2 days | £2,800 |
| **Total** | **£37,500** |
| **Uncommitted headroom** | **£10,500** |

**Why it scores well**

- **Participation:** it keeps the 2023 bundle that produced 38% higher evening attendance and majority new estate attendees. It addresses the barrier the new doorstep survey identifies as dominant among residents who know the centre — getting home after an evening event (57%) — rather than adding more of the activity that already works for existing audiences.
- **Awareness:** the new survey also shows 48% of estate households have never heard of the centre. The £4,200 mailer is targeted to those households and leads with a specific offer (the weekly evening programme plus the late minibus). That is the board's 2022 test — specific audience, specific offer — and is not a repeat of the £9,000 borough-wide poster campaign.
- **Supply:** the partner's written clarification (U3) confirms the Youth Studio is supply-constrained, with a 40-name waiting list and capacity for up to four extra sessions a month for the same £6,500. This is the best-evidenced supply fix in the pack.
- **Access compliance:** the plan does not pretend the building's access problem is solved. It **caps every evening event at 60 attendees** until the accessible entrance is operable, which is the new licensing condition. The first floor stays closed. The lift is a separate capital bid.
- **Deliverability:** no lift works to sequence. The pilot can start in week 1 if the ground floor has an independent step-free route, the named responsible person is rostered, and the operator contract is signed. The lift is not on the critical path.
- **Affordability:** £37,500 of £48,000 (78.1%), leaving £10,500. All five lines are revenue, so the plan does not require a capital/revenue swap. The revenue lines are time-limited to 13 weeks; the mailer and training are one-off.
- **Resilience:** a 15% cut to the following quarter would leave £40,800. The £37,500 revenue plan would still fit if the quotes hold, with £3,300 headroom. The lift would not fit and remains a separate capital decision. This is a better position than round 1's £46,000 plan.

**Weaknesses**

- **The lift is not funded.** The accessible entrance and passenger lift remain out, the first floor remains closed, and evening events are capped at 60. This is an access compromise. The plan recommends a separate capital bid, but it cannot promise that bid will succeed this quarter.
- **The 60 cap may truncate demand.** The target is ≥50 visits per session, so 60 gives only 20% headroom. If events hit the cap, the measured attendance will understate demand. The plan records cap hits and turn-aways rather than treating them as a failure.
- **The mailer is a test, not a proven fix.** The equalities team explicitly says it has not tested whether awareness alone converts. If the mailer does not add reach beyond partner/estate networks, it should not be repeated.
- **The Youth Studio expansion depends on room and materials.** The partner can supply staff for up to four extra sessions a month; the centre must confirm the room is available and the £6,500 covers the materials. If only two extra sessions fit, the same quote still buys the originally quoted two.
- **£10,500 is left uncommitted.** No remaining quoted line fits: the lift is £27,500, the print campaign is rejected, and the café refresh is no longer available. The headroom lapses, not carried.

## Option B — Access-first (lift + evening + youth + mailer)

**What it buys**

| Item | Quoted cost |
|---|---:|
| Lift repair and accessible entrance reinstatement (capital) | £27,500 |
| Evening opening, 13 weeks | £9,600 |
| Additional Youth Studio sessions | £6,500 |
| Targeted mailer to priority-neighbourhood households | £4,200 |
| **Total** | **£47,800** |
| **Uncommitted headroom** | **£200** |

**Why it is not chosen**

- **It omits the minibus.** The 2023 pilot's estate attendance collapsed when the bus stopped, and the new doorstep survey says 57% of aware residents name getting home as the main barrier. An evening programme without the bus reproduces the failure mode, not the success.
- **The lift would be usable only from week 9 at the earliest.** With an eight-week lead, even an instruction in week 1 completes at end week 8. Before that, the accessible entrance is still out; if the ground floor has no independent step-free route, no public evening event can run accessibly until the lift is live. The £27,500 buys about five weeks of in-quarter benefit.
- **It leaves no room for training.** At £47,800, the remaining £200 cannot buy the £2,800 training line.
- **It is the best access fallback, not the best participation plan.** If the week-1 check finds the ground floor is not independently accessible, the board may need an access-first route. But then the evening programme will not reach the estates without a bus, so the board should be explicit that it is choosing building access over estate participation.

**When it would become the right choice:** if the ground floor has no independent step-free route and the board prioritises restoring the building over the transport-linked evening pilot; or if a separate revenue source is found for the bus. In that case, Option B restores access, but it should not be described as an estate-participation plan.

## Option C — Round-1 two-barrier plan (lift + evening + bus)

**What it buys**

| Item | Quoted cost |
|---|---:|
| Lift repair and accessible entrance reinstatement (capital) | £27,500 |
| Evening opening, 13 weeks | £9,600 |
| Late bus subsidy, 13 weeks | £14,400 |
| **Total** | **£51,500** |
| **Over the £48,000 envelope** | **£3,500** |

**Why it is not chosen**

This is the round-1 recommendation with the new lift quote. It is the only combination that fixes both the building and the journey, but it **exceeds the fixed envelope by £3,500**. The lift cannot be phased, so a part-share would not buy a usable repair, and it cannot be charged to revenue without a council variation. The plan cannot assume a variation that has not been agreed.

It is also weaker on deliverability than it looks: the eight-week lead means the lift is usable only from week 9, so the quarter would spend £27,500 on a capital item that is usable for about five weeks. The pilot could run on the ground floor before then, but only under the 60 cap.

**When it would become the right choice:** if the council approves a **£3,500 capital variation** (or a separate capital contribution) and the board wants the access-complete version. That is a legitimate ask, but it is not the current plan, because the current envelope is fixed and the variation is not agreed. Option C is therefore the **contingent access-complete option**, not the recommendation.

## Option D — Transport-only (evening + bus)

**What it buys**

| Item | Quoted cost |
|---|---:|
| Evening opening, 13 weeks | £9,600 |
| Late bus subsidy, 13 weeks | £14,400 |
| **Total** | **£24,000** |
| **Uncommitted headroom** | **£24,000** |

**Why it is not chosen**

It is the minimum viable version of the pilot and the most resilient to a next-quarter cut. But it leaves the 40-name Youth Studio waiting list and the 48% awareness gap unaddressed, and it leaves half the envelope unspent. The new material gives strong, affordable reasons to add the youth expansion and the targeted mailer; a transport-only plan would be deliberately ignoring them.

**When it would become the right choice:** if the board wants maximum headroom for an uncertain next quarter, or if the mailer and youth expansion cannot be mobilised in time. It is a credible fallback, not the best use of this quarter's evidence.

## Option E — Comms-led (print + mailer + training)

**What it buys**

| Item | Quoted cost |
|---|---:|
| Print and poster campaign across the borough | £9,000 |
| Targeted mailer to priority-neighbourhood households | £4,200 |
| Staff training: welcome and access awareness, 2 days | £2,800 |
| **Total** | **£16,000** |

**Why it is not chosen**

The 2022 campaign spent the same £9,000 on the same broad scope and produced attendance within normal seasonal variation, no measurable first-time estate attendance, and word of mouth still dominant at 64%. The board's minute is explicit: *"Do not repeat the poster spend without a specific audience and a specific offer."* The mailer is more specific, but on its own it cannot get anyone home after 19:10. The new doorstep survey says 57% of aware residents name transport as the main barrier, and the equalities team says awareness alone has not been tested as a conversion route. This option buys awareness without the offer that makes it usable.

It is also cheap in a way that is misleading: it leaves most of the envelope unspent while claiming to address participation. It would fail the goal.

## Option F — Access-only (lift + training)

**What it buys**

| Item | Quoted cost |
|---|---:|
| Lift repair and accessible entrance reinstatement (capital) | £27,500 |
| Staff training: welcome and access awareness, 2 days | £2,800 |
| **Total** | **£30,300** |

**Why it is not chosen**

It restores the building's accessible route but buys no programme and no transport. The first floor has no costed programme to put on, and the estate audience still faces the 19:10 last bus. It is an access/compliance plan, not a participation plan. It also uses 63% of the envelope while leaving the centre's priority-neighbourhood goal untouched.

**When it would become the right choice:** only if the board's objective is building compliance this quarter and participation is explicitly deferred. That is not the brief.

## Option G — Status quo or training-only

**What it buys**

- £0 (continue current programme), or £2,800 for two days of welcome/access training.

**Why it is not chosen**

- The lift and accessible entrance remain out; first-floor public events remain prohibited; evening events over 60 remain unlicensed.
- Estate residents still face two bus changes and a 19:10 last bus; evening participation remains structurally limited.
- The council report still cannot show attendance or equalities reach for the priority neighbourhoods.
- Training alone may improve welcome, but it does not change who can get to the building or get home.

This is the safe option only if the board's real objective is to preserve the envelope, which is not the brief.

## Why Option A, in one paragraph

Option A is the only combination that keeps the one participation lever with a positive, priority-estate-specific result in the pack — the transport-linked evening programme — while also acting on the new evidence that half the estates have never heard of the centre and that the Youth Studio has a 40-name waiting list. It does this inside the fixed £48,000 envelope using only quoted revenue costs. The revised lift quote makes the round-1 three-line plan £51,500, so the lift cannot be included; the new 60-attendee condition allows a capped ground-floor evening programme to run lawfully without it. The plan therefore defers the lift to a separate capital bid, caps events at 60, and funds the youth expansion, the targeted mailer, and the access training with the room the lift would have taken. It is honest about the cost of that trade-off: the building's accessible route remains out, the first floor stays closed, and the 60 cap may truncate demand. If the ground floor is not independently accessible, or if the board can secure a £3,500 council variation, the decision changes — Option B or Option C becomes the right route. Under the current envelope, Option A is the best-evidenced compliant plan.
