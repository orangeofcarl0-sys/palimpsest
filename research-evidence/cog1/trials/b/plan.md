## 1. Plan at a glance

| Complementary funded work | **Youth Studio expansion £6,500** (up to 4 extra sessions/month, waiting list first); **targeted mailer £4,200** to Northside and Kiln Field households; **welcome/access training £2,800** |

The plan is deliberately focused. It buys the transport link that the 2023 pilot and the new estates survey identify as the dominant barrier, and it pairs that with the awareness, youth-supply, and welcome measures the new material makes affordable. It does **not** buy the building's accessible-entrance repair, because the revised £27,500 quote makes the three-line access plan £51,500 — £3,500 over the envelope. That is an explicit compromise, managed by the 60-attendee cap and the first-floor closure, not a claim that access is solved.

## 2. The problem this plan is solving

The centre is not short of satisfied visitors. It is short of a way to reach the people who are not in the room, and it is missing a basic physical access route that it cannot now afford alongside the transport fix.

- **Participation is concentrated.** Family Saturdays carries the volume (about 400–600 visits a month). Evening Talks are small (about 36–47 visits per session), and the audience that used to come from the two estates stopped when the 2023 minibus stopped.
- **The estates are transport-isolated in the evening.** Northside and Kiln Field are two bus changes away, the last regular bus is 19:10, and there is no staffed car park after 20:00. An evening event that ends after 19:00 is not reachable by regular bus.
- **Awareness is uneven.** The new doorstep survey of 120 estate households finds **48% have never heard of the centre**, 37% have heard but never attended, and only 15% have attended at least once. Among those who have heard, **57% name getting home after an evening event as the main barrier**, against 22% cost and 11% content. The two barriers are sequential: awareness first for half, transport first for the aware half.
- **The building's accessible entrance and lift are out of service.** First-floor public events are prohibited. The lift repair is now **£27,500** with an **eight-week lead**, and the new licensing condition requires the accessible entrance before **any evening event with more than 60 attendees** is licensed, whether or not it uses the first floor. The three-line plan (lift + evening + bus) is £51,500, £3,500 over the envelope.
- **The centre cannot currently say which programmes reach the priority estates.** There is no neighbourhood or equalities breakdown in the attendance data or the survey. The new doorstep survey is estate-level, not programme-level.
- **The 92% satisfaction figure is not a general licence to stop audience development.** It describes a self-selected, in-person, free-entry Family Saturday sample that was 71% car-borne and 78% with under-12s. It says nothing about non-visitors, evening audiences, disabled visitors, or the estates.
- **The Youth Studio is supply-constrained.** The partner's written clarification confirms sessions halved because its funding changed, not because interest fell. Unique participants rose from 71 to 115, and there is a **waiting list of 40**. It can supply up to four extra sessions a month for the same £6,500.

The plan therefore has four jobs: **get people to the building (transport), tell the right people about it (targeted awareness), use the youth waiting list (supply), and count who comes (equalities data)**. The building access fix is deferred, with the 60 cap and first-floor closure as the compliance boundary.

## 3. Theory of change

```
Late minibus + weekly evening programme (capped at 60)
        │
        ├── removes the 19:10 last-bus barrier
        ├── gives estate residents a reason and a way to attend
        └── produces evening attendance and estate share

Targeted mailer to Northside and Kiln Field households
        │
        ├── reaches the 48% who have never heard of the centre
        ├── leads with the specific evening + minibus offer
        └── tests whether targeted awareness adds reach

Youth Studio expansion (up to 4 extra sessions/month)
        │
        ├── uses the 40-name waiting list
        ├── restores supply without a new post
        └── raises youth participation and unique participants

Welcome and access training
        │
        ├── supports the 60 cap and access-needs recording
        └── improves welcome for new and disabled attendees

Postcode/estate + access-needs data
        │
        ├── answers the council's equalities requirement
        ├── tests whether the pilot reaches the priority estates
        └── gives the board a baseline for future decisions
```

**Assumptions that must hold**

- There is latent evening demand in the estates; the 2023 pilot and the new doorstep survey are the evidence, but the 2023 test is a single-site, 12-week autumn pilot.
- The rota can produce a named responsible person for 13 evenings within 1.5 FTE.
- The minibus operator can deliver the quoted route, times, capacity, and accessibility.
- The ground-floor main hall has an independent step-free route and accessible toilet; if not, the pilot does not start.
- The 60-attendee cap is enough to accommodate demand at the target of 50 visits per session; if it is not, the pilot records unmet demand rather than breaching the cap.
- The mailer reaches the intended households and the registration source data can distinguish it from other channels.
- The partner can supply staff for up to four extra Youth Studio sessions a month, and the centre can provide the room and materials.
- The five funded lines are revenue-eligible; the plan does not require a capital/revenue swap.

If any of these does not hold, the plan does not simply proceed. The decision gates in section 8 are there to stop a bad start.

## 4. Workstreams

### Workstream A — Transport-linked evening opening pilot (Thursday Lates)

**Owner:** Centre manager / pilot lead, with the front-of-house coordinator.
**Cost:** £24,000.
**Duration:** Weeks 1–13.

- Weekly evening event in the ground-floor main hall, using the existing Evening Talks format plus film/participatory content.
- Every event ends by 21:00.
- **Hard cap of 60 attendees per event** until the accessible entrance is operable. Count admissions, stop at 60, record turn-aways.
- Minibus: Northside–Kiln Field loop, 19:15 outward, 21:30 return, 13 weeks.
- Named responsible person on site every evening; existing staff extended, no new post.
- Registration captures postcode/estate, access needs, travel mode, source (including mailer code), and first-visit status.
- No first-floor public event this quarter.

The full design, gates, and risks are in `pilot.md`.

### Workstream B — Youth Studio expansion

**Owner:** Partner youth organisation, with the centre manager.
**Cost:** £6,500.
**Duration:** Weeks 1–13.

- The partner supplies staff for **up to four additional sessions a month** at the same quoted price; the centre supplies the room and materials.
- Priority goes to the **40-name waiting list**.
- Sessions are additional to the existing two a month; the aim is up to six a month in total, subject to room availability.
- If the room or materials can support only two extra sessions a month, deliver the originally quoted two and report the constraint; do not overspend.
- Report sessions delivered, visits per session, unique participants, and waitlist movement monthly.

### Workstream C — Targeted communications and mailer

**Owner:** Pilot lead, with the partner youth organisation and estate networks.
**Cost:** £4,200.
**Duration:** Weeks 1–2 for the mailer; outreach continues through the quarter.

- One mailer to **Northside and Kiln Field households**, leading with the specific offer: a weekly evening programme and the late minibus home.
- A response code or registration source so the pilot can see whether the mailer added reach.
- Partner/estate outreach from week 1; estate community groups, noticeboards, existing local networks.
- Existing email/social channels, word of mouth, and direct invitation to lapsed Evening Talks attendees where records exist.
- **No borough-wide print campaign.** The 2022 evidence and the board minute rule it out. The mailer is the specific-audience, specific-offer alternative, and it is still a test.

### Workstream D — Welcome and access training

**Owner:** Front-of-house coordinator, with the centre manager.
**Cost:** £2,800.
**Duration:** Pre-week 1 (two days).

- Two days of welcome and access awareness training for front-of-house staff and volunteers.
- Covers the 60-attendee cap, access-needs recording, step-free-route enquiries, and welcome for first-time and disabled visitors.
- 100% of front-of-house staff and volunteers trained before the first event.
- Actions logged and reviewed at week 6.
- Training does not replace the missing accessible entrance; it improves how the centre handles access within the current building.

### Workstream E — Measurement and equalities

**Owner:** Pilot lead, with front-of-house.
**Cost:** No new cost; staff time within existing roles.

- Registration form with postcode/estate, access needs (voluntary), travel mode, source (including mailer code), first visit, and consent for follow-up.
- Weekly dashboard: attendance, cap hits, estate share, bus boardings, mailer attribution, youth sessions, data completeness, incidents, spend.
- Short survey at weeks 6 and 13; separate reporting for estate residents, disabled attendees, and first-time attendees.
- Extend the postcode/estate field to the other programmes where feasible, so the centre begins to answer "which programmes reach the estates?" beyond the pilot.
- Quarterly council report with attendance and equalities monitoring for Northside and Kiln Field.
- Small-cell suppression (fewer than five) and a privacy notice before week 1.

### Parallel access track (not funded in this plan)

- The lift/accessible-entrance repair is a separate **£27,500 capital bid** with an **eight-week lead**.
- The board should pursue a council capital variation or a separate capital programme. If a route is approved and instructed in week 1, completion would be at end week 8 and public use from week 9 at the earliest.
- This plan does not rely on that bid, commits no first-floor activity, and caps evening events at 60 until the accessible entrance is operable.
- If the ground floor is not independently accessible, the pilot cannot run; the board must re-plan (see `options.md`).

## 8. Decision gates

### Gate 1 — before the first event (week 1)

**Proceed only if all of the following are true before the first event; the end-of-week-1 review then confirms they still hold:**

- A named responsible person is rostered for all 13 evenings, within the 1.5 FTE envelope.
- The operator contract confirms route, 19:15/21:30 times, capacity, accessibility, and standby arrangements.
- The ground-floor main hall has an independent step-free route and accessible toilet.
- The 60-attendee cap is built into ticketing/door registration, with a turn-away log.
- Registration, privacy notice, and baseline dashboard are ready.
- The mailer has been dispatched or has a confirmed dispatch date in weeks 1–2.
- The Youth Studio room and materials are confirmed for the additional sessions.
- The two-day training has been delivered or is scheduled before the first event.

**If not:** delay the pilot, do not sign the bus contract, and bring a revised start date to the board. A bus without a compliant event is not a pilot. If the ground floor is not accessible, do not run the pilot; re-plan.

### Gate 2 — end of week 6

**Review:**

- Evening attendance per session (green ≥50; amber 40–49; red <40 or <36.5).
- Estate share of new attendees (green ≥50%; amber 35–49%; red <35%).
- Bus use (green ≥50%; amber 30–49%; red <30%).
- Cap hits and turn-aways; any breach.
- Ground-floor access status; first-floor closure.
- Youth sessions delivered and waitlist movement.
- Mailer attribution and source mix.
- Data completeness, incidents, and spend.

**Action:** green — continue and prepare a continuation case; amber — continue with content/outreach changes, no extra spend; red — complete the committed quarter, do not recommend continuation. A cap hit is a demand signal, not automatically a red; a cap breach is a compliance failure. A lift capital-bid delay is reported separately; it does not change the participation thresholds.

### Gate 3 — week 13

**Decide:** continue, revise, or stop. The decision note states what happened, what it cost, whether the hypothesis held, what the limits of the evidence are, and what the board recommends for the next quarter. No carry-over; no assumption that next quarter's budget is unchanged. The separate lift capital bid is reported alongside, not inside, the pilot result.

## 10. Answering the board's open questions

The board asked five questions in `sources/open-questions.md`. The plan does not pretend they are all answered now; it says what is known and what will be known by week 13.

**1. "Our satisfaction is 92%. Does that mean we can stop worrying about audience development?"**

No. The 92% is real for 310 respondents at a free-entry Family Saturday weekend, but the survey was in-person and self-selected, 71% travelled by car, 78% attended with under-12s, and the marketing volunteer explicitly notes that non-visitors and weekday/evening audiences were not surveyed. The new doorstep survey reinforces the point: 48% of estate households have never heard of the centre, and only 15% have attended at least once. Audience development should continue, and this plan measures the audience the survey missed.

**2. "Youth Studio visits fell. Are young people losing interest?"**

No. The partner's written clarification (U3) confirms supply fell, not interest. Sessions fell from four a month to two from June 2024 because the partner's funding changed; monthly visits fell because there were half as many sessions, but visits per session rose from about 49–52 to about 59–76, monthly unique participants rose from 71 to 115, and there is now a waiting list of 40. The earlier interview speculation that young people had "moved on" is withdrawn. The plan funds the expansion at £6,500 for up to four extra sessions a month.

**3. "The 2023 evening pilot worked. Why did it stop working?"**

The transport stopped. When the minibus funding ended, the centre continued the evening programme for six weeks without it; attendance fell back to roughly pre-pilot levels and the new estate attendees stopped coming. The programme did not change; the journey did. That is why this plan funds the evening opening and the bus together, not the evening opening alone.

**4. "The council may cut us next quarter. Which parts of any plan are safe to stop?"**

The revenue lines are time-limited and stoppable: the bus subsidy (£14,400), the evening opening (£9,600), the youth expansion (£6,500), the mailer (£4,200, one-off), and the training (£2,800, one-off) all end at week 13, and no new post is created. The lift is not in this plan; it is a separate capital bid and a separate decision. But "safe to stop" is not the same as "no consequence": the 2023 evidence says that stopping the bus ends the estate reach, and stopping the evening opening removes the offer. A 15% cut is about £7,200. The current £37,500 revenue plan would fit inside a 15%-reduced £40,800 envelope if the quotes hold, with £3,300 headroom — better than the round-1 £46,000 plan, which would not. The lift would still not fit. The board should not pretend that a cut has no effect on the estate outcome, but this plan is more resilient than round 1.

**5. "Which of our programmes actually reaches the Northside and Kiln Field estates?"**

The pack cannot answer this today at programme level. There is no neighbourhood or equalities breakdown in the centre's attendance data or the survey. The new doorstep survey gives a household baseline — 48% never heard, 37% heard but never attended, 15% attended at least once — but it does not say which programme those 15% attended. The 2023 pilot says the majority of new evening attendees came from the two estates, but gives no count or denominator. This plan makes postcode/estate and access-needs collection part of pilot registration from week 1, extends the field to other programmes where feasible, and reports the breakdown to the council at week 13. That is the beginning of an answer, not a substitute for one.

## 11. What success looks like at week 13

**Green**

- Evening attendance ≥50 per session by week 6 and sustained.
- ≥50% of new evening attendees from Northside or Kiln Field.
- ≥50% of priority-estate attendees using the 21:30 return; no stranded attendees.
- 100% of evening events at or below 60; no first-floor public events; no licensing breach.
- Ground-floor step-free route and accessible toilet confirmed in week 1.
- Equalities data ≥95% complete; council report accepted.
- Youth Studio: up to four extra sessions a month delivered, waitlist reduced, ≥70 visits per session.
- Mailer dispatched; source data show whether it added new estate reach.
- Training delivered to all front-of-house staff and volunteers before the first event.
- No serious incidents; spend within the £48,000 envelope, with any use of the £10,500 headroom approved by the board.

**What the board can then say**

> The transport-linked evening model still works for the priority estates, we have begun to reach the half of the estates that had never heard of us, the Youth Studio waiting list is being used, and we now have a neighbourhood baseline. The building's accessible route is still not restored; that remains a separate capital decision.

**If the result is amber or red**

The report says so plainly. The committed quarter is completed unless safety or access requires a stop. The board does not rebrand a weak result as a success, and it does not repeat the 2023 mistake of keeping the evening programme after the transport stops and calling the subsequent decline a programme problem. Nor does it treat a 60-capped attendance figure as proof of demand: cap hits and turn-aways are reported.

## 12. What this plan deliberately does not do

- **No lift repair in this quarter.** The revised £27,500 quote plus the transport-linked pilot would be £51,500, £3,500 over the envelope; the lift cannot be phased, a part-share would not buy a usable repair, and capital cannot be swapped with revenue. The lift is a separate capital bid. The first floor stays closed and evening events are capped at 60.
- **No borough-wide print or poster campaign.** The 2022 campaign of the same scope produced no measurable change; the board minute says not to repeat it without a specific audience and a specific offer. The targeted mailer is the specific alternative.
- **No café refresh.** U4 defers it to the next financial year and it is no longer available this quarter; it is also unrelated to programming.
- **No new post.** The evening opening assumes existing staff are extended; the youth partner supplies its own staff; the mailer and training are bought services.
- **No first-floor public event.** The constraint is not negotiable, and there is no costed first-floor programme.
- **No virement between capital and revenue.** A council variation is required if the lift is to be funded; this plan does not assume one.
- **No promise about next quarter.** The council's possible 15% reduction is verbal and unconfirmed; the plan is built to stop cleanly, not to assume continuation.
- **No claim that the access problem is solved.** The plan is explicit that the building's accessible entrance remains out.

## 13. If the next quarter is cut by 15%

A 15% reduction is about **£7,200** of the £48,000 envelope, leaving **£40,800**. It is not confirmed, and no figure is agreed. The plan does not pre-commit next quarter, but the board should see the shape of the problem now:

- The current revenue plan is **£37,500**. If the same quotes hold, it would fit inside a £40,800 envelope with **£3,300** headroom. That is a material improvement on the round-1 £46,000 plan, which would have been over a reduced envelope.
- The **bus subsidy is £14,400** — twice the size of a 15% cut. It cannot be absorbed by trimming; it would have to stop, be reduced in frequency under a new quote, or be funded from another source.
- The **evening opening is £9,600** — also larger than the cut. Reducing it would require a new quote; the current quote is for 13 weeks.
- The **youth expansion is £6,500** — just under the £7,200 cut. It is the most likely casualty if the board tries to absorb a cut without touching transport, but losing it would leave the 40-name waiting list unaddressed.
- The **mailer and training are one-off** (£4,200 and £2,800), so they are not recurring pressures, but they are also not a source of recurring savings.
- The **lift is not in this plan**. A separate £27,500 capital bid would not fit inside a £40,800 revenue envelope and would need its own capital route.

The honest position: this plan is designed so the board can stop the revenue lines without a stranded post or a capital liability. It is **more resilient** to a 15% cut than round 1 because it no longer carries the lift, but it is **not** designed to deliver the same estate reach on a much smaller budget. If the cut is confirmed, the board should decide explicitly whether to prioritise transport and accept a smaller evening programme, or to end the evening model and preserve the youth/awareness work. The 2023 evidence says the estate audience will not hold without the bus.
