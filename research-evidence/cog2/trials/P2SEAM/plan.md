## 1. Summary

This is a portfolio plan, not a single bet. It protects the session that is working, tests the transport explanation on the one evening when transport has changed, uses the east hall only after it is certified, and keeps a small, honest test of the one slot on which the trust has no local evidence.

1. **Protect the Saturday family workshop.** It is the only session that grew in 2025 (+21%). It runs every Saturday. Family-fund childcare is requested for 20 of the 26 Saturdays, within the reduced £4,000 fund.
2. **Test transport on Fridays.** The town council's late bus starts on Friday 6 February and leaves the square at 21:15. The Friday series ends at 20:30, leaving 45 minutes to reach it. This is a staged test with a stop rule, not an unconditional six-month commitment.
3. **Use the east hall after the certificate.** There is no public event in the east hall before the commissioning certificate. A soft opening follows the certificate, then a four-week textile exhibition from 11 April, subject to two weeks of environmental monitoring and curator sign-off.
4. **Test Sunday afternoon small.** Three salons, with a stop rule, because 12% of survey respondents prefer Sunday afternoon and the trust has no local evidence either way.
5. **Hold a reserve.** The plan commits £55,715, including a 10% contingency, and leaves £4,285 as a board reserve within the £60,000 ceiling.

## 2. What the evidence says

### 2.1 Attendance: a modest overall decline, but a clear mix shift

The attendance CSV is the only continuous quantitative series in the pack. It shows a 3.2% fall in total attendances in 2025 (1,030 to 997), but the fall is not uniform.

| Session | 2024 average per month | 2025 average per month | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Tuesday Craft Group | 33.9 | 29.2 | −14% |
| Thursday Film Club | 20.7 | 16.2 | −22% |
| Saturday Family Workshop | 31.2 | 37.8 | +21% |
| **All sessions** | **85.8** | **83.1** | **−3.2%** |

Two further points matter:

- **The decline is concentrated in the second half of 2025.** January–June 2025 totalled 523 attendances against 515 in the same months of 2024 (+1.6%). July–December 2025 totalled 474 against 515 (−8.0%).
- **The family workshop grew when childcare was added.** Its 2024 average was 26.9 in January–August and 42.3 in October–December, after the childcare pilot began. The 2025 January–June average was 39.7.

The implication is not that the whole programme is failing. It is that the weekday and evening offer is losing people while the Saturday family offer is working. The plan therefore protects the family workshop and treats the weekday and evening offer as the part that needs testing.

### 2.2 The survey: transport is the strongest signal, but it is a stated preference

The 2025 member survey had 240 responses from 610 members (39%). It was self-selected, and the survey volunteer's method note is explicit: the evening-transport figure is a stated preference; people were asked to imagine a bus, not tested on whether they would come, and were not asked what they would pay.

| Survey question | Result |
|---|---|
| What stops you attending more often? (up to two) | Transport 61%; programme does not appeal 34%; cost 29%; caring responsibilities 27%; other 11% |
| Would you attend an evening event if transport were available? | Yes 44%; maybe 21%; no 35% |
| Which time suits you best? | Saturday morning 38%; weekday morning 24%; weekday evening 18%; Sunday afternoon 12%; no preference 8% |
| Would free childcare make it easier to attend? | Yes 27%, of whom 81% are members with children under 12 |

Transport is the strongest signal, but 35% say they would not come even with transport, and the 44% is not a forecast. The time-preference data is more useful for scheduling: Saturday morning is the strongest preference, weekday morning is next, and Sunday afternoon is the weakest. That is why the plan is weighted to Saturday morning and daytime, and why the Sunday test is deliberately small.

The survey also explains why the plan does not put money into a digital campaign. Cost (29%) and caring responsibilities (27%) are addressed through pay-what-you-can pricing, free exhibition entry and the childcare offer, not through a separate subsidy line.

### 2.3 Prior interventions: remove friction, don't just change content

- **2023 Friday evening film pilot.** Three screenings drew 37, 23 and 19 people. Booking data shows 60 seats were reserved for the first night and 48 for the second, so attendance was 62% and 48% of reservations. The series was cancelled. The programmer's note says people liked the films but could not get home; the staff interviews corroborate the 18:40 last bus and a £14 return taxi. The mean attendance was 26.3, and the fall from first to third night was 49%. This is a strong hypothesis, not proof: only three nights, no written evaluation, the room is not recorded, and there is no ticket income data.
- **2024 Saturday childcare pilot.** Attendance rose from an average of 27 in the first eight months of 2024 to 42 in the last four. The family fund paid £180 per session. Staff report that families stayed for the café and some began attending the Tuesday Craft Group. This is the clearest evidence in the pack that removing a practical barrier works.
- **2024 website and ticketing migration.** Web traffic rose 12%, but there is no evidence that this changed attendance; the decline continued through 2025. Digital spend is therefore not treated as a solution.
- **2022 outreach van.** It averaged 11 attendances per visit, was withdrawn when its grant ended, and left no written evaluation. Staff remember it as "worthwhile but not a substitute for the building". It is not a model to scale.

### 2.4 Building, staffing and money are hard boundaries

- The east hall has not been climate-controlled since the HVAC unit failed in January 2025. The 2019 "climate-controlled" description is not relied on. The west hall and workshop room are usable throughout; the basement is damp and unsuitable for sensitive material.
- The pack contains no temperature or humidity readings for the east hall, before or after repair; no blackout data for either hall; and no floor-loading, rigging or fire-safety data. The 120 seated / 180 standing figures are audience capacities, not load ratings. Heavy or suspended work needs a building officer's check.
- The trust has 2.0 FTE permanent staff (director, programmer), 0.5 FTE administrator and eight volunteers. Evening events require two staff present; childcare requires two qualified workers; volunteers are not asked to work alone after 18:00.
- Events may run until 23:00, and the building must be empty by 23:30.
- The programming budget covers artists, materials, marketing, bought-in staff hours and event costs. Building repairs are funded separately. The family fund is separate and capped at £4,000 for this period.

The plan is built around these boundaries: no east hall public events before the certificate; no new Thursday evening series; Friday events end at 20:30; sessional staff cover evenings; no new permanent posts.

The east hall is not used for public events until the commissioning certificate is issued.

### Pricing and access

Pricing is part of access. The soft opening, the exhibition and the Open House are free, with donations welcome. Friday films and talks and the Sunday salons use pay-what-you-can pricing, with a suggested £6 for films and £8 for talks; no one is turned away for inability to pay. The Family Workshop continues on its current terms apart from the reduced childcare offer, with childcare free at the point of use on the sessions the family fund supports.

Friday bookings are recorded, and the box office records reservations, paid tickets and actual attendance separately, so the 2023 no-show rates can be compared with a priced and transport-backed series. The budget does not assume ticket income; any income reduces the net cost to the trust.

## 5. Rationale

### 5.1 Evidence I relied on

I treated the attendance CSV as the primary quantitative evidence because it is the only continuous series in the pack. It shows a 3.2% fall in total attendances in 2025, but the fall is not uniform: January–June 2025 was 1.6% above the same months of 2024, while July–December was 8.0% below; Craft Group fell 14%, the Film Club fell 22%, and the Saturday Family Workshop grew 21%. That is why the plan protects the Family Workshop and treats the weekday and evening offer as the part that needs testing, rather than assuming a general collapse.

I used the 2025 member survey as a signal, not a forecast. Transport is the most cited barrier at 61%, but the 44% who said they would attend an evening event if transport were available were answering a hypothetical question; the survey volunteer explicitly warns that the trust did not test whether they would actually come or what they would pay. I therefore did not plan on 44% turning up. I used the time-preference data — Saturday morning 38%, weekday morning 24%, weekday evening 18%, Sunday afternoon 12% — to weight the programme toward Saturday morning and daytime, and to keep the Sunday test small. Cost (29%) and caring responsibilities (27%) are addressed through pay-what-you-can pricing, free exhibition entry and the childcare offer, rather than through a separate subsidy line.

I used the prior interventions as natural experiments. The 2023 Friday films drew 37, 23 and 19 people, with 60 and 48 seats reserved, and were cancelled; the programmer's note and the staff interviews point to the journey home, with the 18:40 last bus and the £14 return taxi. That is a strong hypothesis, not proof, so the Friday series is a test with a stop rule. The 2024 childcare pilot, where the Family Workshop average rose from 27 to 42, is the clearest evidence that removing a practical barrier works; it is why childcare remains central to the family offer, within the reduced fund. The 2024 website migration produced a 12% traffic increase but no evidence of an attendance effect, which is why no programme money is going into digital. The 2022 van averaged 11 attendances per visit and left no evaluation, which is why the plan does not scale outreach; the small minibus line brings groups to the building instead.

I used the staff interviews and the constraints as corroboration and as hard boundaries. JW and RS describe the 18:40 last bus, the £14 taxi and the failed 2023 minibus funding; AK describes the same barrier from a member's point of view; MD describes the childcare effect and the £180 session cost; PB describes the HVAC failure, the £18,000 quote and the 1 March expectation; LD describes the ageing craft group, the falling Film Club and the absence of Sunday data. The plan is built around those boundaries, not against them.

I checked the eight project journal entries. Two earlier notes were stale and are corrected here: the 2019 "east hall is climate-controlled" note (false since January 2025) and the "members want evening events" note (which reads the 44% "yes" without the 35% "no" or the stated-preference caveat). The journal's warning that the pack has no structural or floor-load data is also reflected: the 120/180 figures are audience capacities, not load ratings, and no heavy or suspended work is programmed without a building officer's check.

I used the 12 January 2026 updates over the seed pack wherever they conflict. The family fund is £4,000, not £9,000; the east hall repair is approved and scheduled, not merely an application; the Friday late bus exists, where the seed pack described only an 18:40 last service; the website update has no programme effect.

### 5.2 How the January updates changed my assumptions

The updates did not change the diagnosis; they changed what is testable and what is affordable.

**Family fund.** Under the seed pack's £9,000, weekly childcare — 26 sessions at £180, or £4,680 — looked affordable. The update sets the fund at £4,000 and shows that even the full fund covers only 22 sessions (£3,960). I have planned 20 sessions (£3,600), held £400 for other family support, and built in six Saturdays without childcare. The family offer therefore changes from "childcare every week" to "childcare on 20 of 26 Saturdays, with the workshop still running every week". From April, the wording changes to "supported by the family fund". This is a material change: the Family Workshop remains the core, but childcare is now a limited enhancement rather than a weekly guarantee.

**East hall repair.** I had treated the east hall as uncertain, with no firm date for sensitive use. The update confirms the grant, the works dates (9–27 February), commissioning on 28 February and expected usability from 1 March. But the building officer says this is a plan, not a guarantee, and nothing should be booked before the commissioning certificate. I therefore did not schedule any east hall public events in January or February. I put the certificate check on 2 March, a soft opening on 14 March and the sensitive textile exhibition on 11 April, with two weeks of environmental monitoring and a curator sign-off before any textile is installed. The east hall changes from "maybe" to "planned, but gated".

**Transport.** I had assumed there was no viable new evening series, because the 2023 pilot ran with an 18:40 last bus. The update adds a Friday late bus from 6 February, last service 21:15, Fridays only, for six months, with a review in July and a ridership check after three months. I changed the plan for a new evening series from "not viable" to a Friday series ending at 20:30, and kept new Thursday evenings off. I did not extend the assumption to other weekdays, and I did not make core sessions depend on the bus. I added a 30 April data pack to feed the council's three-month review. The Friday series is therefore both a programme and an evidence-gathering exercise.

The round-1 journal note recommended a 2–3 event test. The bus update changes the sample: the council will review ridership after three months, and 2–3 nights would not give it or the trust a reliable read. I have therefore staged the series — 13 events to 8 May, then the full June programme only if the 8 May review clears the stop rule; otherwise June reduces to monthly. That is a longer test than the journal proposed, but it is conditional, and the core does not depend on it.

**Website.** The update says the migration is complete, online booking works and there is no programme effect. I have not allocated programme money to the website or a digital campaign, and I have not assumed that the 2024 traffic increase will translate into attendance.

### 5.4 Why this mix, not a single bet

The board has three explanations and no decision. The evidence does not justify betting the whole £60,000 on one of them. The plan weights the budget toward what is already working (the core programme, including the Family Workshop, £18,900), a controlled transport test (Friday, £10,200), a signature programming season (east hall, £10,500, plus the Open House and commissions), and a small Sunday test (£1,260). Access transport, marketing and evaluation make up the rest.

The core is funded by the trust's programming budget, not a single external funder. The family fund only adds childcare; the bus is transport, not programme funding; the east hall repair is capital, not programming. If the Friday test fails, the core still runs. If the family fund is cut, the workshop still runs. If the east hall certificate slips, the west hall programme still runs. If the bus ends, the Friday films can move to an early Friday slot that finishes before the 18:40 bus, or to a Saturday afternoon after a blackout check. That is the point of the portfolio: it gives the board evidence without putting the core programme at risk.

I considered moving the Thursday Film Club to a daytime matinee, to reach the 24% who prefer weekday morning. I did not do so, for three reasons: the pack gives no evidence of a daytime film audience; blackout is not recorded in either hall, and a daytime screening needs it; and the existing club has a local evening audience that a move could lose. The daytime gap is tested instead by the Sunday salons, which need no blackout, and by the existing Tuesday and Saturday morning sessions. If the board wants a weekday-morning film test, it can be added from the reserve after a blackout check.
