# Harrowgate Community Trust — Programme Plan, January–June 2026

**Prepared for:** the board
**Programming budget:** £60,000 (artists, materials, bought-in staff hours, marketing, event costs)
**Excluded from this budget:** building repairs (east hall HVAC, capital grant) and the family fund (capped separately at £9,000)

---

## 1. Summary

The plan keeps the three existing sessions running, adds two tests of the explanations the board has not chosen between, and puts the east hall to work from the moment it is genuinely usable. It spends **£59,680** of the £60,000, leaves £320 unallocated, and holds £5,000 in contingency.

Three things drive the design:

- **The decline is real in every strand, including the one that was growing.** In 2025 the craft group averaged 29.2 (down from 33.9), the film club 16.2 (down from 20.7) and the family workshop 37.8 (up from 31.2) — but the family workshop peaked at 44 in November 2024 and has drifted down since: December 2025 was 39 against 42 a year earlier. The 2024 childcare gain was real; it is now eroding.
- **The board has three competing explanations and no test that separates them.** So the plan buys evidence as well as activity: an evening season with transport actually provided (the one condition the 2023 pilot never met), and a Sunday afternoon series in the slot the trust has never tried.
- **The east hall cannot be used as the 2019 survey describes.** The HVAC failed in January 2025. Nothing sensitive goes in until the repair is commissioned *and* has been monitored.

## 5. Rationale: what I relied on, and what I am uncertain about

### The evidence I relied on

**The attendance record (`attendance-2024-2025.csv`) is the strongest evidence in the pack,** because it is the only source that measures what people did rather than what they say.

| Strand | 2024 average | 2025 average | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Tuesday craft group | 33.9 | 29.2 | −14% |
| Thursday film club | 20.7 | 16.2 | −22% |
| Saturday family workshop | 31.2 | 37.8 | +21% |

Three things follow. First, the decline is not confined to one badly programmed strand — it runs through all three. Second, the family workshop's gain is genuine: comparing like with like (excluding the August trough, which appears in both years) the pre-childcare level was 27.9 and the post-childcare level 40.0, a step of about twelve people rather than the thirteen to fifteen the prior notes claim. Third, that gain is eroding — 43 in January 2025 down to 39 in December, and every strand finished 2025 below its December 2024 figure (craft 30 v 35, film 17 v 22, family 39 v 42). Overall attendances fell only 3% (1,030 to 997) because the family growth masked the other two. That mask is now coming off.

**The 2025 member survey (`member-survey-2025.md`)** explains why we test rather than assume. Transport is the most-cited barrier (61%), then programme (34%), cost (29%) and caring responsibilities (27%). Saturday morning is the preferred slot (38%) and Sunday afternoon the least favoured (12%). That is enough to justify a Saturday-based family strand and a cheap Sunday test, but not enough to justify a large evening bet on its own.

**The staff interviews (`staff-interviews.md`)** supply the mechanism and the prices: the last scheduled bus leaves the square at 18:40, a taxi is about £14 return, the 2023 minibus quote was £240 a night and the pilot ran without one when the driver funding failed. They also supply the two facts that overrule the prior notes — the HVAC failed in January 2025, and the east hall is usable from 1 March only if the capital grant is approved and the unit is commissioned.

**The 2023 pilot (`prior-interventions.md`)** is consistent with the transport explanation but does not prove it: 60 seats were reserved for the first night and 37 people came; 48 were reserved for the second and 23 came. A no-show rate rising from 38% to 52% fits people discovering they could not get home. It equally fits a weak second film. Three nights with no transport and no price test cannot settle it, which is why the plan repeats the test with the missing ingredient present.

**Room capacities, costs and staffing rules (`spaces-and-costs.md`, `constraints.md`)** set the shape rather than the content: the craft group's recorded 29–30 does not fit the workshop room's 24, the west hall's 60 suits a film audience of 16–24, and the east hall is the only room that can hold a festival. Every evening is staffed by two bought-in people, volunteers are never left alone after 18:00, and the building is clear well before 23:30. The family fund is treated as optional throughout — which is also how the plan meets the board's wish not to depend on a single funder for core sessions.

**I treated `PRIOR-NOTES.md` as claims to be checked, not facts.** Note 3 (east hall climate-controlled) is out of date and would have put sensitive work at risk. Note 4 (members want evening events) overreads the survey: 44% said yes, 21% maybe and 35% no, among 240 self-selected members, and the survey volunteer explicitly warns the figure is a stated preference with no price test. Note 1 is directionally right and the plan follows its condition — no evening opening without transport. Note 2 is right in direction, overstated in size. Note 5 (website traffic) shows no attendance effect and is not relied on.

I did **not** fund a repeat of the 2022 outreach van: it reached 11 people a visit, no evaluation survives, and staff remember it as "not a substitute for the building".

### What I am uncertain about

1. **The cause of the decline is still unidentified.** The plan is a set of tests, not a bet. If transport is not the reason, the main loss is the minibus line (£4,560), and the 27 February stop rule caps it at about £1,440.
2. **The survey cannot see the people who left.** It is members only, 39% response, self-selected. Lapsed attenders — the group the plan most needs to reach — are absent from it by construction.
3. **Stated preference is not behaviour.** "Would you come if transport were available?" was asked without a price. The 44% may overstate real uptake, which is exactly what the minibus trial measures.
4. **The attendance file does not record frequency.** It gives one figure per strand per month and does not say whether each strand ran weekly or monthly. I have used the figures as indicative per-session levels and set the cadence explicitly in the schedule; the cost of the plan depends on that choice, which is mine, not the pack's.
5. **The east hall date is contingent.** 1 March depends on a capital grant not yet approved and a repair not yet let. If commissioning slips, the exhibition moves to the west hall with five artists and no sensitive works, and the festival stays in the west hall and café. Even on time, nothing sensitive is hung until four to six weeks of logger data confirm the hall holds stable conditions — the 2019 survey is not evidence about 2026.
6. **Costs may have moved.** The pack's figures are described as typical; the minibus quote dates from 2023. The £5,000 contingency is sized for that.
7. **The childcare money is not committed.** Trustees have not decided whether the family fund can be used every Saturday. The core family session does not depend on it, which also satisfies the board's wish not to depend on a single funder for core sessions.
8. **Six months cannot separate a trend from a season.** August is the trough in both years and the plan does not run into August, so the strongest seasonality effect is outside the window — but a spring-only comparison will still be noisy, and a single term is a small sample.
9. **There is no revenue data in the pack.** The plan therefore forecasts attendance and behaviour, not income; the café and ticket income should be reported alongside it but cannot be projected from what we have.
10. **Some figures are my estimates, not the pack's:** exhibition artist fees (using the per-session rate as a proxy), display materials, the opening event, talk refreshments, Sunday staffing and evaluation. They are marked † and are the lines most likely to move.
11. **The craft group is costed as volunteer-run.** The pack does not say who leads it. If it is in fact a paid leader, that is £6,500 (26 × £250) not currently in the budget, and the choice would be between using the contingency and dropping the guest-artist line.

## 6. How success will be judged

The board has not chosen a measure (open question 6), so this is a proposal for confirmation on 30 January. Whatever is chosen, the data collection is the same; only the headline changes.

**Primary:** average attendance per session for each strand, against its 2025 average — craft 29.2, film 16.2, family 37.8. Success is arresting the decline, not restoring 2024 in one term.

**Secondary:**

- share of evening attenders arriving by minibus (target 25% or more) and the no-show rate against the 2023 pilot's 38–52%;
- Sunday series at 25 or more per event, talks at 30 or more;
- exhibition at 600 visits across the 30 open days (about 25–30 a day); festival at 400 attendances;
- net change in membership against the 610 baseline;
- café takings on event days, which the pack does not currently record and which the plan would start recording in January.

A single number — "did more people come, and did they come from outside the current membership" — is the one I would put in front of the board in June.
