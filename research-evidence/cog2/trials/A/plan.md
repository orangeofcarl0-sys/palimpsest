The central judgement is that the decline is **not uniform**. The Saturday family workshop grew strongly after childcare was introduced and remains the strongest session; the Tuesday craft group and Thursday film club are the sessions that have fallen. The plan therefore protects and stabilises the family strand, refreshes craft and film rather than replacing them, brings the east hall back into use only when the building evidence supports it, and tests the two propositions for which there is no reliable local evidence — a Sunday afternoon series and a transport-enabled evening series — at a small enough scale to stop if they do not work.

---

## 2. Programme logic

The programme is built around five rules:

1. **Protect the one growing strand.** The Saturday family workshop is the only recorded session that grew between 2024 and 2025. It stays weekly, with childcare available every Saturday from the separate family fund, subject to the trustees' approval.
2. **Refresh declining core sessions rather than abandon them.** The craft group keeps its loyal Tuesday morning slot but gains a monthly guest maker. The film club is planned twice-monthly (second and fourth Thursdays), with a curated season and guest introductions, so that marketing and audience effort are concentrated.
3. **Use the east hall only when it is genuinely available.** The 2019 "climate-controlled" description is no longer true. East hall activity starts after written commissioning of the repaired HVAC, and no sensitive textiles or paper are displayed.
4. **Test uncertain demand at small scale.** The evening and Sunday offers are pilots with booking thresholds and review points, not a six-month commitment.
5. **Keep core sessions independent of a single external funder.** All core sessions are funded from the £60,000 programming budget. The family fund is used only to add childcare, so the family workshop still runs if that fund is not approved.

## 8. Rationale: evidence, judgements and uncertainty

### 8.1 What the attendance data actually show

The attendance file gives one figure per session per month — in effect, a per-session average, consistent with the prior notes' use of "average attendance" — for three sessions across 24 months. It does **not** show a uniform collapse:

- Across the 36 month-session records in each year (72 records in total), average attendance fell from **28.6 in 2024 to 27.7 in 2025**, a fall of about **3%**.
- In **January–June 2025** the average was **29.1**, slightly **above** the January–June 2024 figure of 28.6 (about +2%). The fall is concentrated in **July–December 2025**, when the average was 26.3 against 28.6 in the same months of 2024 (about −8%).
- The Saturday family workshop rose from an annual average of **31.3 in 2024 to 37.8 in 2025**; in January–June it rose from **28.5 to 39.7** (about +39%).
- The Tuesday craft group fell from **33.9 to 29.2** (about −14%); in January–June it fell from **35.5 to 30.5** (about −14%).
- The Thursday film club fell from **20.7 to 16.2** (about −22%); in January–June it fell from **21.8 to 17.0** (about −22%).
- August is a trough in both years (54 and 56 on the three-session index), so the plan does not treat the summer dip as evidence of a new crisis.

This matters because the board's brief refers to "a decline in attendance" without distinguishing between sessions. The data point to a **mix problem**: the family session is working, while craft and film are losing people. A plan that cut everything and started again would risk the one growing session; a plan that did nothing would let the declining sessions continue to shrink. The proposed mix is deliberately selective.

The data do not settle the board's three explanations. Transport is a credible barrier, especially for evening attendance (61% of survey respondents named it). Programming is the more likely explanation for the craft and film declines, because the family session grew in the same building and period, and 34% of respondents said the programme did not appeal. Changed post-pandemic habits may explain the weaker second half of 2025 and the summer trough, but the data cannot separate that from seasonality. The plan therefore acts on all three where it can: transport for the evening pilot, programming refresh for craft and film, and new daytime formats for changed habits.

### 8.2 What we relied on for each choice

- **Family Saturdays (weekly, with childcare from the separate family fund).** The strongest evidence in the pack is the 2024 childcare pilot. Saturday attendance averaged **26.9** in January–August 2024 and **40.0** in September–December 2024, after childcare began. The 2025 average was **37.8**, still well above the pre-childcare level, although it drifted from 43 in January to 39 in December. Staff interview MD says families came for the workshop, stayed for the café, and some later joined the craft group. The survey's most popular time is Saturday morning (38%), and 27% cite caring responsibilities, which supports keeping the family session on Saturday and offering childcare. The plan uses the attendance file's 40.0 rather than the "42" in `prior-interventions.md`; `PRIOR-NOTES.md` says "about 40", which matches the file. The difference does not change the direction of the finding, but it is a reminder that the prior notes are summaries, not raw data.
- **Childcare is an enhancement, not the core.** The family fund is separate, capped at £9,000 and not committed by trustees. The plan funds the workshop itself from the £60,000 and asks the family fund for all 26 sessions (£4,680), which is within the cap. The trustees may approve a smaller 15-session tranche (£2,700); in that case the family target is revised to 35. If the fund is unavailable, the workshop still runs.
- **Craft Tuesdays (weekly in this plan, with a guest maker).** The group is loyal but ageing (LD interview) and attendance has fallen by about 14%. Ending it would lose the remaining core members and the social function described by staff. A monthly guest maker is a low-risk way to refresh the programme without changing the regular habit.
- **Film Thursdays (twice-monthly, curated).** Film attendance has fallen by about 22% and a weekly format would cost about £490 per screening in licence, technician and evening staffing at an average audience of 17. That is poor value. Moving to 12 twice-monthly screenings with four guest introductions concentrates marketing, reduces cost per screening, and tests whether curation can restore attendance to the 2024 level. If the current pattern is weekly, this deliberately reduces the number of film screenings and therefore total film attendances; the weekly slot is not filling, and the saved budget supports the family, Sunday and outreach strands. Film success is judged on average attendance per screening, not on preserving a low-attendance weekly habit. The film club remains in its existing Thursday evening slot, so it does not create a new transport problem.
- **East hall from March, conditionally.** `PRIOR-NOTES.md` says the east hall is climate-controlled, citing a 2019 survey. That is no longer true: building officer PB says the HVAC unit failed in January 2025, the repair quote is £18,000, and the hall cannot be used for stable temperature or humidity until the repair is complete and commissioned. The capital application is with the council, with repair scheduled for late February 2026 and use from 1 March **if approved**. The plan therefore schedules the first east hall event on 22 March, requires written commissioning by 27 February, restricts the hall to ordinary performance/talk use, and excludes sensitive textiles and paper from the May exhibition. This directly answers open question 3 and avoids relying on the stale 2019 note.
- **Evening pilot, not a full evening series.** The 2023 Friday film pilot fell from 37 to 23 to 19. Staff and the programmer's note attribute the failure to transport: the last scheduled minibus leaves the square at 18:40, and the pilot had no transport arrangement. But the booking data also shows 60 seats reserved for the first night and 48 for the second, with only 37 and 23 attending — show rates of about 62% and 48%. That suggests demand may be weaker than reservations imply, not only transport-constrained. The 2025 survey's 44% "yes if transport were available" is a stated preference; the survey volunteer explicitly notes that it was hypothetical, did not test actual attendance and did not ask about price. The plan therefore runs six transport-enabled evenings (four film nights and two talks) with a minibus, a booking threshold and an exit survey, rather than committing to a weekly evening series. It uses the existing Thursday slot rather than reviving the failed Friday format, so it does not repeat the 2023 arrangement. This answers open question 2 without pretending the 2023 pilot settled it.
- **Sunday Sessions.** There is no local evidence (LD: "We have not tried a Sunday afternoon series. We have no data on it"). The survey's Sunday afternoon preference is only 12%, the lowest of the time options, but Sunday daytime avoids the evening transport barrier and uses the east hall once available. Four low-cost events are a proportionate test. This answers open question 5.
- **Community tasters.** The 2022 outreach van averaged 11 attendances per visit over six months and was withdrawn when its grant ended; no written evaluation survives, and staff remember it as "worthwhile but not a substitute for the building". That is weak evidence. The plan does not restore a van. It runs six taster workshops at partner venues, at lower cost, to test whether bringing activity closer to estates improves reach. This is a test, not a core commitment.
- **Marketing.** The website migration in March 2024 raised web traffic by 12%, but `prior-interventions.md` explicitly says there is no evidence that this changed attendance, and the decline continued through 2025. The plan therefore does not rely on digital traffic as a success measure. It uses the standard £600 per month for local print and social, plus a targeted launch and access campaign, with source codes so that channels can be compared.
- **Access fund.** Cost is a barrier for 29% of survey respondents and transport for 61%. The £2,300 access fund provides subsidised tickets and taxi/minibus support where the scheduled minibus does not fit. It is a small, flexible complement to the transport pilot, not a substitute for it.
- **No single-funder dependency.** Core sessions are funded entirely from the £60,000 programming budget. The family fund is additive and discretionary. No external grant is assumed for any core session. If the family fund is withdrawn, the family workshop continues without childcare; if the programme budget is reduced, the plan's priority order is family Saturdays, craft, film, then the pilots and exhibition.

The six open questions in the pack are addressed as follows: the decline is a mix problem rather than a single cause (8.1); the evening series is a transport-enabled test, not a repeat of 2023 (8.2); the east hall is used only after commissioning (8.2); childcare is requested every week within the cap, with a 15-session fallback (5.3); the Sunday series is a four-event test (8.2); and success is measured through the basket in section 7.

### 8.3 What we are uncertain about

- **HVAC approval and commissioning.** This is the largest delivery risk. The repair is a capital application, not a confirmed programme input. The 27 February gate and the west hall fallback are designed to prevent a last-minute cancellation. If commissioning slips beyond May, the exhibition should be deferred rather than installed in a hall that cannot hold the conditions.
- **Actual demand for evening and Sunday events.** The survey is a self-selected sample of members (240 of 610, 39% response), not the general public. It measures stated preference, not willingness to pay or actual attendance. The 2023 pilot's show-rate decline is a warning. The pilots are intentionally small and have stop/reallocate rules.
- **Attendance data limitations.** The file has only three sessions, one figure per month, and no raw session counts, ticket income or demographic data. The averages are a reasonable basis for direction, not a precise forecast. The targets in section 7 are indicative and should be revised after the April review if the baseline proves unreliable.
- **Session frequency.** The file does not record how many times each session ran in a month. The plan assumes the family and craft sessions run weekly and that the film club currently runs weekly, which is consistent with the prior notes' description of regular sessions and with the per-session costs in the pack. If the current frequency is lower, the number of paid sessions should be re-phased; the unit costs remain valid.
- **Family fund decision.** The trustees have not committed the fund to every Saturday. The plan requests all 26 sessions (£4,680), within the £9,000 cap. If trustees approve only a 15-session tranche (£2,700), the first 15 Saturdays are prioritised and the family target falls to 35; if they approve none, the workshop continues without childcare. There is £4,320 of family fund headroom at the full request.
- **Childcare effect.** The 2024 comparison is observational: attendance rose after childcare began, but autumn term, Christmas and other changes may also have contributed. The plan treats childcare as the best-supported lever, not a guaranteed cause. The April review will compare childcare and non-childcare Saturdays where both exist, and track take-up against attendance.
- **Costs.** The unit costs are 2025 typical costs. Artist fees, materials and marketing may rise, and the exhibition has estimated display and private-view costs. The £4,000 contingency (6.7% of the budget) is the main buffer. If costs rise by more than about 5%, the exhibition and the extra marketing are the first items to reduce.
- **Staff and volunteer capacity.** The trust has 2.0 FTE, 0.5 FTE administration and 8 volunteers. May is particularly busy, with the exhibition, market, Sunday Session and evening pilot overlapping. The freelance coordination line and the volunteer briefing are intended to protect capacity; if delivery slips, the exhibition workshops are the first activity to trim.
- **Exhibition conditions.** Even after the HVAC repair, commissioning evidence is required. The plan avoids sensitive material and loans, so a delay affects the exhibition's scale, not the core programme.
- **Income.** The budget is gross and assumes no ticket or café income. That is prudent, but it means the plan does not forecast net cost. If income is stronger than expected, the board could use it to extend childcare or add family sessions; if weaker, the contingency and the pilot cancellation rules protect the core.

### 8.4 What would make us change course

- **If the east hall is not commissioned by 27 February:** Sunday Sessions move to the west hall (60 seats); the May exhibition is deferred or recast as a smaller west hall showcase; the released exhibition budget supports additional family sessions and the access fund.
- **If an evening pilot event has fewer than 18 bookings seven days before:** cancel that event and reallocate the minibus and event budget. Do not run a transport-enabled event for a handful of people.
- **If the Sunday Sessions average below 25 after two events:** stop the series and reallocate the remaining budget to family and craft. The survey's low Sunday preference means this is a genuine test, not a protected commitment.
- **If family attendance falls below 35 for two consecutive months:** review the artist mix, childcare sessions and marketing before the summer. The family strand remains the priority, but it is not assumed to be immune from the wider decline.
- **If the family fund is not approved:** continue the family workshop without childcare, revise the family target and report the difference clearly to the board.
- **If costs rise by more than 5%:** use the contingency first, then reduce the exhibition and extra marketing before touching core sessions.
