# Harrowgate Community Trust

## Six-month programme plan: January–June 2026

**Prepared for:** the board  
**Programming budget:** £60,000  
**Plan total:** £59,088 (including a £5,372 contingency)  
**Separate family fund request:** £6,840 (outside the £60,000)

---

## 1. The plan in brief

Harrowgate's attendance record does not show a general collapse. It shows a shift: the Tuesday craft group and the Thursday film club are smaller, while the Saturday family workshop is larger. This plan responds to that shift rather than to the headline.

It does four things:

1. **Protects and extends the family session.** 24 weekly Saturday workshops and 14 school-holiday sessions, with childcare from the separate family fund.
2. **Refreshes the adult core.** 22 Tuesday craft sessions, 16 of them led by visiting specialists, and six monthly Thursday film screenings.
3. **Tests the transport question properly.** Four Friday film nights with a minibus, compared with the Thursday film club and the 2023 pilot.
4. **Brings the east hall back into use.** A Spring Gathering on 27 March, a Makers Market on 16 May, and a community textile project with a display in June — all conditional on the HVAC repair being commissioned.

Alongside these, 15 school and community group visits bring new people into the building, and two Sunday afternoon pilots test the one time slot with no local evidence.

The total is **£59,088**, leaving **£912** against the £60,000 ceiling. The £6,840 childcare request is separate and within the family fund's £9,000 cap. The plan does not assume the east hall is available, the family fund is committed, or the 2023 pilot settled the evening question; each is a decision gate.

## 2. What the evidence says

### 2.1 Attendance: a shift, not a collapse

| Session | 2024 average | 2025 average | Change | Jan–Jun 2025 average |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Tuesday craft group | 33.9 | 29.2 | -14.0% | 30.5 |
| Thursday film club | 20.7 | 16.2 | -21.8% | 17.0 |
| Saturday family workshop | 31.2 | 37.8 | +20.8% | 39.7 |

Totals for the three sessions: **1,030 in 2024; 997 in 2025 (-3.2%)**. For the comparable January–June period: **515 in 2024; 523 in 2025 (+1.6%)**.

Two things follow. First, the aggregate decline is small, and in the comparable half-year it is absent. Second, the mix has changed: craft and film are down, family is up. The decline the board is reacting to is real for the adult weekday and evening sessions, but it is not a general withdrawal from the building. The record covers only these three sessions; it does not include the café, room hires, other events, or revenue, so it cannot tell us whether the building as a whole is busier or quieter.

Source: [`sources/attendance-2024-2025.csv`](sources/attendance-2024-2025.csv).

### 2.2 The childcare step change

The family workshop averaged **26.9** in January–August 2024 and **40.0** in September–December 2024 — a **48.7% step change** at the point the childcare pilot began. The pack's summary says 42 for the last four months; the monthly rows give 40.0 for September–December and 42.3 for October–December, so the pack figure is not exactly reproducible. The direction is robust; the precise size is not.

The 2025 average was **37.8**, below the late-2024 peak. The effect has faded, not disappeared. This is a natural experiment, not a controlled trial: September is a ramp-up, December includes a Christmas special, and other things changed. We use it to justify protecting the family session, not to promise continued growth. Staff also report that families stayed for the café and some began attending the Tuesday craft group; that is qualitative, but it supports designing the family day as a gateway to the rest of the programme.

Sources: [`sources/prior-interventions.md`](sources/prior-interventions.md); [`sources/attendance-2024-2025.csv`](sources/attendance-2024-2025.csv).

### 2.3 The 2023 film pilot did not test transport

Attendance across the three 2023 Friday screenings was **37, 23 and 19**; 60 and 48 seats were reserved; the series was cancelled. The show rate fell from 62% (37 of 60) to 48% (23 of 48). The programmer's note says people liked the films and the problem was getting home.

But the pilot ran **without the minibus** the staff had costed at £240 a night, because driver funding fell through. It therefore cannot distinguish transport from content, novelty, weather, or the time of year. Attendance fell by **49% from night one to night three**, with no transport on offer. The right conclusion is not "evenings do not work"; it is "the transport hypothesis has not been tested". The plan tests it with four Friday films in the same format, with a minibus, and measures attendance, show rate and boardings.

Sources: [`sources/prior-interventions.md`](sources/prior-interventions.md); [`sources/staff-interviews.md`](sources/staff-interviews.md).

### 2.4 The survey is directional, not demand

240 of 610 members responded (39%), online and on paper, in October 2025. Transport is the top barrier (61%), followed by programme (34%), cost (29%) and caring responsibilities (27%). 44% say they would attend an evening event if transport were available. 27% say free childcare would make it easier to attend, and 81% of those are members with children under 12. Saturday morning is the most popular slot (38%), ahead of weekday morning (24%), weekday evening (18%) and Sunday afternoon (12%).

The survey volunteer's own note says this is a **stated preference**: it did not test whether people would actually come, or what they would pay. The survey is members-only and self-selected, so it under-represents the lapsed and non-member households whose attendance the board wants to recover. We use it to justify a bounded test, not to size the evening market.

Source: [`sources/member-survey-2025.md`](sources/member-survey-2025.md).

### 2.5 Building, money and staffing facts

- The east hall's HVAC failed in January 2025. The repair quote is £18,000, a capital grant application is with the council, and if approved the repair would be scheduled for late February 2026, with the hall usable from **1 March**. The 2019 "climate-controlled" description is no longer true. The west hall has never been climate-controlled; the basement is damp in winter.
- The family fund is separate from the £60,000, capped at **£9,000** for January–June 2026, and the trustees have not committed it.
- The building can host events until 23:00 and must be empty by 23:30. Evening events require two staff; childcare requires two qualified workers; volunteers are not asked to work alone after 18:00.
- Room capacities are: east hall 120 seated / 180 standing; west hall 60 / 90; workshop room 24; café 40.

Sources: [`sources/spaces-and-costs.md`](sources/spaces-and-costs.md); [`sources/staff-interviews.md`](sources/staff-interviews.md); [`sources/constraints.md`](sources/constraints.md).

## 3. Programme design

| **F. | **G. Schools and community visits** | 15 artist-led visits, 25 people each, in term time | Bring new audiences into the building; the 2022 van was not a substitute | 375 participants; conversion to family sessions recorded |

| **I. Marketing** | £600/month plus a spring launch | Necessary for a relaunch; but traffic did not convert in 2024, so not the main lever | Enquiries, bookings, minibus use |
| **J. Evaluation** | Session-level data, boardings, new/returning, income | Total attendance hides mix; the board needs a decision-ready report | Monthly dashboard; end-of-period report |

The plan is a portfolio, not a bet on one explanation. The board has three explanations and no decisive evidence. Rather than choose, the plan funds the delivery that is justified now (family, craft, film, building) and buys two pieces of evidence (transport and Sunday) for about **8% of direct spend** (£4,200 of £53,716). If the transport test is positive, the second half of the year can scale evenings; if it is negative, the board can stop subsidising minibuses and look at content or changed habits. Either way, the board learns something it can use.

### What we deliberately did not do

- **We did not re-run an outreach van.** The 2022 van averaged 11 attendances per visit, had no written evaluation, and was remembered as "worthwhile but not a substitute for the building". The 15 school and community visits bring groups into the building instead.
- **We did not increase marketing substantially.** The 2024 website migration raised traffic 12% but there is no evidence it changed attendance; the decline continued through 2025. Marketing is needed for the relaunch, but it is not the binding constraint.
- **We did not schedule a textile exhibition on the basis of the 2019 survey.** The HVAC failed in January 2025. The Threads display is conditional on commissioning and environmental monitoring.
- **We did not treat the 2023 pilot as settling the evening question.** It ran without the minibus that the staff had costed.
- **We did not put childcare inside the £60,000 or assume the family fund is committed.** The family workshop is core-funded; childcare is a separate, discretionary enhancement.
- **We did not add permanent staff.** The programme uses existing staff, artists, bought-in evening cover and volunteers.

### What each month is for

**January — establish the baseline.** There is no new public event. The priorities are measurement, the family-fund request, the minibus route, and the east hall decision timeline. The family workshop resumes on 3 January with childcare if the fund is confirmed by 2 January; otherwise the first session runs without childcare and the position is reviewed at the next board meeting. The craft refresh starts on 6 January; the film club returns on 15 January. The board should receive a one-page baseline by 31 January.

**February — first transport test and the east hall gate.** The half-term programme runs 17–20 February. The first Friday film with the minibus is on 27 February. **13 February is the east hall gate:** if the capital grant is not approved and a repair date confirmed, the March Spring Gathering moves to the west hall and the Makers Market is re-planned for the west hall/workshop. **27 February is the commissioning gate:** if the HVAC is not signed off, no east hall public events take place until it is.

**March — the east hall relaunch.** If commissioning is confirmed, the Spring Gathering opens the east hall on 27 March. The second transport night is 20 March. This is the highest-risk month: two evening events and a possible first east hall event. If the hall is not ready, the Spring Gathering runs in the west hall at 60 capacity, with the same programme and a smaller audience; the board is informed.

**April — the Easter family intensive.** Six holiday sessions in two weeks, plus three Saturday workshops. The third transport night is 24 April. Threads workshops begin. The Easter programme is the main volume driver of the month, and it tests whether holiday demand is as strong as Saturday demand.

**May — the busiest month.** The Makers Market on 16 May is the main daytime east hall event; the fourth transport night is 8 May; the May half-term programme runs 26–29 May; Threads production continues. Staff capacity is the main risk. The market and the family workshop share the building on 16 May, so the day is planned as one combined family/market day with separate rooms.

**June — test, display, review.** The Sunday pilots are on 14 and 28 June. The Threads display opens on 20 June and runs to 28 June, in the east hall only if the HVAC has been commissioned and the environmental log is within the display protocol; otherwise it moves to the west hall with robust works. The end-of-period review and the second-half recommendation go to the board by 30 June.

### Capacity and staffing notes

- The workshop room holds 24, so the 28-person craft group and the 40-person family workshop use the **west hall** (60), with workshop-room access for kiln and sink work. The workshop room is used for Threads (about 9 per session) and small groups. The east hall (120 seated) is used only for the Spring Gathering, the Makers Market and the Threads display. The café (40) is used for the Sunday pilot. School visits use the west hall or east hall, not the 24-person workshop room. No session is planned above its room's capacity.
- All public events end by 22:00 and the building is clear by 22:30, inside the 23:00/23:30 licence. The Sunday pilot ends at 16:00, the café's normal closing time, so no additional café staffing is budgeted.
- Every evening event has two paid staff. Volunteers are paired and are never left alone after 18:00.
- Childcare is delivered by two qualified workers for four hours per session, funded from the family fund at £180.
- The Threads display is invigilated within existing staff and volunteer cover; no additional staffing line is budgeted.

## 6. Rationale: evidence, judgment and uncertainty

**In short:** the attendance record shows a mix shift, not a general collapse; the childcare pilot is the strongest evidence of a mechanism; the 2023 pilot did not test transport; and the survey is directional. The plan protects the one growing session, refreshes the two declining ones, tests transport with a bounded series, and gates the east hall on commissioning. The main uncertainties are east hall availability, the family fund, and whether transport actually changes behaviour.

### 6.1 What we relied on

The plan rests on four pieces of evidence, in descending order of strength.

1. **The monthly attendance record** ([`sources/attendance-2024-2025.csv`](sources/attendance-2024-2025.csv)). A complete 24-month run for three sessions. It is the best evidence in the pack because it is consistent and comparable. It shows the decline is concentrated, not general: the aggregate is down 3.2% for 2025 but up 1.6% for January–June. We used it to set baselines and targets, not to explain why.
2. **The 2024 childcare pilot** ([`sources/prior-interventions.md`](sources/prior-interventions.md); [`sources/attendance-2024-2025.csv`](sources/attendance-2024-2025.csv)). The family workshop moved from 26.9 (January–August 2024) to 40.0 (September–December 2024), a 48.7% step change at the point childcare began. This is the strongest evidence of a mechanism, but it is a natural experiment, not a trial. The 2025 average of 37.8 shows the effect has faded; we use it to protect the session, not to forecast growth.
3. **The 2023 film pilot** ([`sources/prior-interventions.md`](sources/prior-interventions.md); [`sources/staff-interviews.md`](sources/staff-interviews.md)). 37, 23, 19; no minibus because driver funding fell through. This is the key negative evidence, and it is routinely misread. It does not show that evenings fail; it shows that a film series without transport lost audience. We replicate it with the missing ingredient.
4. **The 2025 member survey** ([`sources/member-survey-2025.md`](sources/member-survey-2025.md)). 240/610 (39%), members only, stated preference. Transport is the top barrier (61%) and 44% say they would come in the evening if transport were available, but the survey volunteer explicitly warns this is hypothetical. We use it to justify a test, not to size demand.

We also used the staff interviews and building facts ([`sources/staff-interviews.md`](sources/staff-interviews.md); [`sources/spaces-and-costs.md`](sources/spaces-and-costs.md)) for specific, corroborated details: the 18:40 last bus, the £240 minibus quote, the HVAC failure, the £18,000 repair, the late-February completion and 1 March availability. We did not rely on the 2019 "climate-controlled" description; it is contradicted by the January 2025 failure.

### 6.2 The judgments we made

- **The decline is a mix shift, so we target sessions, not the total.** A generic "more events" plan would not address the fact that craft and film are losing people while family is gaining them. The primary measure is therefore attendance by session, not a single aggregate.
- **We protect the family session because it is the only strand with a measured growth mechanism.** We do not assume it will keep growing: the 2025 average is below the late-2024 peak, and the family fund is discretionary. The core workshop is funded from the £60,000, so it survives without the family fund.
- **We refresh craft rather than replace it.** A 14% decline in a loyal, ageing group is a quality signal, not a reason to close the group. Sixteen specialist-led sessions add challenge and a reason to return without changing the weekly rhythm.
- **We keep the Thursday film club as a low-cost core and a comparison.** It runs without a minibus; the Friday test runs with one. The comparison is not a controlled experiment — different day, different content — but it is a useful local signal.
- **We use the east hall for events that do not need stable temperature or humidity first.** A spring gathering, a makers market and a community textile project can run before a textile display is safe. The display is gated on commissioning and monitoring. This satisfies the board's east hall requirement without betting the programme on a stale 2019 note.
- **We keep marketing at the pack's typical rate plus a launch.** The 2024 website migration raised traffic 12% but did not change attendance; reach is not the binding constraint. The marketing spend is for the relaunch and the minibus route, not a general brand campaign.
- **We bring groups into the building rather than repeat the 2022 van.** The van averaged 11 attendances per visit and was withdrawn when its grant ended; staff remember it as worthwhile but not a substitute for the building. Fifteen school and community visits are a pipeline to the family sessions.
- **No core session depends on a single external funder.** The core programme is funded from the board's £60,000. Childcare is the only element that relies on the separate family fund; the east hall repair is a separate capital grant; neither is load-bearing for a core session.

### 6.4 How we will measure success

The primary measure is **attendance by session**, not total attendance. In 2025 total attendance was almost flat while the adult core fell; a single aggregate would have hidden the problem. The board should judge the plan on four questions: have craft and film stabilised; has the family session held; did the transport test produce a clear answer; did the east hall come online safely. Revenue and new members are secondary measures; the pack has no baseline, so we will establish one before setting targets.
