# Harrowgate Community Trust

## Six-month programme plan: January–June 2026

**Prepared for the board | January 2026 | Programming budget: £60,000**

---

## 1. Summary

This plan protects the Saturday family workshop — the only session that grew in 2025 — tests whether the new Friday late bus can make an evening film series work, renews the ageing Tuesday craft offer, and brings the east hall back into use after the HVAC commissioning with non-sensitive events.

It deliberately does **not** schedule a textile or paper exhibition. The 2019 “climate-controlled” description of the east hall is out of date, and the repair has not yet been proved in operation. East hall use in this plan is limited to robust, non-sensitive material and participant-made work that is not sensitive or is expendable, and only after the building officer issues the commissioning certificate.

The £60,000 budget commits **£55,550** and holds **£4,450** as a trigger-based reserve. The separate **£4,000 family fund** is shown as a memo; it is not part of the £60,000. Core sessions are funded from the trust’s programme budget, not from a single external funder.

**Headline decisions requested:** approve the programme and budget; note the family-fund base of 20 crèche sessions; authorise east hall holds only after commissioning; note the review points at the end of March, early May, end of May and end of June.

## 4. Delivery check: staffing, rooms and licensing

### 4.1 Staffing

The trust has 2.0 FTE permanent staff (director, programmer), 0.5 FTE administrator and eight volunteers. No new permanent posts are proposed. The programme is designed around that capacity:

- **Evenings:** the director and programmer share the evening rota and are both present at each evening event; no volunteer counts towards the two-staff requirement. No volunteer works alone after 18:00; volunteers are paired with staff. There are no two evening events on the same night.
- **January** has four Thursday film evenings. **February** has one Thursday and one Friday. **March** has one Thursday and two Fridays. **April** has one Thursday, two Fridays and the Spring Assembly Friday. **May** has one Thursday and two Fridays. **June** has one Thursday and one Friday. The maximum is four evening events in a month: January (four Thursdays) and April (three film nights plus the Spring Assembly Friday), each spread across four different weeks; every other month has at most three.
- **Childcare:** two qualified workers are required and are funded by the family fund at £180 per session. The 20 sessions are within the fund.
- **Weekends:** the Saturday family workshop runs every week; there are one or two Saturday afternoon events in most months, three Sundays and two festival weekends. These use existing weekend staffing and time off in lieu. If capacity is breached, the first cuts are the extra Friday nights and the Community Commission, not the core Tuesday, Saturday or family sessions.
- **Daytime:** the New Makers course, Wednesday Family Art and Community Commission are artist-led; existing staff provide cover. The Tuesday Craft Group is assumed to remain peer-led with volunteer support, so the budget covers materials and six guest artists rather than a weekly paid leader. If a paid leader is required every week, that is an additional £6,500 (26 × £250) and is not in the committed budget. No additional permanent hours are assumed.

### 4.2 Rooms, capacity and licensing

- West hall: 60 seated / 90 standing; workshop room: 24; east hall: 120 seated / 180 standing; café: 40. The Tuesday Craft Group meets in the west hall because its average attendance (about 30) exceeds the workshop room’s capacity of 24; the workshop room is open for kiln and sink access in rotations.
- The east hall is not booked for public events between 9 and 27 February. No east hall booking is confirmed until the commissioning certificate is issued.
- East hall use is limited to non-sensitive material and participant-made work that is not sensitive or is expendable. No loans, archives, valuable textiles or paper are displayed there until the building officer confirms the hall’s environmental performance. The west hall is not a fallback for sensitive material either; it has never been climate-controlled.
- The basement store is not used for sensitive material.
- Events end by 21:00; the building is empty by 23:30. The Friday films and the Spring Assembly Friday opening end at 20:45 to allow time to reach the 21:15 bus.
- The café stays open for the Spring Assembly, Summer Showcase and Sunday sessions, staffed by the existing café team.
- Spring Assembly numbers are attendances across the day, not simultaneous: capacity is counted at 180 and timed entry is used if the hall approaches capacity.

### 4.3 Transport

- The Friday late bus runs from 6 February, last service 21:15, Fridays only, for six months. The Friday films and the Spring Assembly Friday opening are programmed to end at 20:45.
- The walk from the building to the square must be confirmed and published; if it is longer than 20 minutes, the film end time moves earlier.
- There is no late bus on Thursdays or Sundays. Thursday Classics attendees arrange taxis or lifts; the trust will signpost taxi sharing but does not fund it in the base budget. This is a known limitation of the Thursday comparison, not an oversight.
- If the council withdraws the Friday bus, the minibus reserve is activated for the remaining Friday events.

## 5. How we will measure success

The board’s sixth open question is what success means. This plan uses a small, consistent dashboard rather than a single number:

- **Attendance:** door counts at every session, plus bookings and show rate for ticketed events.
- **Reach:** unique participants and new members, recorded at sign-up and through the booking system.
- **Retention:** repeat attendance, especially crèche families and Friday cinema bookers.
- **Transport:** the number and share of Friday attendees arriving by bus, and whether they would have come otherwise.
- **Income:** ticket and café income, reported but not assumed in the budget.
- **Cost per attendance:** direct event cost divided by attendances, to compare strands.
- **Equity:** access referrals, free places and feedback from under-represented groups.

A three-question exit card (how did you travel; would you come again; what would make it easier) runs at every event, online and on paper.

## 6. Rationale

### 6.1 Evidence I relied on

**Attendance data (`sources/attendance-2024-2025.csv`) is the strongest evidence.** Total attendances fell from 1,030 in 2024 to 997 in 2025, a 3.2% decline. But the decline is not uniform:

- Tuesday Craft Group fell from 407 to 350 (−14.0%).
- Thursday Film Club fell from 248 to 194 (−21.8%).
- Saturday Family Workshop rose from 375 to 453 (+20.8%).

The timing matters. In the first half of 2025, total attendances were 523 against 515 in the first half of 2024 (+1.6%); the second half of 2025 was 474 against 515 (−8.0%). The Saturday family workshop grew year-on-year but plateaued at about 39–40 per session after the 2024 childcare pilot, and it fell from 43 in January 2025 to 39 in December. The “decline” is therefore a mix of a strong family session, a weakening craft group, a declining film club and a weak autumn. The plan protects the family session, changes the film and craft offers, and does not assume that childcare alone will restore growth.

**The 2023 film pilot and the transport evidence (`sources/prior-interventions.md`, `sources/staff-interviews.md`).** The Friday film pilot drew 37, 23 and 19 people over three nights and was cancelled. Reservations were 60 for the first night and 48 for the second, giving show rates of 62% and 48%; the third-night reservation figure is missing. The programmer’s note says people liked the films but could not get home. Staff confirm the last minibus left at 18:40, a taxi was about £14 return, and the minibus quote of £240 a night was never funded. The pilot is not a clean test of evening demand: it ran without the transport that staff identify as the binding constraint. The plan therefore treats a 2026 evening series as an untested, transport-dependent proposition, not a revival of a proven model.

**The 2024 childcare pilot (`sources/prior-interventions.md`, `sources/staff-interviews.md`).** Attendance at the Saturday family workshop rose from an average of about 27 in the first eight months of 2024 to about 40 in the last four (42 in October–December). Families stayed for the café and some joined the Tuesday craft group. This is the most promising family intervention in the pack. But the 2025 data shows the uplift plateaued, so the plan maintains the offer rather than assuming it will keep growing, and it does not promise weekly childcare beyond what the fund can support.

**The 2025 member survey (`sources/member-survey-2025.md`) is directional, not a forecast.** Transport is the most cited barrier (61%), followed by programme appeal (34%), cost (29%) and caring responsibilities (27%). A further 27% say free childcare would make it easier to attend, and 81% of those are parents of under-12s. But only 18% choose weekday evening as their best time, against 38% for Saturday morning, 24% for weekday morning and 12% for Sunday afternoon. The 44% who say they would attend an evening event if transport were available is a stated preference: the survey volunteer’s method note says people were asked to imagine a bus, and actual attendance and willingness to pay were not tested. The response rate was 39% and the sample is members only. The plan therefore prioritises Saturday and weekday-morning family activity, and treats Friday evening as a test rather than the core bet; the Sunday test is small because the 12% preference is not behavioural evidence.

**Operational evidence (`sources/staff-interviews.md`, `sources/spaces-and-costs.md`, `sources/constraints.md`).** The east hall HVAC failed in January 2025 and the hall is not climate-controlled; the west hall has never been climate-controlled; the basement is damp. The trust has 2.0 FTE staff, 0.5 FTE administration and eight volunteers; evening events need two staff; childcare needs two qualified workers; volunteers cannot work alone after 18:00. These constraints shape the limited evening cadence (a weekly January baseline, then monthly from February, plus the Friday series), the 20-session crèche base, and the non-sensitive east hall programme.

**Prior interventions.** The 2022 outreach van averaged 11 attendances per visit, was withdrawn when its grant ended and left no written evaluation. The 2024 website migration raised traffic by 12%, but there is no evidence that it changed attendance; the decline continued through 2025. Neither is a strong basis for a 2026 programme, so the plan does not repeat the van and does not allocate a digital transformation budget.

**The Round 1 notes (`sources/PRIOR-NOTES.md`) were used as a check, not as a plan.** They correctly identify two stale claims: the 2019 “climate-controlled” description of the east hall is no longer true, and the 44% survey figure does not establish that members want an evening series. I have carried those corrections forward. The January updates then changed several of the Round 1 assumptions, as set out below.

I treated staff recollections, the 2019 survey summary and the prior notes as opinions or prior analysis, not measurements. The attendance data is the strongest evidence; the survey is directional; the pilot evidence is confounded.

The family fund is the biggest single change. Before the update, the pack’s £9,000 cap would have covered all 26 Saturday childcare sessions. The update cuts the fund to £4,000, so a weekly offer is no longer affordable: 26 × £180 = £4,680, and even the 22-session maximum leaves only £40. That is why the family fund is kept outside the £60,000, the base is 20 sessions with a £400 hold, and the plan does not promise permanent free childcare.

The bus is the second big change. It turns the evening question from “can we afford a minibus?” into “will people use a 21:15 Friday bus?” That is a real test, but only on Fridays. The plan does not generalise it to Thursday, Saturday or Sunday.

The east hall update is good news but still conditional. The plan uses the hall after commissioning, but it does not schedule the sensitive textile exhibition that the old notes might have encouraged. The building officer’s certificate and a check on actual environmental performance come first.

### 6.3 What I am uncertain about

- **Bus ridership and walk time.** The pack does not give the walk from the building to the square, the bus fare, or evidence that people will use a 21:15 service. The 44% survey figure is stated preference. The bus may be withdrawn after the May review. The plan mitigates this by ending at 20:45, measuring bus uptake, keeping a minibus reserve and setting a stop rule.
- **Family-fund continuity and demand.** The trustees may use part of the £4,000 for other family support; demand for crèche may exceed 20 sessions; families may not return if crèche stops in June. The plan mitigates this with the £400 hold, the 22-session stretch option and a workshop that runs without crèche. It does not raid the £60,000.
- **East hall performance.** The commissioning certificate is not yet issued, and the pack has no temperature or humidity readings, no post-repair commissioning data, and no confirmation that the HVAC repair restores the full 2019 climate-control standard. The plan avoids sensitive material and loans, and treats the first use as a test. If the certificate is delayed, the First Look moves and the Spring Assembly falls back to 25–26 April or the west hall with a reduced capacity cap.
- **Demand for new strands.** Wednesday Family Art, Sunday Afternoon Sessions, New Makers and the Community Commission are tests, not certainties. They have low fixed costs and clear stop rules. The reserve is not released until demand is demonstrated.
- **Data limits.** Attendance is session counts, not unique people. There is no revenue data, no recorded reason for individual non-attendance and no evaluation of the 2023 pilot beyond the programmer’s note. The numbers are small and seasonal. The plan uses door counts, bookings, repeat attendance and a short exit card to improve the evidence, but six months will not settle the “transport versus programming versus habits” question definitively.
- **Survey limits.** The 240 respondents are self-selected members (39% of 610). The 44% evening figure is a stated preference and cannot be treated as a forecast. The plan uses it only to justify a small test.
- **Staffing capacity.** The programme is full for 2.0 FTE staff, 0.5 FTE administration and eight volunteers. The limited evening cadence and the use of time off in lieu are intended to keep it within current arrangements, but a sick absence or a busy festival week could stretch the team. If capacity is breached, the extra Friday nights and the Commission are the first cuts.
- **Cost estimates.** Several lines are estimates rather than pack rates: the £200 First Look, the £300 half-month cinema launch, the £500 evaluation line and the £1,000 access contingency. The participant assumptions (30 craft, 40 family, 20 Wednesday, 15 New Makers, 10 Commission, 200 Spring Saturday, 100 Spring Sunday, 100 Summer Showcase, 80 Open House, 30 First Look) may be wrong. The Tuesday Craft Group is assumed to remain peer-led; a weekly paid leader would add £6,500. The 10% contingency and the flex in materials costs absorb some of this, but not a weekly craft leader; that would require a board decision to reduce other activity or find new funding.
- **Income.** No ticket income is assumed. Pricing and willingness to pay are untested. If income is lower than expected, expenditure will not increase; if it is higher, it replenishes the reserve first.
- **“Changed habits.”** The pack has no direct measure of post-pandemic habits. The first-half/second-half pattern is consistent with seasonality, habit and mix change, not a single cause. The plan varies times and formats and compares same-month attendance, but it cannot prove habit change in six months.

**What would change my mind:** if Friday attendance is strong but Thursday collapses, transport is likely the binding constraint and the Friday series should expand. If both are weak despite curation, programming quality or habit is more important and the evening series should not expand. If Wednesday Family Art outperforms Saturday, weekday-morning demand is real and the family budget should rebalance. If crèche families do not return, childcare is not enough and the family content needs rethinking. If the east hall certificate is delayed, the anchors move; they are not forced.

### 6.4 Why this mix

The plan is deliberately a set of comparisons rather than a bet on one explanation:

- **Thursday versus Friday film:** similar curation, different transport. The programmer will pair each month’s Thursday and Friday films by genre and length where possible, so the comparison is as close as the programme allows. Thursday has no late bus; Friday does. This is the cleanest available test of the transport explanation, although it is not a controlled experiment.
- **Saturday versus Wednesday family:** Saturday has crèche and a weekend slot; Wednesday is carer-accompanied and weekday morning. This tests the survey’s time preferences and the childcare hypothesis. Two family film matinees extend the Saturday offer at low marginal cost (licence and technician only).
- **New Makers, guest artists and the Community Commission:** New Makers is a six-week introduction for new participants; the Commission is an eight-session studio group producing work for the Summer Showcase. Together they improve the quality and renewal of the craft offer, which the staff describe as loyal but ageing, and test whether “programme does not appeal” is a content problem rather than only a transport problem.
- **Sunday afternoons:** a low-cost test of the one time slot for which the pack has no local evidence.
- **East hall:** the board asked for an activity once the hall is available. The First Look, Spring Assembly and Summer Showcase provide three, all non-sensitive, without risking valuable material.

The core Tuesday, Saturday and film sessions are funded from the £60,000. The family fund adds crèche; the council bus pilot adds transport; the capital grant repairs the hall. None of the core sessions depends on a single external funder. If the family fund or the bus disappears, the core programme continues in a reduced form.
