# Harrowgate Community Trust

## Six-month programme plan: January–June 2026

**Programming budget:** £60,000 · **Committed:** £59,468 · **Contingency held:** £3,400 · **Unallocated:** £532

The board's real question is not what to put on, but why people stopped coming. This plan is built so
that the trust can answer that from its own data by 30 June, while running a programme worth
attending in the meantime. It does three things at once: it protects the Saturday family session,
the only part of the programme that has grown; it tests the transport explanation at a size the
trust can afford, in the slot where the decline is steepest; and it opens the east hall in March
with a curated exhibition that tests whether a stronger offer brings people back.

Nothing is scheduled in the east hall before the HVAC repair is commissioned. No Friday evening
opening is proposed. No core session depends on a grant that has not been awarded.

### Budget notes

1. **The family fund is outside this budget.** Childcare at £180 for each of 26 Saturdays is £4,680,
   within the £9,000 cap, and leaves £4,320 of that fund unspent. It is not counted in the £60,000. The
   trustees are asked to decide by 31 January so that marketing is accurate. If they decline, the
   Saturday workshop still runs, at the historical no-childcare level of about 27, and this budget
   does not change.
2. **Evening staffing is charged for all 25 film evenings.** The licence requires two staff present and
   the permanent staff's paid hours are daytime, so £220 an evening is a real cost, not an overhead
   already covered.
3. **Invigilation is 120 paid hours** — 24 open days × 5 hours — which covers every public opening hour
   without relying on volunteers. Volunteers add capacity; they are not the plan.
4. **Rates are the pack's 2025 unit costs, not quotes.** The minibus figure is a 2023 quote and will be
   re-tested before the contract is signed. The contingency absorbs modest uplifts; a large one changes
   the test, not the total (see section 3.6).
5. **The £3,400 contingency** may be drawn only with board approval, in £500 increments, for: minibus
   re-quote uplift, additional exhibition invigilation if the run is extended, or materials above 40
   people a week. The £532 headroom is unallocated and not spendable without a board decision.
6. **Building costs are excluded.** The HVAC repair is a separate capital grant application. No repair,
   decoration or equipment cost sits in this table.

## 3. Rationale

### 3.1 What the evidence says

**The decline is recent, and it is not uniform.** Total attendances across the three recorded sessions
were 1,030 in 2024 and 997 in 2025, a fall of 3.2%. That headline hides the shape of it. The first half
of 2025 was 1.6% *above* the first half of 2024. The second half was 8.0% below. October–December 2025
was 12.5% below the same quarter a year earlier (266 against 304). Whatever is happening has
accelerated in the last six months, which is why a six-month plan is the right instrument.

**The three sessions are moving in different directions**, which is the strongest clue in the pack.
Between 2024 and 2025 the craft group fell 14.0% (33.9 to 29.2), the film club fell 21.8% (20.7 to
16.2), and the family workshop rose 20.8% (31.2 to 37.8). If changed post-pandemic habits were the
whole explanation, all three would be falling together. They are not. Something specific to the two
adult sessions is happening, and the family session proves that a change to the offer can move numbers.

**The childcare pilot is the only intervention with a measurable effect, and it is now flattening.**
The eight months before childcare averaged 26.9; the sixteen months after averaged 38.3, a step change
of about 11 people, at £180 a session — roughly £15.80 per additional attendance. But the year-on-year
gap has narrowed month by month: +14 in January 2024 against January 2025, +4 by September, and −1 to
−3 in October, November and December. Childcare bought a level, not a trend. Continuing it protects
what works; it will not by itself reverse the decline.

**The 2023 evening pilot failed on transport, but the evidence is thin.** Attendance was 37, 23 and 19
against 60 and 48 seats booked. The programmer's note at the time said people liked the films and the
problem was getting home. The last scheduled minibus leaves the square at 18:40 and a taxi is about £14
return. Three nights, falling within the run, is suggestive rather than settled — and the booking data
shows that even people who reserved did not come.

**The survey measures what people say, not what they do.** Of 610 members, 240 responded (39%). 61% name
transport as one of up to two barriers, 34% the programme, 29% cost and 27% caring responsibilities.
44% say they would attend an evening event if transport were available. The volunteer's method note is
candid: they asked people to imagine a bus, did not test take-up, and did not ask what anyone would pay.
The same survey says 38% prefer Saturday morning — which is where the trust's strongest session already
sits. And the 2024 website migration raised traffic 12% with no detectable effect on attendance: reach
is not the binding constraint.

### 3.2 What the plan does about it

Three moves follow from that reading.

1. **Protect and refresh the family session.** It is the best-evidenced attendance the trust buys:
   about £12.25 a head, or £16.75 with childcare, for a session that has held around 40 people for
   sixteen months. It meets the board's requirement for a recurring family session, so it stays weekly,
   in the same slot, with childcare requested for all 26 Saturdays. The maker residency refreshes the
   craft group alongside it, because that group is loyal but ageing and its attendance is drifting down.
2. **Test transport where it can be measured.** The minibus runs on the existing Thursday film slot for
   thirteen weeks, bracketed by four baseline evenings in January and eight withdrawal evenings in May
   and June. Same room, same time, same films, same staff. That is as close to a controlled comparison
   as a small arts centre can get, and it costs £3,120 rather than the £9,000 or more that committing
   to a permanent evening season would.
3. **Add one strong offer and one untested time.** The exhibition is the only way to test the
   programming explanation at scale, and it uses the east hall the trust has been paying for and not
   using. The Sunday Sessions test the one slot the trust has never tried, at about £17 a head, sized at
   six events so a negative answer is cheap.

The unit economics explain the balance. The film club costs about £30 per attendance at current numbers
(£490 a night against 16 people), the most expensive thing the trust runs; with a full bus and 26
attending it is still about £28. Transport is therefore not justified by unit cost. It is justified
because it settles a question the board has been guessing about, and because a clear negative result
lets the trust stop weekly film with evidence and redirect its £5,500 of evening staffing.

### 3.3 What I am uncertain about

- **Whether stated preference becomes behaviour.** The 2023 show rate was 62% and 48% of bookings. A
  half-empty bus is a real possible result, and it should be read as an answer about demand, not as a
  failure of the bus.
- **Whether transport or programme quality is the binding constraint on film.** They have moved
  together and the pack cannot separate them. The before/during/after design separates transport from
  everything except the films themselves, which is why the film programme is unchanged during the test.
- **How much of the childcare effect is causal.** The pre-period includes an August trough; excluding
  it, the lift is about 10.4 rather than 11.4. Seasonality, word of mouth and the café are tangled in.
  The effect is large under any reading, but its exact size is not settled.
- **The east hall date.** "Late February" depends on a capital grant that has not been approved. The
  plan treats 27 February as a hard gate and holds a fallback (section 3.6), but a slip would push the
  exhibition into May–June and compress the summer.
- **Costs.** These are unit rates, not quotes, and the minibus rate is from 2023. A re-quote above £300
  an evening would cut the test from thirteen evenings to ten.
- **Sunday demand.** There is no local evidence at all, only the 12% who prefer Sunday afternoon. Six
  events is a deliberately cheap way to be wrong.
- **What the attendance figures count.** They are attendances, not people. A family of four counts four
  times, so family growth flatters the total. The pack contains no revenue data, so cost per attendance
  is the only efficiency measure available and it is a blunt one.
- **Loans and insurance.** The plan assumes the trust's existing insurance covers loaned textile work
  and that lenders accept the trust's conditions. That must be confirmed before any loan letter is
  signed, and it is the most likely reason the exhibition would shrink.

### 3.4 Notes I did not rely on, and why

The pack warns that some inherited notes may be out of date. Three are worth naming.

- **"The east hall is climate-controlled" (2019 survey, carried forward).** It is not. The HVAC unit
  failed in January 2025 and cannot be run until it is repaired. The 2019 description is the reason the
  plan is safe: it schedules nothing in that room before written commissioning, and it keeps loaned
  material out of the damp basement. A plan that trusted the old note would put textiles in an
  uncontrolled room in March.
- **"Members want evening events" (2025 survey, headline figures).** They say they do, under a
  hypothetical. The 2023 pilot is the behaviour, and it is a negative result. The plan tests rather than
  assumes, and it does not repeat the Friday slot the journal warns against; it uses the existing
  Thursday habit.
- **"Saturday childcare raised attendance" (2024 review).** True, but the note describes a level shift
  that has since flattened. Reinstating it is necessary, not sufficient.

The plan commits to thresholds before the results are in.

| | | 20–29 | Partial. Run one transported evening a month, not a weekly season. |
| | | < 20 | Not the constraint. Stop the bus; move film to fortnightly; redirect £5,500 of staffing to talks and Sundays. |
| Is Sunday worth having? | 6 events, April–June | Average ≥ 25 | Continue in autumn. |
| | | 15–24 | Keep quarterly. |
| | | < 15 | Stop. |
| Was the exhibition worth it? | 24 open days, 900 visits target | ≥ 900 | Repeat annually; seek project funding. |
| | | 600–899 | Keep as a biennial. |
| | | < 600 | Do not repeat in this form. |
| Is the family session holding? | Weekly, with childcare | < 33 for three consecutive weeks | Review the format, not the childcare. |
| Is attendance recovering overall? | Jan–Jun 2026 against 523 in Jan–Jun 2025 | Floor 540 · target 600 · stretch 650 | Report against all three at the July board. |

The headline measures are total attendances in the three core sessions, the number of distinct bookers,
the show rate on transported evenings, and the share of attenders arriving by bus, car, taxi, walk or
lift. New members and café takings are recorded as secondary measures. Revenue is reported but not
targeted, because the pack gives no baseline to target against.

### 3.6 Constraints and risks

The plan stays inside the trust's current staffing and licensing arrangements. Every evening event has
two staff present and no volunteer works alone after 18:00. Childcare is staffed by two qualified
workers. Film ends at 21:00, the last minibus leaves at 21:15 and the building is clear well before
23:30. No licence variation is sought. There are no new permanent posts: the curator and invigilators
are bought in, and the director and programmer share the evening rota. Core sessions are funded from
the £60,000, not from a single external funder, and no grant is assumed.

| Risk | Response |
| --- | --- |
| HVAC commissioning slips past 27 February | Exhibition moves to 16 May–27 June, and the Summer Showcase becomes its closing day. The £3,400 contingency absorbs rebooking costs and any extension of the run. |
| Minibus unavailable or no driver | Second operator on standby; taxi reimbursement capped at £240 an evening, so the test continues at the same cost. |
| Transport demand is low | Keep the booked dates and record the result. Do not cancel mid-block; a cancelled test answers nothing. |
| Staff capacity in March and April | Curator owns loans, catalogue and install; invigilation is fully paid; no new programming is added in those two months. |
| Severe weather in January or February | Cancel and reschedule within the block; the baseline needs four evenings, not four specific dates. |
| Family fund not committed | Saturday continues without childcare at the historical level; the programming budget is unaffected. |
