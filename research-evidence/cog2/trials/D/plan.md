# Harrowgate Community Trust

## Six-month programme plan: January–June 2026

**Programming budget:** £60,000  
**Period:** 1 January – 30 June 2026  
**Prepared for:** the Board of Trustees

> The £60,000 covers programming only. The east hall repair is a separate capital grant application, and the family fund is separate and capped at £9,000. Neither is included in the £60,000.

---

## 1. The plan in brief

The trust's attendance decline is not confined to evenings. Tuesday craft fell 14% and Thursday film fell 22% in 2025. Saturday family rose 21% year-on-year, but that rise coincides with the introduction of childcare and is not continuing growth: once both years had childcare (September–December), family attendance was flat (40.0 in 2024 against 39.2 in 2025). Transport may be part of the story, but it cannot explain the fall in Tuesday-morning craft or the flattening of Saturday family attendance. The plan therefore keeps the core programme running as a baseline and adds four bounded pieces of work, each aimed at a different explanation for the decline.

| Strand | What happens | Explanation tested |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core programme (baseline)** | Tuesday craft, Thursday film and Saturday family workshop continue | Is the decline general, or specific to one session? |
| **Family and childcare** | Weekly Saturday workshop (programming budget) with free childcare (family fund) | Caring responsibilities; family demand |
| **Friday Nights** | Eight evening events with a hired minibus, free entry and measured attendance | Transport; evening demand |
| **Sunday Sessions** | Six low-cost afternoon talk-and-making sessions | Changed habits and time preference; there is no local evidence |
| **East hall** | A spring exhibition and a May–June community season, gated on HVAC commissioning | Programming quality and scale; better use of the building |

The strands map onto the board's three explanations and the survey's caring-responsibility barrier: Friday Nights tests transport; the family strand tests caring and family demand; Sunday Sessions test time and habit; the east hall season tests programming quality and scale. The core programme is the baseline against which the new work is judged.

Two gates keep the board in control: **20 February** (east hall) and **27 April** (Friday Nights). A final evaluation on **30 June** recommends what should continue.

## 4. Rationale: evidence and uncertainty

### 4.1 What I relied on

The attendance record (`attendance-2024-2025.csv`) is the strongest evidence because it records behaviour rather than opinion. Comparing 2025 with 2024:

- Tuesday craft: average 33.9 → 29.2 (−14%).
- Thursday film: 20.7 → 16.2 (−22%).
- Saturday family: 31.2 → 37.8 (+21%), but the gain is concentrated in January–August 2025, when 2024 had no childcare. In September–December, when both years had childcare, the averages are 40.0 (2024) and 39.2 (2025). The family session has the strongest attendance level, but it is not on a continuing growth curve.

The decline is therefore broad-based across the core programme: craft and film are down, and the family gain is a step-up that has flattened. That matters. Transport may depress evening attendance, but it cannot by itself explain the fall in Tuesday-morning craft or the flattening of Saturday-morning family attendance. The plan does not treat transport as the single cause. The staff description of the craft group as “loyal but ageing” is consistent with the slow decline; the plan keeps it as a core service but does not rely on it for growth.

The 2025 member survey (`member-survey-2025.md`) puts transport first (61%), but the volunteer's method note is explicit that the evening-transport question was hypothetical: “we asked people to imagine a bus”, with no price test and no behaviour test. 35% said they would not attend even with transport. I treat the survey as a reason to test transport, not as proof of demand. Childcare is cited by 27%, and 81% of those are members with children under 12; that is consistent with the family strand, but it is still a stated preference.

The staff interviews (`staff-interviews.md`) provide two useful factual claims and one opinion. The account says the 2023 pilot had no minibus because driver funding fell through; the booking records show attendance fell 37 → 23 → 19 while bookings fell 60 → 48. The opinion is: “people liked the films; the problem was getting home.” The opinion is plausible, but the pilot is not a clean test of evening demand. It never offered transport, and the first-night booking-to-attendance conversion was only 62%, so something else may also have been happening. The right response is a small, instrumented retest with transport, not a permanent ban or a full-scale commitment.

The prior interventions (`prior-interventions.md`) support a portfolio rather than a single bet. The 2024 childcare pilot is the best positive signal (about 27 → 40 in the attendance record; the prior review rounds this to 42), but it is a before/after comparison confounded by seasonality, novelty and the fact that 2024 was already a declining year. Staff also report that families stayed for the café and some moved to the Tuesday craft group; that spillover is worth measuring, not assuming. The 2022 outreach van had no written evaluation. The 2024 website migration raised traffic 12%, but there is no evidence that it changed attendance, which suggests that marketing and website work alone will not reverse the decline. The 2023 film pilot is a negative signal about evening events without transport, not a verdict on evening events with transport.

The building and money facts (`constraints.md`, `spaces-and-costs.md`) set the boundaries. The east hall HVAC failed in January 2025; the £18,000 repair is a separate capital grant, expected in late February 2026 and usable from 1 March only if approved and commissioned. The 2019 “climate-controlled” description is stale and is not relied on. The family fund is separate and capped at £9,000; 26 Saturdays × £180 = £4,680, so weekly childcare fits the cap with £4,320 to spare. On these figures, the binding constraint is trustee discretion, not the cap, so the plan asks for a decision but does not make the family workshop depend on it.

The 2023 note's own condition — do not repeat an evening series without transport — is met: every Friday Night has a minibus and two staff. That is the difference between repeating the pilot and testing the question it left open.

### 4.3 What I am uncertain about

- **The cause of the decline.** It is probably multiple. The plan is designed to discriminate between explanations, not to prove one. If the Friday pilot succeeds and Sunday fails, transport and evening access look more important. If both fail while the east hall exhibition draws new people, programming and scale look more important. If everything stays flat, changed habits are the better explanation.
- **Whether transport is the binding constraint.** The survey is stated preference, and the 2023 pilot lacked transport. The Friday test gives a behavioural answer, but eight events is a small sample and winter weather may depress turnout.
- **Whether childcare is causal.** The step-up could be a one-off level shift, seasonality or novelty. The plan continues it because it is affordable and consistent with the evidence, but it measures unique families and repeat attendance rather than assuming the effect.
- **East hall timing.** The repair and commissioning are outside the trust's control. The 20 February gate and the west hall fallback protect the programme, but if commissioning slips beyond June, the board's east hall requirement cannot be met in this period.
- **Sunday demand.** There is no local evidence, and the survey ranks Sunday afternoon last (12%). The pilot is deliberately small; it is a probe, not a core bet.
- **The attendance unit in the CSV.** The file does not say whether the figures are monthly totals or per-session averages. The materials assumptions use 10–12 participants per session and should be checked against the register. If the figures are per-session, materials will cost more and the craft group would need the west hall; the contingency or the board will need to adjust.
- **Income.** The pack contains no ticket price or earned-income data, so the plan does not assume income. Revenue will be tracked, not targeted.
- **Staff capacity.** The plan budgets bought-in evening cover, but 20 film screenings plus 10 Friday Nights is a heavy evening roster. If cover cannot be recruited, the film club should move to fortnightly and the savings held.

### 4.4 How success will be judged

A balanced scorecard, not a single number:

- **Attendance:** core sessions at or above the January–June 2025 monthly averages (craft 30.5, film 17.0, family 39.7). New strands must add net new attendances, not just move people from one session to another.
- **Reach:** new attendees, unique families, postcodes and transport mode. Target at least 20 new member households by 30 June.
- **Revenue:** ticket, donation and café income recorded by strand; no target because the pack has no price data.
- **Cost per attendance:** reported by strand for the April and June decisions, using the same unit as the attendance record.
- **Qualitative:** would-return, what people came for and what got in the way, collected through a short exit survey at the pilots and exhibition.

**Decision gates**

- **20 February — east hall:** written commissioning confirmed? If yes, install and open on 14 March. If no, west hall fallback at reduced scale.
- **27 April — Friday Nights:** average attendance at least 25, at least 40% of booked seats taken, at least 12 minibus passengers per event (a 16-seat minibus at least three-quarters full), and at least 40% of attendees from outside the town centre. If met, release the two extension nights; if not, stop and return £1,500.
- **30 June — whole programme:** report against the scorecard and recommend what continues into autumn/winter.

These are proposed thresholds; the board should confirm them before the programme opens.
